## 1. Story Title

**Report the signed price delta on every prospective reorder line through one read-only Shop API query, so that a Returning Buyer sees what each item costs now against what they paid before any cart line is written.**

Short form, transcribed from the epic's story table without rewording so that the two files agree: **Preview price changes before commit** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table].

The unit of work the title names is one finished slice. The new query exists, it accepts a reference to one source the buyer owns, it enforces ownership and channel scope from the authenticated session, it reads the price the buyer was charged on each source line alongside the price that line's variant carries in the requesting channel today, and it returns both figures with their signed difference as integers in the smallest unit of one named currency on one reconciled tax basis. **It writes nothing.**

Three things that could be mistaken for part of it are not, and each belongs to a named sibling: the availability half of the same payload is STORY-001-03-02, the published payload contract with its absent-versus-zero encoding and its staleness caveat in the schema documentation is STORY-001-03-03, and deciding what to *do* about a line whose price or availability moved is FEATURE-001-04. This story reports, and stops.

**One clarification the title cannot carry, and it is the clarification most likely to be missed.** The figure this story reports is the **current catalogue price**, not a quote for what a commit will charge. Four documented mechanisms can make the committed price differ from it, none of them a race, and the parent feature enumerates all four [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.9a The Reported Price Is The Current Catalogue Price]. Section 4.3 states the consequence for this story's field naming, and section 7 covers the decisive case rather than disclaiming it.

## 3. PRECEDENT In This Repository

**PRECEDENT:** partial

The searched set is named so the claim is checkable rather than asserted: the whole of `packages/dev-server/example-plugins/`, the whole of `packages/dev-server/test-plugins/`, and `packages/dashboard/test-plans/`. That is the same set the epic searched for its nomination [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.6 Nomination] and the same set the parent feature searched for its own disclosure [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.12 Precedent In This Repository].

**Why the value is `partial` and not `none`, and why it is not `near-identical` either.** The verdict splits cleanly along one line: the *computation* of a price delta is shipped, taught and tested in this repository, while the *pre-commit read* of one has no analogue anywhere in the searched set.

- **Shipped, and therefore precedent.** `OrderLine.unitPriceChangeSinceAdded` and `unitPriceWithTaxChangeSinceAdded` are calculated getters that report a non-zero delta once a line is in an order [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L163-L187], derived from `initialListPrice` [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L113], `listPrice` [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L121] and `listPriceIncludesTax` [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L128]. Both are already published on the Shop API [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/order.type.graphql:L133] and [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/order.type.graphql:L137]. A signed integer delta over a repriced variant is therefore not a new idea in this codebase.
- **Also shipped: the decision about which price wins.** `ChangedPriceHandlingStrategy` declares one method, `handlePriceChange` [packages/core/src/config/order/changed-price-handling-strategy.ts:L32], called when adding to or adjusting order lines where the latest price differs from the price at the time the line was created [packages/core/src/config/order/changed-price-handling-strategy.ts:L25-L30]. Its own documentation states that by default the latest price is used and that the resulting change is reflected in the GraphQL `OrderLine.unitPrice[WithTax]ChangeSinceAdded` field [packages/core/src/config/order/changed-price-handling-strategy.ts:L12-L13], and that it is configured through the `orderOptions.changedPriceHandlingStrategy` property of the deployment's configuration [packages/core/src/config/order/changed-price-handling-strategy.ts:L17-L18].
- **And tested.** A shipped specification exercises the whole mechanism [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L44]. Its first case asserts that the change field starts at exactly 0 [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L76] while the unit price is a non-zero integer [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L77] and the strategy has not been called at all [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L78]. A later case asserts 626 after a reprice and a second add [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L106], and the adjust-path case asserts **-2374** [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L131].
- **Not shipped anywhere in the searched set: a pre-commit read.** The closest analogue for the shape of a customer-scoped Shop API addition is the wishlist example plugin, and its entire surface is three operations — `activeCustomerWishlist`, `addToWishlist(productVariantId: ID!)` and `removeFromWishlist(itemId: ID!)` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L12-L19]. **It carries no price field at all**, no stock field, and no comparison of any kind. What this story borrows from it is one mechanism and nothing else: the `extend type Query` idiom for adding an operation without touching an existing one [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L12-L14].

### 3.1 The Do-Not-Duplicate Boundary, And The One Sentence Of Novelty

The epic's inventory forbids rebuilding either shipped mechanism, and states the prohibition in terms this story is held to: do not add a plugin-owned column, field or getter that recomputes an in-cart price delta, and do not implement plugin logic that decides which price wins after an add [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§5. Existing Mechanisms That Must Not Be Duplicated]. The parent feature carries the same two prohibitions with its own reasoning [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.7 The Existing Mechanisms This Feature Consumes Rather Than Rebuilds].

**The one sentence of novelty: the shipped mechanism requires an `OrderLine` to exist, and this story's read happens when no order line exists yet.**

Everything else follows from that sentence and is checkable rather than rhetorical. The getters compare a line against its own history — `initialListPrice` is documented as the price calculated when the line was **first added to that Order** [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L113] — so on a placed historical order both operands belong to that order and neither is the price the variant carries today. The comparison a reorder buyer wants spans **what they paid** on a past line and **what the variant costs now**, and the existing getter never brings those two operands together because one of them is not in the order at all [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.3 Why This Is Not Already Solved].

**The unflattering half of this disclosure is required output, so it is stated plainly.** This story looks more original than it is. Delta reporting, signed integers, tax-basis reconciliation and a repriced-variant test fixture all exist in this checkout already, and any wording implying this story invents them would read as false to a reviewer holding the specification above. What it adds is narrow and it is stated narrowly: the *timing* of the read, and the *correlation* of a chosen source's lines against today's catalogue in one response. That is the whole of the residue, and it is enough — the parent feature notes that this feature's risk is the reverse of its siblings', being that it looks novel while sitting one step from a shipped mechanism [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.12 Precedent In This Repository].

## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line when the file is read on any renderer and when it is pasted into an external tracker. **Every story file in this set uses that same convention**, so a reader comparing two stories compares like with like.

```text
As a Returning Buyer,
I want to see, for every line of a past order I am about to reorder, what I paid for it and what it costs now,
So that I learn which items were repriced before any of them reaches my cart, instead of discovering it at checkout.
```

### 4.1 INVEST Criteria

- **Independent** — **no story inside this feature is a prerequisite.** This story and STORY-001-03-02 read different operands over the same already-resolved source lines, share no new state because this feature introduces none, and can be built in either order or concurrently [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§4.3 Intra-Feature Story Order]. Independence is claimed at that scope and no wider: the feature-level dependency on FEATURE-001-02 is real, it is the reason section 2.1 records a runner-up placing, and section 8 classifies it rather than leaving it implied.
- **Negotiable** — parts of the shape are open and parts are deliberately not. Whether the source order is addressed by its `code` [packages/core/src/entity/order/order.entity.ts:L70] or by its id is a design choice this story may settle either way, and the maximum number of source lines one call accepts is an open product decision this story may not invent [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. **What is not negotiable is that the input types are reused from FEATURE-001-02 rather than mirrored** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces], that both deltas are reported separately on their own tax basis, that an absent historical operand yields an absent delta rather than a zero, and that every monetary value is an integer in the smallest unit of a named currency.
- **Valuable** — it is the first point at which a Returning Buyer learns that an item was repriced **before** committing to it, which is the clause C3 promise in section 2 and the one step of the buyer journey this story owns [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.2 Contribution To The Epic]. It delivers that value on its own: a storefront can render the comparison from one response with no further story merged.
- **Estimable** — the operands, the arithmetic and the tax-basis rule all already exist and are cited, the nearest shipped behavioural analogue is a specification in this checkout [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L44], and the nearest structural analogue is a three-operation example plugin [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L12-L19]. The surface to be written is therefore bounded and countable. Section 9 carries the numbers.
- **Sized Appropriately** — one query field, one read method added to a service that already exists after FEATURE-001-02, one bulk variant load, and a per-line two-operand reconciliation, over **zero new tables, zero migrations, zero permission definitions and zero asynchronous work** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.11 No New Table, No New Error Result]. It is not a candidate for splitting: removing the tax-basis reconciliation or the absent-delta encoding would leave a payload that reports a number without saying what it means.
- **Testable** — every criterion in section 5 names one published GraphQL operation and asserts an exact integer in the smallest unit of a named currency, an exact error type name, an exact line count, or the byte-equality of two responses. No criterion asserts a state that has to be judged, and none states a figure this repository does not declare.

**Step 4 — the one operation, and this is the whole demonstration.** Back in the customer session, execute:

```graphql

    sourceState
    readAt
    sourceCurrencyCode
    targetCurrencyCode
    lines {
      productVariantId
      requestedQuantity

```

**Expected response, and it is exact rather than indicative.** `lines` holds one entry for the source order's single line. `historicalUnitPrice` equals the `unitPrice` noted in step 3. `currentCataloguePrice` equals the integer the variant was repriced to. `unitPriceDelta` equals the second minus the first, as a positive integer. `currencyCode` equals the channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88], and every one of the three monetary fields is an integer in the smallest unit of that currency. `readAt` carries the instant the read was taken.

**The second half of the demonstration is what did *not* happen, and it is the half worth showing a non-coder.** Execute `activeOrder` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L11] immediately afterwards: it returns exactly what it returned before the preview. **No cart line was created, no price was locked and no stock was held.** Running the preview a second time after a further reprice returns the newer integer, which demonstrates the point-in-time property directly rather than describing it.

### 4.3 The Prospective Operation And Payload This Story Fixes

Everything in this sub-section is a **target named in ticket text**. None of it exists in this checkout, so none of it carries a citation, and a downstream reader must not treat any identifier here as verified. The type names were checked as free against the checked-in Shop API introspection snapshot [schema-shop.json:data.__schema.types]. This story is the first of its feature, so the shape it settles is inherited by STORY-001-03-02 and STORY-001-03-03 rather than re-decided by them.

The operation is one **Shop API query**, added through the plugin's `shopApiExtensions` using `extend type Query`, following the idiom the shipped wishlist example demonstrates for a query over the authenticated customer's own data [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L12-L14]. It is additive: the root `Query` type declared nineteen root queries **before any feature of this plugin** [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L1-L52] and this story adds **exactly one field** to it **by addition rather than by modification**, with every pre-existing signature byte-identical afterwards. **The claim is a delta of one against the captured baseline rather than a total of twenty**, because FEATURE-001-01 contributes two root queries and FEATURE-001-05 one, taking the plugin's cumulative total to twenty-three [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings].

```graphql
extend type Query {
  reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!): ReorderPreview!
}
```

**The return type is the payload itself and not a result union, and that is a settled decision rather than a simplification.** An earlier revision of this story and of its parent published `ReorderPreviewResult` as a union while simultaneously requiring that an absent source, another customer's source and another channel's source be indistinguishable — two requirements that cannot both hold, because returning a not-found member for an id that is not the caller's *is* the enumeration channel it was meant to close. The settled contract is one always-representable payload carrying a `sourceState` of `RESOLVED` or `NOT_RESOLVED`, with the parent feature holding the full field list [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces].

- **The input carries a source selector.** Either the id of a placed order belonging to the authenticated customer, or the id of a `ReorderList` owned by that customer — the list entity being FEATURE-001-01's, named here and not defined here [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched]. **The selector type is FEATURE-001-02's `ReorderSourceInput`, reused rather than mirrored**, so the preview and the commit describe the same set of lines by construction instead of by coincidence [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces].
- **The input carries an optional per-line selection with a quantity override**, likewise FEATURE-001-02's `ReorderLineSelectionInput`. Omitting it means every line of the source. Supplying a quantity is what lets a buyer ask "what would three of these cost now", and it is also the only genuine invalid-input path this story has — which is why section 5 gives it a criterion of its own.
- **The result carries, for every projected line:** the variant reference, the requested quantity as an integer, **every contributing source line inside the entry's own `contributors` collection with that line's own recorded prices**, the **historical undiscounted** unit price pair, the **historical paid** pair which is reported and never differenced, the **current catalogue** unit price pair, and the **signed** integer difference on matched bases — each monetary field read against the response's two currency codes, `sourceCurrencyCode` for the historical figures and `targetCurrencyCode` for the current ones, on one stated tax basis. Each entry also carries a `requestIndex` matching the index the commit mutation assigns to the same line, so a storefront can join a preview to a commit outcome without guessing.
- **The historical operand is settled, and the choice is not this story's to reopen — but the *description* of it is corrected here.** The differenced operand is `OrderLine.unitPrice` and `unitPriceWithTax` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/order.type.graphql:L127-L129], which the platform documents as the price of a single unit **excluding tax and discounts** [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L146-L152] — **not** "what the buyer was actually charged", which an earlier revision of this bullet claimed. The actually-charged figure is `proratedUnitPrice` and its gross partner, documented as the true economic value of a single unit after order-level discounts are prorated [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L213-L221]; this story publishes that pair beside the first, labelled as the paid figure, and **never differences it against a catalogue price**, because the catalogue price is undiscounted and the subtraction would report a lapsed promotion as a price rise. The differenced pair is the right operand precisely because it is undiscounted on both sides, and it arrives as a matched net-and-gross pair. It is **not** `listPrice` [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L121] and **not** `initialListPrice` [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L113], which are pre-adjustment catalogue prices carried for the platform's own change getters; comparing either against today's catalogue would answer a different question from the one a buyer asks. The parent feature chose among all four candidates with reasons and its definition of done restates the exclusion [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces] and [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§5. Definition of Done (Feature-Level)]. **One discrepancy inside the parent has since been closed, and it is recorded here rather than dropped:** its dependency list named `listPrice` as the historical operand while its own section 2.6 and its definition of done named the charged unit pair, and that dependency line now names `unitPrice` and `unitPriceWithTax` too [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§4.4 Existing Entities, Services And Configuration Required]. All three statements in the parent and this story now name one pair. This story uses `listPriceIncludesTax` [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L128] only as the record of the historical **tax basis**, which is a different use from being an operand and is why that column still appears here at all.
- **The current operand is a per-request computation, not a stored row.** `ProductVariant.price` and `priceWithTax` are calculated getters that resolve the tax basis before rounding [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L76] and [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L92], published as `price: Money!` and `priceWithTax: Money!` with `currencyCode: CurrencyCode!` alongside [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L53], [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L55] and [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L54].

**Money invariant, and it needs two currency codes rather than one.** Every monetary value is an **integer in the smallest unit of its currency, stated alongside the currency code it belongs to**. The response carries **two** codes: `targetCurrencyCode`, the currency the request resolved to — the channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` unless the request selected another of the channel's available codes [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88] and [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L89-L91], resolved by the platform's own currency resolution which refuses a code the channel does not offer [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L147-L156] — and `sourceCurrencyCode`, the code the source order permanently recorded [packages/core/src/entity/order/order.entity.ts:L142]. Every current catalogue figure is in the target currency and every historical figure in the source currency, **so a delta is computed only where the two codes are equal and is null where they differ**: one code could not have said which currency a subtraction was performed in, and no conversion is attempted because this repository declares no rate. A decimal monetary value anywhere in this story is a defect, not a formatting preference. The platform models money as an integer column in its shipped strategy, and the published scalar is declared as `scalar Money` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-types.graphql:L7]. **The honest qualification belongs here rather than in a footnote:** that scalar is based on the native float scalar [packages/core/src/api/config/money-scalar.ts:L29] and its own description admits signed fractional values [packages/core/src/api/config/money-scalar.ts:L33-L35], so the schema does **not** enforce the integer form. This story's payload documentation states it, and STORY-001-03-03 owns publishing that statement.

**Signed-delta convention, and it must cover a decrease.** The difference is `current minus historical`, so a rise is positive and a **reduction is negative**. That is not a preference: the repository's own specification already asserts a negative value on the equivalent field [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L131] alongside a positive one [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L106] and a zero [packages/core/e2e/order-changed-price-handling.e2e-spec.ts:L76]. A story covering only a price rise would be incomplete, so section 5 gives the decrease its own criterion. **Net is subtracted from net and gross from gross**, which is why there are two deltas rather than one and why a mixed-basis subtraction is unrepresentable rather than merely discouraged.

**Absent is not zero, and there are now three reasons a delta is absent rather than one.** A delta is **absent** where the source line records no historical price at all — a list-sourced line records none; where the source and target currency codes differ, for the reason the money invariant gives; and where a projected key's contributors recorded **different** historical prices, in which case both key-level historical fields are null, `historicalPriceVaries` is true, and each contributor's own figures are carried on its own `contributors` entry. Reporting any of the three as a zero delta would assert that nothing changed, which is a false statement dressed as a number; **averaging two contributors' prices would be worse**, because the mean is a figure the buyer never paid. An unchanged price, by contrast, reports a delta of exactly 0, and all four states are distinguishable in the response.

**Error channel, and this story fixes it for the feature.** This feature declares **none** of the six new error results in this ticket set [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.11 No New Table, No New Error Result], so it contributes no member to the published `ErrorCode` enum, whose members are derived from every type implementing the `ErrorResult` interface [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-error-code-enum.ts:L9]. Two paths follow:

- **A non-positive or over-maximum quantity override is refused by a thrown input error, and this replaces an earlier revision's use of `NegativeQuantityError`.** That type's own description covers "attempting to set a negative OrderLine quantity" [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L43-L47] and the platform returns it for `quantity < 0` only [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2267-L2271], so it describes neither a zero nor a preview input, and returning it here would have named an error for a condition it does not describe. The settled path is the one FEATURE-001-02's shared selection contract fixes for every operation that accepts these inputs: the platform's own `UserInputError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L26-L30], observed by a client as `extensions.code` of `USER_INPUT_ERROR` with the plugin-owned key `error.reorder-selection-quantity-out-of-range`, thrown before any projection, variant load or price read [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces]. **The consequence for the result type is what closes the union question:** with no error returned as a value, the operation needs no union and returns `ReorderPreview!` directly, which is also what makes the non-disclosure below representable rather than self-contradictory. The other rules of that contract — one source field set, one id form per entry matching the source, no duplicate id, no over-size array, every id naming a line of the resolved source — apply here unchanged and unweakened, each with its own key. This story adds no error result and no enum member the two error results its sibling features declare [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces], and does not list this one. Because the published payload shape is STORY-001-03-03's to close, the union-membership question is referred there and to the epic's collision section rather than settled here by widening a sibling's contract [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.1 Repository-Discovered Collisions].
- **An unresolvable, unowned or foreign-channel source returns `sourceState` of `NOT_RESOLVED`, zero preview lines and no error type at all**, and an unauthenticated request returns that same shape rather than a refusal, because this is a read and the shipped read convention normalises rather than refuses [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings]. The precedent is published and exact: `order(id: ID!): Order` is nullable [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L34] and its own description states that only orders belonging to the currently-authenticated user may be queried [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L31-L32]. Following it keeps this story inside the verified error vocabulary and closes an enumeration channel: a buyer cannot learn whether another customer's order exists by asking. **No error type is invented here.** Where one appears to be needed, it is referred to the epic's collision section rather than named into existence.

**Point-in-time, not a reservation.** The result is a read taken at `readAt` and nothing more. It holds no stock, locks no price, creates no allocation and confers no entitlement, and no field in it may be named or documented as a quote, a total, an expected charge or a locked price [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.10 A Point-In-Time Read Is Not A Reservation]. Between the read and a later commit an administrator can reprice the variant and another order can consume the stock, so **a preview and a commit disagreeing is expected behaviour rather than a defect** — and the commit is the authoritative one. **No freshness window is stated, in any unit**, because this repository declares none and a stated window would imply a guarantee the read does not make. Section 5 asserts this inside a criterion rather than leaving it to prose.

**Channel and language scoping.** Both are properties of the request rather than arguments a caller may omit [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.5 Request Prerequisites]. The channel is identified by a unique token [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62] read from a request header whose key defaults to `vendure-token` [packages/core/src/config/vendure-config.ts:L176-L184] and is taken from the request at [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L130-L131]; the language comes from a `languageCode` query parameter and otherwise falls back to the channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L136-L145] and [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74]. A source belonging to a different channel token does not resolve, and **the boundary matters more to a preview than to a write**: both operands are channel-resolved, so a cross-channel read would subtract two figures that are each accurate in their own channel and meaningless against one another. The tax basis of the current operand follows `Channel.pricesIncludeTax` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L113]. This story stores and translates no string of its own, so nothing it adds is language-varying.

**Authorisation, and a read is not exempt.** The query is gated `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` and registers **no custom permission definition**, because the only permission a customer session can hold is `Permission.Authenticated` [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L443] and the Customer Role cannot be amended to hold more [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L290-L291]. **`Permission.Owner` is held by nobody** — it is declared `assignable: false` and `internal: true` [packages/core/src/common/constants.ts:L27-L31] — so what the gate achieves is that the guard sets `ctx.authorizedAsOwnerOnly` on the request context [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L104-L110]; no criterion in this story says a session holds it. The gate alone is not the control: the platform states that a resolver relying on ownership without enforcing it is the equivalent of public access [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-permissions.ts:L32-L34], and the owner gate is admitted for a session that does not hold the permission being checked [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L109]. **The control is the service-layer owner-and-channel predicate**, applied by the same service the commit path uses. This is not a formality on a read path: the entire content of a preview is one customer's purchase history.

**Read shape.** Variants are hydrated in **one** bulk call [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L150] rather than one call per line, duplicate source lines are projected onto one entry per distinct variant before anything is read, and the source size is bounded by the commit path's own configured maximum with an over-bound request **refused rather than truncated**, by a thrown `UserInputError` carrying `error.reorder-selection-above-maximum` before any variant is loaded [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L26-L30] — a silently truncated preview would report that nothing changed about lines it never looked at [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.5.2 The Read Shape]. Section 10 makes the query count an asserted number rather than a described intention.

## 5. Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria. **Each names exactly one published GraphQL operation under test**, so a failure points at a single contract. Criteria name operations, their arguments, and entity and column names as database preconditions; none names a resolver class, a file path or an internal method name, because those are not published contracts. The narrative in section 4 is not restated here — the story carries the value and the criteria carry the testable outcomes.

Three preconditions hold for every criterion without being repeated in each, because they are properties of the request rather than arguments a caller may omit [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.5 Request Prerequisites]: an authenticated session, a channel resolved from the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62], and a language resolved from the channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74]. Each criterion still names its channel token and language code, because a monetary figure is meaningless without the scope that resolved it. **AC-6 is the deliberate exception to the first of the three**, since one of its branches removes the session on purpose.

**How the eight map onto the five required coverage classes.** Valid input is AC-1. Invalid or incomplete input is AC-5 and AC-6. An error naming the exact type returned is AC-5. Edge cases are AC-2, AC-3, AC-4 and AC-7. An existing operation asserted unchanged is AC-8, which does double duty as the zero-write proof. **AC-2, AC-3 and AC-4 exist as three criteria where one would have been tempting**, because the three encodings a delta can take are the three an implementation most often collapses: a signed negative, an exact zero, and an absence. Collapsing any two of them into one criterion would let a test pass on the wrong encoding.

Every monetary figure below is an integer in the smallest unit of the currency named beside it. The example integers are **data preconditions a test author sets**, not targets and not measurements. No criterion states a service level, a timing figure, a rate or a business figure, because this repository declares none [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§2.2 Business Value].

**A note on AC-6, and on how the non-disclosure comparison is actually performed, because an earlier version of this criterion asked for something no implementation can deliver.** It required the three responses to be **byte-identical**, and they cannot be: the wrapper carries `readAt`, the instant that read was taken [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces], so three responses taken at three instants differ in that field by design. A criterion no implementation can satisfy is worse than a loose one, because a conforming implementation fails it. The comparison is therefore specified as follows and the specification is part of the criterion rather than a note about it:

- **Every disclosure-bearing field is compared and none is exempted by omission.** The comparison covers `__typename`, the entry list and its length, `currencyCode`, and every field of the source wrapper — the fields through which an inference about the source could travel. A test that compares only the entry list would pass while the source wrapper echoed the requested identifier back.
- **`readAt` is the single excluded field, and the exclusion is explicit rather than incidental.** The assertion names it as excluded, so a reviewer sees which field is not being compared instead of discovering it from a passing test.
- **Time is not frozen to make the comparison work.** Replacing the clock — a fake timer, a fixed system time, an injected clock stub — would make three responses byte-identical and would prove nothing about non-disclosure, because the property under test is that the *disclosure-bearing* fields do not vary with the source, not that the timestamp does not vary with the clock. The three calls are issued against the real clock and the real server, exactly as a caller would issue them.
- **The same rule governs every other response-equality assertion in this story and in its two siblings**, including the empty-source scenario in section 7, which asserts equality against the AC-6 response under the same field set and the same single exclusion.

## 7. Edge Cases

Five scenarios: the three categories required of every story in this set, plus the two conditional categories that apply because this story reads a mutable value and reads across a request scope. **Each is labelled with its category inline**, so the category matrix never appears as a table in a story file.

* Scenario: Category — zero, null or empty collection. A source that resolves to no eligible line at all.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer on the channel token `e2e-default-channel` [packages/testing/src/config/test-config.ts:L15] whose `lines` collection is empty, and separately a `ReorderList` owned by that buyer carrying no line
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed against each source in turn
   * Then each call returns **zero preview lines and no error type**, with `sourceState` of `RESOLVED` — the source *was* the caller's own, it simply held nothing — and the response still carrying its `readAt` and both its currency codes so a storefront can render an empty comparison rather than an error state. **This is the one case that differs observably from AC-6's `NOT_RESOLVED` shape, and the difference discloses nothing about another buyer**: it separates "your source is empty" from "that identifier is not yours", which is exactly the distinction a nullable single-entity read already makes. No row is written and no cart is created. The response is equal to the non-disclosing response of AC-6 **in every declared field except `readAt`**, compared under the field set and the single exclusion the note on AC-6 fixes, which is deliberate: an empty source and an unreadable source must not be distinguishable, or the difference becomes an enumeration channel

* Scenario: Category — an item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase. A source line whose variant no longer resolves.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer carrying three lines, one of which references a variant whose `enabled` column is now false [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L53] or whose `deletedAt` column is now set [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L48]
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed with a source naming that order's id
   * Then **the other two lines report their operands and deltas as normal, and the request does not fail** — a preview whose whole purpose is per-line disclosure cannot discard the basket because one item was withdrawn. The affected line is still present at its own `requestIndex`, carrying its `sourceOrderLineId`, its `productVariantId` and **both historical operands**, and reporting `availability` of `NOT_ADDABLE_VARIANT_UNAVAILABLE` with `addableQuantity` of exactly 0. Its `currentCataloguePrice`, `currentCataloguePriceWithTax`, `productVariant` and `stockLevel` are **all four null together**, which is the parent's single unavailable branch rather than four independent absences [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces], and both deltas are consequently absent because there is no current operand to subtract from — the same absent-not-zero rule AC-4 asserts

* Scenario: Category — a price changed since the last purchase. The category stated in its own right, even though it is this story's core case.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer carrying two lines whose recorded `unitPrice` values are 129900 and 4500 [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/order.type.graphql:L127-L129], the first line's variant having since been repriced upward and the second's not repriced at all
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed with a source naming that order's id
   * Then the two lines are reported **differently and both are reported**: the first carries a non-zero signed integer delta and the second carries a delta of exactly the integer 0, in one response, on one currency code and one tax basis. The mixed case is the scenario rather than either pure case, because an implementation that reported only moved lines would leave a storefront unable to distinguish "unchanged" from "not looked at"

* Scenario: Category — a point-in-time read gone stale between check and commit. The read is re-evaluated at commit and confers nothing.
   * Given a preview taken over a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer, reporting a current catalogue price of the integer 134900; and, after that response is returned, the variant repriced by an administrator, **and separately** a deployment whose `orderOptions.changedPriceHandlingStrategy` is configured to hold the earlier price [packages/core/src/config/order/changed-price-handling-strategy.ts:L17-L18]
   * When the buyer proceeds to commit the reorder through the mutation FEATURE-001-02 owns
   * Then the commit re-reads the price and **is authoritative over the preview**, so the committed figure may differ from the previewed one in both arrangements: after a reprice because the catalogue moved, and under the held-price configuration because the deployment decided which price wins [packages/core/src/config/order/changed-price-handling-strategy.ts:L12-L13]. **Both outcomes are specified behaviour and neither is a defect.** The preview is not corrected to guess the strategy's outcome, because inferring a deployment-owned decision is exactly the duplication the epic's inventory forbids [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§5. Existing Mechanisms That Must Not Be Duplicated]. No freshness window is stated in any unit, and the payload documentation carries the caveat instead [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.9a The Reported Price Is The Current Catalogue Price]

* Scenario: Category — a channel, currency or language mismatch between the original order and the reorder context. The source sits in one channel and the preview is requested from another.
   * Given a placed source order created under one channel token and owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer, and that same buyer requesting a preview under a **different** channel token carried in the `vendure-token` header [packages/core/src/config/vendure-config.ts:L176-L184], the two channels having different `defaultCurrencyCode` values [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88]
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed with a source naming that order's id
   * Then the source **does not resolve**, `sourceState` is `NOT_RESOLVED` and the response carries zero preview lines, identical to AC-6's non-disclosing response. **A second arrangement is asserted in the same scenario because it is the one a channel check does not catch:** where the source order was placed *in the same channel* but recorded a `currencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/order/order.entity.ts:L142] other than the one the request resolves to — which a channel whose `availableCurrencyCodes` column lists more than one code permits [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L89-L91] — the source **does** resolve, every line is reported with its current catalogue price in `targetCurrencyCode` and its historical figures in `sourceCurrencyCode`, and **both deltas are null with no conversion attempted**. The boundary is not crossed and no operand from the other channel is read, because subtracting a price resolved in one channel from a price resolved in another yields a figure that is arithmetically well-formed and meaningless. Where a source *does* resolve, the reported `currencyCode` is the **requesting** channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88] and never the source order's, and the language follows the requesting channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74] by the documented fallback [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L136-L145]
