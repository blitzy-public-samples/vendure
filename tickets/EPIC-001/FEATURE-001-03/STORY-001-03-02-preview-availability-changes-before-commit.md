## 1. Story Title

**Report the availability of every prospective reorder line through the same read-only Shop API query, so that a Returning Buyer learns which items cannot be obtained at the quantity they want before any cart line is written.**

Short form, transcribed from the epic's story table without rewording so that the two files agree: **Preview availability changes before commit** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table].

The unit of work the title names is one finished slice, and it is an **addition to an operation that STORY-001-03-01 introduces** rather than a second operation. The query already exists after that story; this story adds the availability fields to each entry it returns, resolves them through the platform's own composed stock call, and classifies each prospective line into one of the four states the parent feature fixes. **It writes nothing, it holds nothing, and it reserves nothing.**

Three things that could be mistaken for part of it are not:

- **The price half is STORY-001-03-01's**, including the operation's input shape, its error channel and its integer-money invariant. This story inherits all three and re-decides none of them.
- **The published payload contract is STORY-001-03-03's**, including the absent-versus-zero encoding and the staleness caveat that has to appear in the schema documentation rather than only in a ticket.
- **Resolving an unobtainable line — skipping it, reducing it to the quantity that is obtainable, substituting it or abandoning the request — is FEATURE-001-04's.** This story reports, and stops.

**One clarification the title cannot carry, and it is the one that decides whether this story is worth building.** The commit path this preview precedes does not treat every unobtainable line the same way. A quantity or stock shortfall is accumulated per item and reported alongside the lines that did succeed, but a variant that is disabled or soft-deleted makes the platform's bulk add **throw and abandon the whole batch**. Section 4.3 sets out the evidence line by line. That asymmetry is why reporting availability *before* the write is worth an operation of its own, and it is the argument this story leads with rather than files as a footnote.

## 2. Source Traceability

The label is a **quoted objective clause**. The clause is C4 [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§10.2 Objective-Clause Map], reproduced verbatim from the objective statement:

> "before they commit to a reorder"

Its origin is the objective statement itself, and the epic's clause map assigns it to two stories — this one and STORY-001-04-01 [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§10.2 Objective-Clause Map]. The pairing is deliberate rather than accidental: the adjoining clause C3, on buyers being made aware of price and availability changes, lands on STORY-001-03-01, so **the two halves of one sentence are carried by two sibling stories** and this feature is the only one holding a story against each [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.2 Contribution To The Epic].

Read the clause precisely, because it decides what this story is measured against. C4 is a claim about **timing**, not about outcome. It does not promise that a reorder will succeed, that stock will be held, or that the availability reported will still hold at the moment of the write — and section 4.3 is explicit that it cannot promise any of those. What it promises is that the buyer is told **before** the commit rather than after it, so the observable outcome is the presence of a per-line availability classification in a response that is returned while the cart is still untouched.

Two consequences follow, and both are carried into section 5 rather than left here:

- **A timing claim is verified by a state assertion, not by a measurement.** The verifiable outcomes are an exact availability token, an exact classification, an exact entry count and the demonstrable fact that the cart is unchanged after the read. This repository declares no service level, latency figure or business target, and the epic reports that absence as a finding rather than filling it in [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§2.2 Business Value]. None is supplied here, and none is needed.
- **"Before" is not "instead of".** Because the read confers nothing, the commit still decides. A story that read C4 as a guarantee would have to hold stock, and holding stock against an uncommitted intention is a write that this feature does not perform [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.10 A Point-In-Time Read Is Not A Reservation].

### 3.1 The Do-Not-Duplicate Boundary, And The One Sentence Of Novelty

The epic's inventory forbids rebuilding the price mechanisms and forbids bypassing the stock ones [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§5. Existing Mechanisms That Must Not Be Duplicated]. The price half of that boundary is inherited from the sibling story rather than re-argued: the in-cart, post-add change is already reported by the calculated getters `unitPriceChangeSinceAdded` and `unitPriceWithTaxChangeSinceAdded` [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L163-L187], and the decision about which price wins after an add is already a deployment-owned configuration point, `handlePriceChange` [packages/core/src/config/order/changed-price-handling-strategy.ts:L32]. **Only the before-commit read is new**, and the parent feature proves that with a shipped specification rather than asserting it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.3 Why This Is Not Already Solved].

**The one sentence of novelty: the shipped availability field answers "is this variant in stock" one variant at a time and knows nothing about a quantity, a source or a cart, and this story answers "which lines of the source I chose can I obtain at the quantities I want, in the cart I already have".**

Everything else follows from that sentence. The existing field takes no quantity argument [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L56]; the saleable computation behind it is per variant [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L323]; and the stock layer beneath it resolves one variant at a time before delegating to the configured location strategy [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L72] and [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L79]. Correlation across a set of lines, at a requested quantity, against the buyer's own active order, is the residue — and the parent feature settles the arithmetic of it so that this story consumes a rule rather than choosing one [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.7a The Preview Is Computed Against The Target Cart].

**The unflattering half of this disclosure is required output, so it is stated plainly.** Nothing about reading stock is novel here. The three tokens, the threshold arithmetic, the untracked short-circuit, the location strategy and the test fixture that sets four stock columns all exist in this checkout already, and a reviewer holding the shipped specification would reject any wording implying otherwise. What this story adds is the correlation and the timing. That is narrow, it is stated narrowly, and it is enough — because the commit path's asymmetric treatment of a withdrawn variant, set out in section 4.3, means a buyer who is not told beforehand loses the whole batch rather than one line.

## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line when the file is read on any renderer and when it is pasted into an external tracker. **Every story file in this set uses that same convention**, so a reader comparing two stories compares like with like.

```text
As a Returning Buyer,
I want to see, for every line of a past order or saved list I am about to reorder, whether I can still obtain it at the quantity I want,
So that I learn which items have gone out of stock or been withdrawn before I commit the reorder, instead of losing the whole basket at the moment I submit it.
```

Independence is claimed at that scope and no wider: the feature-level dependency on FEATURE-001-02 is real and section 8 classifies it rather than leaving it implied.
- **Negotiable** — parts of the shape are open and parts are deliberately not. Whether an unobtainable line is ordered first in the response, and whether the entry carries the deployment's own display string alongside the machine-readable state or only the latter, are choices this story may settle either way. **What is not negotiable** is that availability is resolved through the platform's composed call rather than recomputed, that no field publishes a raw stock figure, that the four availability states are the parent feature's [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces], and that the read is documented as conferring no reservation.
- **Valuable** — it is the point at which a Returning Buyer learns that an item is unobtainable **while the cart is still untouched**, which is the clause C4 promise in section 2. Its value does not depend on FEATURE-001-04 shipping: a storefront can render "two of these five lines cannot be obtained" from one response and let the buyer edit the request by hand, with no resolution mechanism merged at all.

- **Sized Appropriately** — availability fields added to one existing prospective payload, one composed platform call consumed once per distinct variant, and one clamp against the buyer's active order, over **zero new tables, zero migrations, zero new error results, zero permission definitions and zero asynchronous work** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.11 No New Table, No New Error Result]. It is not a candidate for splitting: separating the disabled-variant classification from the stock classification would ship a report that tells a buyer an item is available when the commit is about to reject the entire batch over it.
- **Testable** — every criterion in section 5 names one published GraphQL operation and asserts an exact availability token, an exact machine-readable state, an exact entry count, an exact error type name or the equality of two responses. Preconditions are database column values a test author sets, following the shipped specification's own pattern [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L595-L611]. ### 4.2 Demonstration Path

One path, concrete, and observable to a non-coder as one named operation with its inputs and its expected response. It ends on an **exact** availability token, so every step that produces it is written out rather than left to the reader to arrange. Every command is a subshell carrying its own working directory, so the steps can be pasted in sequence without a second directory change failing against the first; the two terminals are numbered, because one step occupies its terminal for as long as the demonstration lasts.

**On credentials, stated honestly rather than conveniently.** The operation under demonstration is a **Shop API query executed as an authenticated customer**, and no administrative session can reach it or is required to execute it — **this story exposes no Admin API surface of its own.** The documented default credentials [CONTRIBUTING.md:L189-L193] are needed for exactly one preparatory step: setting the stock columns on a seeded variant, which is how the shipped specification establishes the same precondition [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L595-L611]. No credential value is reproduced in this file; the location that documents them is cited instead.

**Step 1 — seed the catalogue and customers.** Run from the dev-server package, because neither script exists at the repository root:

```bash
# Terminal 1. Clears and seeds the development database.
( cd packages/dev-server && bun run populate )
```

That is the `populate` script [packages/dev-server/package.json:L8], invoked with the working directory the contribution guide shows [CONTRIBUTING.md:L147-L150].

**Step 2 — start the server.** This occupies Terminal 1 until interrupted:

```bash
# Terminal 1. Occupies this terminal for the rest of the demonstration.
( cd packages/dev-server && bun run dev )
```

That is the `dev` script [packages/dev-server/package.json:L13], again with the guide's working directory [CONTRIBUTING.md:L178-L181]. The Shop API is then reachable at `http://localhost:3000/shop-api`, because the shared port constant is 3000 [packages/common/src/shared-constants.ts:API_PORT] and the shop path constant is `shop-api` [packages/common/src/shared-constants.ts:SHOP_API_PATH]. The `vendure-token` header is omitted throughout, so the request resolves to the default channel, and the `languageCode` query parameter is omitted, so the language resolves to that channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74] by the documented fallback [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L136-L145].

**Step 3 — establish the preconditions, in Terminal 2.** Authenticate as a seeded customer, place one order carrying two lines through operations that already ship, and note its id. Then, in an administrative session, set the stock columns on the **first** line's variant so that its saleable level falls below one — `trackInventory` to the tracked value, `useGlobalOutOfStockThreshold` to false, its own `outOfStockThreshold` to 0 and its `stockOnHand` to 0 — leaving the second line's variant with a `stockOnHand` of 3 on the same settings. The saleable level is `stockOnHand` minus `stockAllocated` minus the effective threshold [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L336-L340], so the two variants resolve to different tokens.

**Step 4 — the one operation, and this is the whole demonstration.** Back in the customer session, execute:

```graphql
query {
  reorderPreview(input: { source: { sourceOrderId: "<the placed order id>" } }) {
    ... on ReorderPreview {
      readAt
      lines {
        productVariantId
        requestedQuantity
        addableQuantity
        availability
        stockLevel
      }
    }
  }
}
```

**The expected response, named exactly.** Two entries are returned in one response. The first carries `stockLevel` of exactly `OUT_OF_STOCK` [packages/core/src/config/catalog/default-stock-display-strategy.ts:L17-L21], `availability` of `NOT_ADDABLE_OUT_OF_STOCK` and an `addableQuantity` of 0. The second carries `stockLevel` of exactly `IN_STOCK`, `availability` of `ADDABLE_IN_FULL` and an `addableQuantity` equal to its `requestedQuantity`. Both tokens are the shipped `DefaultStockDisplayStrategy` output [packages/core/src/config/catalog/default-stock-display-strategy.ts:L14], which is the deployment default and is named as such rather than assumed. **The confirmation a non-coder can see for themselves:** `activeOrder` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L11] returns exactly what it returned before the query — the read created no cart, added no line and reserved nothing.

### 4.3 The Prospective Availability Fields And Their Preconditions

Everything named in this sub-section that does not carry a citation is a **target named in ticket text**. `reorderPreview`, `ReorderPreviewInput`, `ReorderPreview`, `ReorderLineAvailability` and the plugin package do not exist in this checkout, so none of them carries a citation and a downstream reader must not treat any of them as verified. The fields this story adds to each entry the sibling story's query already returns:

```graphql
"Added by this story to the entry type STORY-001-03-01 introduces."
type ReorderPreviewLine {
  "The deployment's own display token, passed through unchanged. Typed String, never an enum."
  stockLevel: String!
  "The plugin's own exhaustive machine-readable state."
  availability: ReorderLineAvailability!
  "min(requestedQuantity, max(saleable - alreadyHeldInCart, 0)). Never exceeds the request."
  addableQuantity: Int!
}
enum ReorderLineAvailability {
  ADDABLE_IN_FULL
  ADDABLE_AT_REDUCED_QUANTITY
  NOT_ADDABLE_OUT_OF_STOCK

```

#### 4.3.1 The Database Preconditions — And They Are Preconditions, Never Response Fields

Every column below is set by a test author or by seeded data **before** the query runs. **None of them is a field the Shop API returns**, and section 4.3.3 states that as a prohibition rather than a preference.

- **`StockLevel.stockOnHand`** [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L40] and **`StockLevel.stockAllocated`** [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L43], on a row keyed by `productVariantId` [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L30] and `stockLocationId` [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L37] under a unique composite index on the pair [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L19]. **Read that index precisely, because it decides what a precondition may assume:** it is unique on the *pair*, not on the variant, so one variant holds as many stock rows as there are locations stocking it. How those rows combine into one answer is the configured location strategy's decision [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L79], not an addition a test or a plugin may perform.
- **`ProductVariant.trackInventory`** [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L155] — a three-valued inherit flag rather than a boolean, which is why a precondition states which of the three values it sets.
- **`ProductVariant.useGlobalOutOfStockThreshold`** [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L152] and **`ProductVariant.outOfStockThreshold`** [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L144] — the pair that decides whether the variant defers to the deployment-wide threshold or supplies its own.
- **`ProductVariant.enabled`** [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L53] and the soft-delete column **`deletedAt`** [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L48] — the two states that make a line unobtainable irrespective of stock, and the subject of section 4.3.4.

#### 4.3.2 The Inventory Settings That Actually Govern — The Global Row, Not The Channel Columns

**This is the single easiest way to write a criterion that cannot pass, so it is stated as a ruling rather than as a note.** Every stock precondition in this story seeds **the single global settings row plus the variant-level fields above** — never a Channel column. That is the epic's binding ruling R6 [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings], and the parent feature carries the same rule for every story in this feature [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.9 Channel Scoping, Language Scoping And Monetary Values].

The reason is the code path, and it is checkable in this checkout:

- `getSaleableStockLevel` destructures `outOfStockThreshold` and `trackInventory` from the **global settings service** [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L323-L324], and its private threshold helper reads the same source [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L343-L344].
- That service returns **one** row, ordered by creation date and cached per request, with no channel predicate of any kind [packages/core/src/service/services/global-settings.service.ts:L63-L75].
- Even the component whose name promises channel awareness resolves a variant's effective settings the same way, reading the global pair and combining it with the variant's own two flags [packages/core/src/config/catalog/multi-channel-stock-location-strategy.ts:L181-L195].

**The documentation discrepancy this story references and does not follow.** `Channel.trackInventory` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L100] and `Channel.outOfStockThreshold` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L108] are declared as columns with their own descriptions [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L93-L98] and [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L102-L106], and the global pair carries `@deprecated` annotations naming them as the replacement [packages/core/src/entity/global-settings/global-settings.entity.ts:L32-L45]. **In 3.7.0 the annotation states an intent that the implementation has not reached**, and the asymmetry is worth naming: the two Channel columns carry no deprecation marker while the global pair does, so a reader following the annotations alone would seed exactly the values no read path consults. Meanwhile the `ProductVariant` comment directing a reader to the global settings for the threshold [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L146-L152] happens to describe the live path. The epic records all of this as one documentation discrepancy across three surfaces and corrects none of them in place, because this run is additive [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.3 Repository Documentation Discrepancies — Noted, Not Propagated]. **No criterion in section 5 sets a Channel column and then asserts an availability outcome from it**, and where availability must differ by channel the plugin computes the difference from the resolved request channel and section 5 says so.

#### 4.3.3 The Publicly Observable Outcome — One Token, And No Raw Number

The saleable level is `stockOnHand` minus `stockAllocated` minus the effective out-of-stock threshold, the effective threshold being the global value where the variant defers to it and the variant's own value otherwise [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L336-L340]. **Where inventory is not tracked the computation short-circuits to the maximum safe integer before issuing any stock query at all** [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L326-L331] — it does not fall through to zero, and section 5 asserts that behaviour rather than assuming a numeric level.

That level is then handed to the configured display strategy [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L364], and under the shipped default the outcome is **exactly one of three tokens**: `OUT_OF_STOCK` where the saleable level is below one, `LOW_STOCK` where it is at or below the strategy's `lowStockLevel`, and `IN_STOCK` otherwise [packages/core/src/config/catalog/default-stock-display-strategy.ts:L17-L21]. **`lowStockLevel` defaults to 2** [packages/core/src/config/catalog/default-stock-display-strategy.ts:L15], a figure declared in this repository and documented in the strategy's own description [packages/core/src/config/catalog/default-stock-display-strategy.ts:L9-L10]; using it is therefore a citation rather than an invented threshold, and it is cited every time it is used.

**A criterion asserting one of the three tokens names the shipped default strategy in its Given.** The field's published type is `String!` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L56], the strategy behind it is deployment-configurable through `catalogOptions.stockDisplayStrategy` [packages/core/src/config/catalog/stock-display-strategy.ts:L14], and the three tokens are one implementation's return values rather than an enumeration the schema constrains — so an unconditional assertion would assert a value a deployment is free to change [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.5.1 The Three Stock Tokens]. That is also why this story publishes `stockLevel` as `String!` and reports its own exhaustive `ReorderLineAvailability` enum beside it: narrowing the string to an enum would break a deployment that had supplied its own vocabulary.

**The prohibition, and it is absolute.** No field this story adds returns `stockOnHand`, `stockAllocated` or an unbounded saleable level — the epic's ruling R7 [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings]. The reason is the platform's own: the display strategy exists **because** directly exposing stock levels over a public API is treated as a leak of sensitive information, while a storefront still wants some indication of whether a variant is in stock [packages/core/src/config/catalog/stock-display-strategy.ts:L7-L10]. `StockLevel` is not a Shop API type at all — its absence from the checked-in introspection snapshot is asserted executably by the local validator suite rather than by a locator, because an absence is the one claim a locator cannot express [schema-shop.json:data.__schema.types] and [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§11.10 The Local Validator Suite]. Internally the two raw figures do exist: `getAvailableStock` returns the on-hand and allocated pair [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L72] as the `AvailableStock` shape [packages/core/src/config/catalog/stock-location-strategy.ts:L18-L21], by delegating to the configured location strategy [packages/core/src/config/catalog/stock-location-strategy.ts:L61]. **They stay internal**, and consuming the composed call rather than the parts is what makes that a property of the call graph rather than a rule someone must remember.

**The one integer that is published, and why its bound is the whole of its justification.** `addableQuantity` is `min(requestedQuantity, max(saleable − alreadyHeldInCart, 0))` — the same clamp the commit applies for the same inputs [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.7a The Preview Is Computed Against The Target Cart]. Because it is bounded above by the caller's own request, asking for two and being told two discloses that at least two exist and nothing more. The disclosure level is no greater than the platform's own, since `InsufficientStockError` already publishes `quantityAvailable: Int!` on a public Shop API mutation [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L50] and [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L53]; the change is only that a buyer learns it in time to act. **An untracked variant therefore reports `addableQuantity` equal to `requestedQuantity` and never the maximum safe integer** [packages/core/src/service/services/product-variant.service.ts:L326-L331].

**One combination is specified rather than contradictory.** Because the token describes the variant and the state describes the request in this cart, an entry may carry `stockLevel` of `IN_STOCK` alongside `availability` of `NOT_ADDABLE_OUT_OF_STOCK` — when the buyer's active order already holds every obtainable unit. That pairing is expected output, and section 5 asserts it rather than treating it as a defect.

#### 4.3.4 A Withdrawn Variant Is A Distinct Class — The Argument For This Story

`OrderService.addItemsToOrder` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L654] is the commit path this preview precedes, and its own documentation advertises that it accumulates per-item failures and advises checking the returned `errorResults` array [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L645-L653]. **It does that for a quantity or stock failure and not for a withdrawn variant, and the difference is visible in the control flow:**

- A quantity, state or order-limit failure is pushed onto the accumulator and the loop continues to the next item [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L681].
- A stock shortfall is clamped [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L703] and, where the clamp reaches zero, reported as an `InsufficientStockError` carrying the available quantity before the loop continues [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L710] and [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L712].
- **A variant that is disabled or soft-deleted is resolved through a call that throws rather than returns.** The lookup requires `enabled` to be true [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L687] and `deletedAt` to be null [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L688] on an entity fetch that raises when no row matches [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L684], and a disabled parent product throws explicitly [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L693]. **Nothing is accumulated, so the whole batch is abandoned.**

Two rules follow, and both are asserted in section 5:

- **A withdrawn variant is its own availability class, `NOT_ADDABLE_VARIANT_UNAVAILABLE`, distinct from a stock shortfall.** Collapsing the two would tell a buyer "out of stock, try later" about an item that will never return and whose presence in the request destroys the rest of it.
- **The plugin resolves and excludes such variants before delegating to the platform's bulk add**, which is the rule STORY-001-02-01 already fixed for the commit path and which this story agrees with rather than renegotiating [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings].

#### 4.3.5 This Story Reports; FEATURE-001-04 Resolves

The boundary is stated explicitly so this story does not annex the next feature's scope and so that feature can consume this vocabulary unchanged. **Reporting** an availability token, a machine-readable state and a bounded obtainable quantity is this story. **Resolving** — skipping the line, reducing it to the obtainable quantity, substituting another variant or abandoning the request — is FEATURE-001-04, and STORY-001-04-01 is the story that acts on the states named here [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§4.2 Downstream]. No criterion in section 5 asserts that a line was skipped, reduced, substituted or abandoned, because this story performs none of those.

#### 4.3.6 Point-In-Time, And Not A Reservation

The availability reported here is a read taken at one instant and nothing more. **It holds no stock, creates no allocation, confers no entitlement and expires from nothing**, and no field may be named or documented in a way that suggests otherwise [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.10 A Point-In-Time Read Is Not A Reservation]. Between this read and a later commit, another order can consume the same stock and an administrator can disable the variant, so **a preview and a commit disagreeing is specified behaviour rather than a defect** — and the commit is the authoritative one, because it re-evaluates each line at write time [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L703]. **No freshness window is stated, in any unit**, because this repository declares none and a stated window would imply a guarantee the read does not make. Section 5 asserts this inside a criterion rather than leaving it to prose, which is the whole point of stating it here.

#### 4.3.7 Channel And Language Scoping

Both are properties of the request rather than arguments a caller may omit [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.5 Request Prerequisites]. The channel is identified by a unique token [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62] carried in a request header whose key defaults to `vendure-token` [packages/core/src/config/vendure-config.ts:L176-L184] and taken from the request at [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L130-L131]; the language comes from a `languageCode` query parameter and otherwise falls back to the channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L136-L145] and [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74]. A source belonging to a different channel token does not resolve, and section 7 covers the mismatch.

Two scoping facts matter to availability specifically:

- **The stock rows a variant can be read through are channel-scoped where they are read per location:** `getStockLevelsForVariant` filters on the request context's channel [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L56] and [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L63]. So the same variant can resolve to a different token under a different channel token, and section 7 states that outcome plainly.
- **The threshold, by contrast, is not channel-scoped in this checkout**, per section 4.3.2. A multi-channel deployment can therefore return a channel-resolved availability answer computed against a deployment-wide threshold. That is the platform's current behaviour rather than something this story introduces, and the preview reports it as it is rather than compensating — compensating would mean disagreeing with `ProductVariant.stockLevel` about the same variant in the same request.

**Nothing this story adds is language-varying.** Every field is an identifier, an integer or a machine-readable token, and the availability token is deliberately not a sentence, so there is no string for this story to own in any translation catalogue. Monetary values are not this story's subject; where one appears it is an integer in the smallest unit of its currency stated alongside its `currencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88], which is the invariant STORY-001-03-01 fixed and this story inherits.

## 7. Edge Cases

Five scenarios: the three categories required of every story in this set, plus the two conditional categories that apply because this story reads a mutable value and resolves it within a request scope. **Each is labelled with its category inline**, so the category matrix never appears as a table in a story file.

* Scenario: Category — zero, null or empty collection. A source that resolves to no line, and a variant that has no stock row at all.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer on the channel token `e2e-default-channel` [packages/testing/src/config/test-config.ts:L15] whose `lines` collection is empty; separately a `ReorderList` owned by that buyer carrying no line; and separately a source order carrying one line whose variant has **no** `StockLevel` row for any location [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L19] while `trackInventory` is set to the tracked value [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L155]
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed against each source in turn
   * Then the first two calls return **zero entries and no error type**, with the response still carrying its `readAt` so a storefront renders an empty report rather than an error state; the third call **succeeds rather than raising**, resolving availability through the same service call [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L72] and reporting the token the configured strategy returns for a saleable level of zero, which under the shipped default is exactly `OUT_OF_STOCK` [packages/core/src/config/catalog/default-stock-display-strategy.ts:L17-L21], with an `addableQuantity` of 0. **No row is written by any of the three**, no stock row is created and no cart is created. The first two responses are identical to the non-disclosing response of AC-5, which is deliberate: an empty source and an unreadable source must not be distinguishable, or the difference becomes an enumeration channel

* Scenario: Category — an item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase. The whole point of the story, stated as its own scenario.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer carrying three lines, one referencing a variant whose `enabled` column is now false [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L53] and one referencing a variant whose `deletedAt` column is now set [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L48]
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed with a source naming that order's id
   * Then both affected lines are **reported rather than thrown**, each carrying `NOT_ADDABLE_VARIANT_UNAVAILABLE` and an `addableQuantity` of 0, and the third line reports its own token as normal — a preview whose whole purpose is per-line disclosure cannot discard the basket because one item was withdrawn. The classification is deliberately distinct from the out-of-stock one, because the commit path resolves the variant through a fetch that raises when the row does not match its `enabled` and `deletedAt` predicate [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L684] rather than accumulating a per-item error the way it does for a stock shortfall [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L710], so a buyer who is not warned here loses the whole batch at commit rather than one line

* Scenario: Category — a price changed since the last purchase. Required of every story in this set, and deliberately kept short here.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer carrying one line whose variant has since been repriced, and whose stock row carries `stockOnHand` 10 and `stockAllocated` 0
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed with a source naming that order's id
   * Then the availability fields this story owns are **unaffected by the reprice**: the entry still reports `stockLevel` of exactly `IN_STOCK` and `availability` of `ADDABLE_IN_FULL`, because availability is computed from the stock columns and the thresholds and not from any price. The price fields on the same entry are reported by STORY-001-03-01 and **the price vocabulary is deferred to that story rather than restated here**, so the two cannot diverge on the same payload; the two halves are independent by construction [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§4.3 Intra-Feature Story Order]

* Scenario: Category — a point-in-time read gone stale between check and commit. The value is re-read at commit and nothing was held.
   * Given a preview taken over a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer, reporting `ADDABLE_IN_FULL` with `stockLevel` of exactly `IN_STOCK` for a variant whose stock row carried `stockOnHand` 3 and `stockAllocated` 0; and, after that response is returned, another customer's order allocating all three units
   * When the buyer proceeds to commit the reorder through the mutation FEATURE-001-02 owns
   * Then the commit **re-evaluates the line against the saleable level at write time** and is authoritative over the preview [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L703], reporting the shortfall as an `InsufficientStockError` carrying the available quantity for that item while the lines that can still be added are added [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L710] and [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L712]. **That divergence is specified behaviour and not a defect**, because the preview held nothing: no allocation, no hold and no claim of any kind [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.10 A Point-In-Time Read Is Not A Reservation]. No freshness window is stated in any unit, and the published schema documentation carries the caveat instead

* Scenario: Category — a channel, currency or language mismatch between the original order and the reorder context. The same variant can report a different token under a different channel token.
   * Given a placed source order created under one channel token and owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer, and that same buyer requesting a preview under a **different** channel token carried in the `vendure-token` header [packages/core/src/config/vendure-config.ts:L176-L184]; and separately a variant stocked at two `StockLocation` rows assigned to different channels, whose per-location stock rows are read under a channel predicate [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L63]
   * When `reorderPreview(input: ReorderPreviewInput!)` is executed with a source naming that order's id
   * Then the cross-channel source **does not resolve** and the response carries zero entries, identical to AC-5's non-disclosing response — the boundary is not crossed and no operand from the other channel is read. Where a source *does* resolve, the availability token is the one the configured location and display strategies return **for the requesting channel** [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L79], so the same variant can report a different exact token under a different channel token, and the language follows the requesting channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74] by the documented fallback [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L136-L145]. **The threshold, by contrast, does not vary by channel in this checkout** [packages/core/src/service/services/global-settings.service.ts:L63-L75], so a scenario asserting a channel-varying token varies the stock locations and never a Channel column, per section 4.3.2
