### 1.2 The Argument For This Story, Which Is An Argument About Ordering

The platform already decides what happens to a short line. It decides **unilaterally**, and it decides **after** it has written. That inversion, not a missing capability, is what this story exists to fix, and the evidence is in the control flow of the one method the commit path calls.

- **A validation failure behaves as an implicit `skip` already.** `OrderService.addItemsToOrder` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L654] pushes the failure onto an accumulator and continues to the next item rather than abandoning the batch [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L680-L683], which its own documentation advertises by advising a caller to check the returned array [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L649-L650]. So the *effect* of `skip` is shipped. What is absent is a buyer having asked for it.
- **A stock shortfall behaves as an implicit `reduce quantity` already, and in the wrong order.** The requested quantity is clamped before the write [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L703], the line is then written **at the reduced quantity** [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L736-L741], and only afterwards is an `InsufficientStockError` appended [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L743-L749]. A shipped specification asserts exactly that sequence: five units requested against three obtainable returns `quantityAvailable` of three and still writes a line at three, with the specification's own inline comment recording it [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L645-L661]. **The write happens first and the notification second.**
- **`abort` does not exist at all.** Once the per-item loop ends, price adjustments are applied to whatever was written and the method returns [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L751]. Nothing removes the lines that succeeded, so abandoning a reorder is expressible only as a decision not to call the commit — which is the sharpest available argument that the decision has to precede the write [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§2.4 What The Commit Path Already Does Today].
- **And a withdrawn variant is not expressible per line at any point.** The bulk add resolves each variant through a fetch requiring `enabled: true` and a null `deletedAt` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L684-L689], and throws again when the parent product is disabled [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L693]. There is no `try` and no `catch` in the method, so one withdrawn line ends the whole batch rather than producing one failed entry among many.

**Read those four together and the inversion is exact.** The platform will silently shorten a line a buyer might have preferred to drop, and will destroy a nine-line reorder over a tenth line a buyer would have been content to skip. **That is the inverse of what a buyer would choose in each case**, and it is why a caller-supplied per-line instruction — carried in the request, evaluated before the write — is the only shape that fixes it.

## 3. PRECEDENT In This Repository

**PRECEDENT:** partial

The searched set is named so the claim is checkable rather than asserted: the whole of `packages/dev-server/example-plugins/` — its six directories being `digital-products`, `minimum-order-quantity`, `multivendor-plugin`, `product-bundles`, `ui-extensions-library` and `wishlist-plugin` — the whole of `packages/dev-server/test-plugins/`, including `reviews`, `scheduler-race-test`, `job-queue-test`, `issue-1873`, `custom-history-entry` and `customer-avatar`, and `packages/dashboard/test-plans/`, **which holds exactly one file** and is named as a directory of one rather than implied to be a corpus. That is the same set the epic searched for its nomination [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.6 Nomination] and the same set the parent feature searched for its own disclosure [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§2.13 Precedent In This Repository].

**Why the value is `partial` and not `none`.** Three legs, in descending order of how much they cost this story's novelty claim. All three are stated plainly, because an overstated novelty claim is the failure mode this disclosure exists to prevent.

- **The strongest leg, and it is unflattering: the *effect* of `skip` already exists unbidden.** `OrderService.addItemsToOrder` accumulates a per-item failure and continues to the next item [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L680-L683]. A buyer whose reorder contains one over-quantity line already gets the remaining lines and an error for that one. **Saying this story introduces skipping to this platform would be false.** What it introduces is the buyer having chosen it, for a line the platform would otherwise have shortened rather than dropped.
- **The second leg: an all-or-nothing shape exists in the searched set, achieved by a rollback.** The `product-bundles` example plugin is the **only** caller of the bulk add anywhere in the searched set: it builds an item array and calls it once [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/product-bundles/services/product-bundle.service.ts:L99], and then, whenever the returned collection holds anything at all, it rolls its transaction back and returns the first entry [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/product-bundles/services/product-bundle.service.ts:L114-L117], with the same shape repeated for its adjustment path [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/product-bundles/services/product-bundle.service.ts:L153-L155]. **That is precedent for the *shape* of `abort` and precedent against everything else about it.** It is unconditional rather than elected, it is post-hoc rather than pre-commit, and it discards every entry after the first so a buyer learns nothing per line. This story does not adopt it: the parent feature fixes `abort` as declining to call the commit at all [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§2.4 What The Commit Path Already Does Today], and declining never writes, whereas a rollback writes and then unwinds.
- **The third leg: the nearest shipped extension point that alters an add rejects rather than offers.** `MinMaxOrderInterceptor implements OrderInterceptor` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/minimum-order-quantity/order-quantity-limits.plugin.ts:L25] hooks `willAddItemToOrder` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/minimum-order-quantity/order-quantity-limits.plugin.ts:L34] and `willAdjustOrderLine` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/minimum-order-quantity/order-quantity-limits.plugin.ts:L50], and is registered by pushing an instance onto the platform's interceptor array [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/minimum-order-quantity/order-quantity-limits.plugin.ts:L107]. Every branch of it returns a refusal message. **It is a partial precedent for the extension shape and not a precedent for the capability**, because it decides on the deployment's behalf and never asks the buyer anything.

**Why the value is not `near-identical` either — the residue, stated as narrowly as it holds.** Two things, and no more.

- **A caller-supplied, per-line resolution instruction evaluated before the write.** Nothing in the searched set asks a buyer what to do about a line that cannot be obtained. The platform decides, and it decides after writing.
- **An elected, all-or-nothing `abort` that core does not offer at all.** There is no rollback in the bulk add and nothing removes the lines that succeeded [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L751], so this outcome is unreachable through the platform's own surface and is reachable in the searched set only by the unconditional rollback described above.

**The honest summary, at the strength it actually holds: this story's mechanisms are borrowed and its authority over the decision is new.** That is weaker than "no precedent" and stronger than "already shipped", and it is the accurate reading.

## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line when the file is read on any renderer and when it is pasted into an external tracker. **Every story file in this set uses that same convention**, so a reader comparing two stories compares like with like.

```text
As a Returning Buyer,
I want to choose, for each line of my reorder that cannot be obtained at the quantity I asked for, whether to skip it, reduce it to the quantity that is obtainable, take a substitute, or abandon the whole reorder,
So that the cart I am handed contains exactly the lines and quantities I chose, and a single unobtainable item neither disappears without my say nor destroys the rest of the order.
```

### 4.1 INVEST Criteria

### 4.3 The Resolution Instruction, And The Three Boundaries Around It

Everything named in this sub-section that carries no citation is a **target named in ticket text**. `applyReorderToActiveOrder`, `ApplyReorderInput`, `ReorderLineResolutionInput`, `ReorderLineResolutionAction`, `ReorderResult`, `ReorderLineOutcome`, `ReorderLineOutcomeCode`, `reorderPreview` and the plugin package do not exist in this checkout, so none of them carries a citation and a downstream reader must not treat any of them as verified. **Their shapes are FEATURE-001-02's and FEATURE-001-03's and are inherited rather than re-decided here** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces].

**What this story implements, field by field, on an input that is already published.** `ApplyReorderInput.lineResolutions` carries zero or more `ReorderLineResolutionInput` entries, each addressing one projected line by the `requestIndex` the plugin's own correlation contract assigned it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.13 The Outcome Correlation Contract], and each naming one member of `ReorderLineResolutionAction`.

- **`SKIP` carries neither a quantity nor a variant.** Its effect is that the line is absent from the item array the plugin builds, and its reported outcome is `SKIPPED_BY_RESOLUTION`.
- **`REDUCE` carries a quantity, and its ceiling is the `addableQuantity` the preview reported for the same `requestIndex`** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§2.8 The Existing Mechanisms This Feature Consumes Rather Than Rebuilds]. A quantity above that ceiling, or one that is not a positive integer, is a request-level input failure refused before any write rather than a value silently clamped: the buyer asked for a specific number and is entitled to be told it is no longer obtainable rather than to receive a different one.
- **`SUBSTITUTE` carries `substituteProductVariantId`, and this story names the outcome without supplying its mechanism.** The candidates are the strategy's, the strategy is STORY-001-04-02's, and the validation that the named variant is one the strategy actually returned for that line belongs there with it. **No criterion in section 5 exercises `SUBSTITUTE`**, and that is a stated boundary rather than an omission.
- **`ABORT_IF_UNAVAILABLE` carries neither a quantity nor a variant.** Its effect is that the commit is not called at all, so nothing is written for any line, including lines that were obtainable. The member's longer name is deliberate: the abandonment is conditional on a line actually being unavailable, so submitting it for a reorder in which every line is obtainable is not a request to abandon a healthy reorder [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§2.3 The Four Resolution Outcomes].

**The obtainable figure is read, never invented, and the ordering is the whole point.** `InsufficientStockError.quantityAvailable` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L53], on the type whose own description covers adding more items than are available [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L49-L50], is the platform's own obtainable figure — but it arrives on an error the platform returns **after** it has already written the shortened line [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L743-L749]. A resolution that needed it would have to call the commit to learn the number and call it again to use it, with the first call having already put an unauthorised line in the cart. **So the pre-commit source is the preview's `addableQuantity` and the commit-time authority is `quantityAvailable`**, and section 5 asserts both: the two agree under a quiescent precondition, and the commit governs where they do not. **No number in this file is one a ticket chose.**

**Three boundaries, stated as rules so this story does not annex a neighbour's scope.**

- **It does not detect unavailability.** The availability classification and the obtainable figure are STORY-001-03-02's, consumed here as inputs [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§4.1 Upstream].
- **It does not write to the order.** A resolved line set is handed once to `OrderService.addItemsToOrder` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L654], which FEATURE-001-02 already calls exactly once per reorder; this story adds no second call site and no per-variant loop.
- **It reimplements no stock arithmetic.** Availability is resolved through the platform's own composed call, which reaches `StockLevelService.getAvailableStock` [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L72] and delegates the multi-location question to the configured strategy [packages/core/src/service/services/stock-level.service.ts:L79]. **No plugin query reads a `StockLevel` row and no plugin code sums `stockOnHand`** — a variant with a negative threshold is obtainable well beyond its on-hand figure, as a shipped specification asserts by writing eight units against a `stockOnHand` of three [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L719-L738], so arithmetic in plugin code would classify an obtainable line as unavailable.

**A point-in-time read is not a reservation, and no field or document may suggest otherwise.** Adding to a cart reserves nothing in this platform: a shipped specification has two customers each add one unit of a variant whose `stockOnHand` is one and **both adds succeed** [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L1301-L1316], with the allocation recorded only at payment [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L1324-L1327]. Resolving a line before adding it holds even less. **No freshness window is stated in any unit**, because this repository declares none and a stated window would imply a guarantee the read does not make [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§2.12 A Point-In-Time Read Is Not A Reservation].

## 7. Edge Cases

Five scenarios: the three categories required of every story in this set, plus the two conditional categories that apply because this story reads a mutable value and because it writes through a path another session can reach at the same moment. **Each is labelled with its category inline**, so the category matrix never appears as a table in a story file. **The channel, currency and language mismatch category is carried as an acceptance criterion rather than as a sixth scenario** — AC-1's two closing assertions cover the foreign-channel source and the currency mismatch — because the maximum here is five and the allocation is deliberate rather than an omission.

* Scenario: Category — zero, null or empty collection. Every line of the source is resolved away, so the item array is empty.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer on the channel token `e2e-default-channel` [packages/testing/src/config/test-config.ts:L15] carrying exactly three lines, every one of whose variants has a stock row leaving its saleable level at 0 under the settled inventory fixture; and a resolution entry of `SKIP` submitted for each of the three indices the request assigned
   * When `applyReorderToActiveOrder(input: ApplyReorderInput!)` is executed naming that order as its source
   * Then the request **settles and reports** rather than failing: the plugin does not call the bulk add with an empty item array, the buyer's cart is exactly what it was, and the outcome report carries exactly three entries in ascending index order, each with an added quantity of exactly 0, an outcome of exactly `SKIPPED_BY_RESOLUTION` and a null error code. **`NoReorderableLinesError` is deliberately not returned**, because that error's own declared meaning is a source that projected to zero addable lines while this source projected three and the buyer emptied the projection itself — a condition that does not exist until this story lands, which is why this story fixes it rather than inheriting an answer [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces]. A separate call whose source genuinely resolves to no line at all still returns that error with a resolved-source-line count of 0, so the two conditions stay distinguishable

* Scenario: Category — an item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase. A class distinct from out of stock, and the class that would otherwise destroy the request.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer carrying exactly three lines, the first line's variant having had its `enabled` column set to false since the order was placed [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L53], the second line's variant having had its `deletedAt` column set since then [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L48], and the third line's variant `enabled` with `deletedAt` null and a stock row carrying `stockOnHand` 10 and `stockAllocated` 0
   * When `applyReorderToActiveOrder(input: ApplyReorderInput!)` is executed with `SKIP` resolved for the first two indices
   * Then the request **partially fulfils with a report**: the third line is written to the cart at its recorded quantity while the first two are absent from it, each reported with an added quantity of exactly 0 and an outcome of exactly `SKIPPED_BY_RESOLUTION`. **This is a distinct class from out of stock and is resolved out of the item array before delegation**, because the bulk add resolves each variant through a fetch requiring `enabled: true` and a null `deletedAt` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L684-L689] and throws again for a disabled parent product [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L693], with no `try` and no `catch` in the method — so a withdrawn line left in the array abandons the whole batch rather than yielding one failed entry. **Only `skip`, `substitute` and `abort` are offered for such a line and never `reduce quantity`**, because a withdrawn variant has no obtainable quantity to reduce to; the decision tree draws that branch explicitly [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-04-unavailable-line-resolution.md:§2.9 Figure F4-FLOW]

* Scenario: Category — a price changed since the last purchase. A reduced line is priced from the current catalogue, and the reduction is not a price lock.
   * Given a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer carrying exactly one line at a recorded quantity of 5 whose variant has since been repriced, and whose stock row leaves its saleable level at exactly 3 under the settled inventory fixture
   * When `applyReorderToActiveOrder(input: ApplyReorderInput!)` is executed with a `REDUCE` resolution at the previewed obtainable quantity of 3
   * Then the line is written at `quantity` exactly 3 and is **priced from the current catalogue** by the platform's own adjustment pass, not from the historical figure the source line recorded: this story copies no monetary value, performs no arithmetic on money and applies no rate, so there is no place in it where a stale price could survive. Every monetary value on the returned order is an integer in the smallest unit of the currency the payload's target code names [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88], with the gross-or-net basis following `Channel.pricesIncludeTax` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L113], and **a decimal monetary value anywhere is a defect**. Reporting the price delta to the buyer beforehand is STORY-001-03-01's and is not restated here

* Scenario: Category — a point-in-time read gone stale between check and commit. The availability the buyer resolved against is not a reservation.
   * Given a preview taken over a placed source order owned by the authenticated Returning Buyer, reporting an obtainable quantity of exactly 3 for a variant whose stock row carried `stockOnHand` 3 [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L40] and `stockAllocated` 0 [packages/core/src/entity/stock-level/stock-level.entity.ts:L43]; and, after that response is returned, another customer's order consuming all three units
   * When the buyer submits a `REDUCE` resolution at 3 through `applyReorderToActiveOrder(input: ApplyReorderInput!)`
   * Then the commit **re-evaluates the line at write time and is authoritative over the read** [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L703], reporting an added quantity of exactly 0 with the platform's own `InsufficientStockError` attributed to that line [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L50], which is what the platform accumulates where the clamp reaches zero [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L710-L715]. **The divergence is specified behaviour and not a defect**, because the read held nothing: adding to a cart reserves nothing in this platform, as a shipped specification demonstrates by letting two customers each add the single available unit successfully [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L1301-L1316] and recording the allocation only at payment [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L1324-L1327]. **No freshness window is stated in any unit**, and the published schema documentation carries the caveat rather than only this ticket

* Scenario: Category — a concurrent modification or race. Two commits reaching for the same units, and one buyer committing from two sessions.
   * Given the settled inventory fixture and a variant whose stock row carries `stockOnHand` 1 and `stockAllocated` 0, set as the shipped concurrency specification sets its own precondition [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L1287-L1298]; two placed source orders, one owned by each of two authenticated Returning Buyers, each carrying a line for that variant at a recorded quantity of 1 and each having previewed an obtainable quantity of exactly 1; and separately the **same** buyer holding one active order and issuing two reorder commits from two sessions at the same moment, each carrying a `REDUCE` resolution at 1 for the same variant
   * When both commits are issued through `applyReorderToActiveOrder(input: ApplyReorderInput!)` without either awaiting the other
   * Then the expected system response is to **partially fulfil with a report**, and it is stated as one behaviour rather than a family. For the two-buyer case both commits settle and each reports its own per-line outcome, which is the platform's own behaviour and not this story's choice: two carts may each hold the single unit because nothing is allocated until payment, and the shipped specification asserts exactly that pair of successful adds followed by the second customer being unable to complete [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L1301-L1316] and [packages/core/e2e/stock-control.e2e-spec.ts:L1329-L1330]. For the same-buyer case the two commits settle against **one** cart and the second is clamped by what the first already placed there, because the platform sums the quantity already present on other lines of the same variant before clamping [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L695-L702] and [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L703] — so the cart holds exactly 1 unit of the variant rather than 2, and the second commit reports an added quantity of exactly 0 with `InsufficientStockError` attributed to that line [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L50]. **Neither case blocks and neither aborts**, and no resolution is retried on the buyer's behalf, because a retry would be a second write the buyer did not request
