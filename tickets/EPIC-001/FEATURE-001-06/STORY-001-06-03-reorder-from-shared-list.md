## 1. Story Title

**Reorder from a shared list, so that a Returning Buyer turns a colleague's curated list into lines in their own cart without owning that list and without reaching anything else.**

Short form, transcribed from the epic's story table without rewording so that the two files agree: **Reorder from a shared list** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table]. The heading above is the same sentence in action-object-outcome form, measured at 176 characters and so inside the 255-character ceiling this tier sets.

What this story delivers is the one place in this epic where a customer legitimately acts on another customer's row: a seat of a buying account invoking FEATURE-001-02's cart write against a list somebody else owns, with the authorisation predicate deciding whether the call proceeds and with the resulting lines landing in the caller's own cart rather than the owner's.

**Three things it is often assumed to add and does not.**

- **It publishes no operation.** `applyReorderToActiveOrder` and its whole input, payload and union are fixed field for field by FEATURE-001-02 [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces — One New Mutation, Zero Existing Signatures Changed], and the list-source form of it is delivered by `STORY-001-02-03`. This story invokes that mutation as a customer who is not the list's owner and adds nothing to its surface.
- **It writes no share row and no list line.** Granting is `shareReorderList`, owned by `STORY-001-06-01`; withdrawing is `revokeReorderListShare`, owned by `STORY-001-06-02`; authoring a list is FEATURE-001-01's eight operations [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces — Eight Shop API Operations, Zero Existing Signatures Changed]. This story reads all three and writes none of them.
- **It defines no table, no permission definition, no authentication strategy and no session mechanism.** The plugin package and its registration in the dev configuration are a prerequisite of the whole epic that STORY-001-01-01 owns and prices [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.9 Project Bootstrap — Owned By STORY-001-01-01, And Priced There]. They are named in section 8 as a precondition and are absent from this story's estimate.

**What makes it a story of its own rather than a criterion on `STORY-001-02-03` is the identity question.** That story materialises a list its caller owns, so the cart it writes into and the list it reads from belong to one person and no authorisation branch is reachable. Here they belong to two people, which is what makes the ownership-or-valid-share predicate observable and what makes the cart-ownership assertion in section 5 something a test can fail.

## 3. Precedent In This Repository

The structural half of this story — plugin-owned rows read against the authenticated customer — is that pattern.
- **The bulk add this story reaches is platform code and is consumed unchanged.** `OrderService.addItemsToOrder` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L654] resolves an existing line per variant, validates each item and accumulates per-item error results instead of aborting, returning the order together with an error array. No line of it is rewritten here, and this story does not call it directly at all — it reaches the cart through FEATURE-001-02's own service [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.4 Named Services].
- **The gate is the shipped gate for a buyer-owned resource.** Every customer-facing operation in the wishlist example is declared `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/wishlist.resolver.ts:L11-L12], which is the same gate this story's resolver path carries.
- **The end-to-end harness exists.** `packages/core/e2e/custom-permissions.e2e-spec.ts` already exercises permission behaviour end to end over three blocks — superadmin holding custom permissions automatically [packages/core/e2e/custom-permissions.e2e-spec.ts:L74], unauthorised access being prevented [packages/core/e2e/custom-permissions.e2e-spec.ts:L106] and adding permissions enabling access [packages/core/e2e/custom-permissions.e2e-spec.ts:L147] — and section 10 names it as the coverage shape this story's specification mirrors.

**Half two — nothing in this repository lets one customer act on another customer's saved data. That is the new part, and it is the whole of this story.**

The search above returns no plugin, no core entity and no Shop API operation through which one customer reads or writes a row another customer owns. The closest shipped analogue is single-owner by construction: the wishlist plugin's entire published surface is three operations — `activeCustomerWishlist` declared through `extend type Query` and `addToWishlist` with `removeFromWishlist` declared through `extend type Mutation` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L12-L19] — with **no sharing surface and no cart-materialisation path at all**. Its data hangs off exactly one customer through an internal relation field [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/wishlist.plugin.ts:L17-L26], a shape that cannot express a second reader, so there is no second-party read to copy and no cart write to copy either. The parent feature reaches the same verdict from its own side and lists the five things that are concretely new across the feature [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.13 Precedent In This Repository — Partial].

**So the verdict is `partial` rather than `none` or `near-identical`.** The entity idiom, the gate, the schema-extension mechanism, the bulk add and the harness are all re-application of shipped work. Concretely new in this story are a cart write whose authorisation depends on a row the caller does not own, the per-request revalidation that makes a lapsed seat lose the ability to reorder without any grant being withdrawn, and the assertion about which of two customers' carts the write lands in.

## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line on any renderer and when the block is pasted into an external tracker. Every story file in this set uses that same convention.

```text
As a Returning Buyer,
I want to reorder from a reorder list that a colleague of my buying account shared with me, without owning that list, and to have the resulting lines land in my own cart,
So that placing the account's repeat order is one call against a list somebody has already curated rather than a rebuild of the same order line by line, and so that acting on a colleague's list gives me reach into their list and into nothing else of theirs.
```

### 4.1 INVEST Criteria

- **Independent:** **true independence is not achievable here, and the prerequisite is named rather than glossed.** The minimum prerequisite set is two stories and no more: `STORY-001-06-01` for the `ReorderListShare` row this story reads, and `STORY-001-02-03` for the list-source form of the mutation it invokes. Both are classified in section 8, the first as a data dependency and a shared code path and the second as a shared code path, and the intra-feature order places 06-01 ahead of this story and never behind it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§4.3 Intra-Feature Story Order]. **The split story still delivers value on its own:** once those two are merged, this story is buildable, demonstrable and acceptable without any part of `STORY-001-06-02`, FEATURE-001-03, FEATURE-001-04, FEATURE-001-05, FEATURE-001-07 or FEATURE-001-08 existing, and it needs nothing from its own feature beyond a single grant row.
- **Negotiable:** open to a maintainer are whether a seat may reorder from a shared list into a cart that already holds lines from a different source, whether any per-seat reorder frequency bound exists, and whose active order a shared reorder materialises into — all three recorded in section 8.1 as decisions rather than answered there [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8. Decisions Required Before Build]. **Not negotiable** is that the authorisation decision is re-evaluated per request rather than cached on the session, that every refusal a caller without visibility can provoke returns one identical result, that no existing operation signature changes, and that no new authentication strategy or session mechanism is introduced.
- **Valuable:** this is the story that makes a shared list worth sharing. Without it `STORY-001-06-01` grants a capability that has nothing to exercise, and the buying-account use case the parent feature exists for — one colleague curating, another buying — has no buying half at all.
- **Estimable:** the shape is one authorisation predicate extended over one existing mutation with no new table, no new operation and no new error result, which the epic's rubric prices at five points as consumption of a core service with per-item outcome accumulation [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric, So The Numbers Are Reproducible]. Both the mutation's contract and the predicate's four conjuncts are fixed by parent documents, so there is a known surface to estimate against rather than a design to invent.
- **Sized Appropriately:** five points on the epic's Fibonacci scale, sitting exactly on the five-point line-of-code anchor with no upward adjustment, and the scale deliberately omits thirteen so that an oversized story is split instead of estimated [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric, So The Numbers Are Reproducible].
- **Testable:** every criterion in section 5 names one GraphQL operation and asserts a state a test can read — an exact cart line count with its ordering, an exact per-line outcome code with its requested and added quantities, an integer monetary value in the smallest unit of a named currency code, an exact error result type name observed inside the response data rather than as a top-level entry, an exact stored column value on the grant row, or the authenticated identity a request was refused under.

### 4.2 Demonstration Requirement

One path, runnable end to end, ending on **one named operation** with its inputs and its expected response, plus the read that proves whose cart changed. Because this story turns on identity, the two customers are named and it is stated at every step which of them is acting. **Customer A owns list L and performs no part of the demonstration's central call. Customer B is the seat holding a valid share on L and is the customer that performs the reorder.**

**Before step 1 — the shared demonstration prerequisite, which this story references rather than restates.** The toolchain check, `bun install --frozen-lockfile`, `bun run build` and `docker compose up -d mariadb` are the epic's verified demonstration prerequisite, stated once for all twenty-five stories [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.9.1 The Verified Demonstration Prerequisite]. **Not one step below runs on a clean checkout without it**, because the dev-server script this path starts the server with executes the built command-line interface of a sibling package [packages/dev-server/package.json:L13] and its seed script loads a `ts-node` require hook from the installed workspace [packages/dev-server/package.json:L8], neither of which exists before the install and the build. Run that sequence, confirm its two closing checks — `docker compose version` prints a version, and `packages/cli/dist/cli.js` exists — and then continue here.

> **`bun run populate` destroys and recreates the contents of whichever database the dev-server configuration selects, with no prompt and no dry run** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§11.11.2 Step 1 — A Disposable Database, And What The Seed Does To It]. Point it at a disposable development database and nothing else.

**Steps 1 and 2 — seed the disposable database, then start server and worker, as one chained subshell.**

```bash
( cd packages/dev-server && bun run populate && bun run dev )
```

**The chain matters to this story in particular.** This path needs two distinct customers in one account, and a server started against a database the seed failed to populate has no second customer at all — so the failure would surface as an unresolvable email address four steps after its cause. The `&&` chain stops the run at the seed [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§11.11.3 Step 2 — Seed, Then Start, Chained So A Failed Seed Stops The Run], and the subshell carries its own working directory so a later block is unaffected by this one. `populate` and `dev` are the two scripts that package declares [packages/dev-server/package.json:L8] and [packages/dev-server/package.json:L13], with the working directories the contribution guide documents [CONTRIBUTING.md:L147-L150] and [CONTRIBUTING.md:L178-L181]. **Step 3 — from terminal 2, put customer A and customer B in one buying account.** This is the one step that touches an Admin API surface, and it does so because **no Shop API operation creates a customer group** — seat membership is administered on the Admin API and this feature adds no surface for it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.11 What This Feature Does Not Build — The Security Boundary Stated Honestly]. **Step 4 — as customer A, build the list and grant B the capability to reorder from it.** Authenticate as A over the Shop API [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L116] with the password `test`. Create one list with `createReorderList` and record its identifier as `L`; add exactly two lines to it with `addItemToReorderList`, the first naming a variant at quantity 2 and the second naming a different variant at quantity 1, both variants enabled and in stock. Then grant B the reorder capability with `shareReorderList`, passing `reorderListId` of `L`, `customerGroupId` of `G`, `granteeEmailAddress` of B's address and `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER` — the member of FEATURE-001-01's closed capability pair that admits materialising a list into the grantee's cart, the other member admitting a read alone [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces — Two New Mutations, Zero Existing Signatures Changed]. **Step 5 — as customer B, open a cart of B's own.** Authenticate as B over the Shop API [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L116] and add one unrelated variant at quantity 1 with the existing `addItemToOrder` mutation [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L72], so that B holds an active order carrying exactly one line before the central call. Send no `vendure-token` header: the dev configuration sets no default channel token and the seed creates only the default channel, so channel resolution returns that channel.

**Step 6 — the demonstration, as customer B. One mutation.** Substitute the list identifier from step 4. The shape below is transcribed from the one document that declares it and is written as a target rather than as a citation, because it does not exist in this checkout [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces — One New Mutation, Zero Existing Signatures Changed].

```graphql
mutation {
    applyReorderToActiveOrder(input: { source: { reorderListId: "<L>" } }) {
        ... on ReorderResult {
            requestOutcome
            order { id totalQuantity lines { id quantity } }
            lineOutcomes {
                requestIndex
                productVariantId
                requestedQuantity
                addedQuantity
                orderLineId
                outcome
                errorCode
            }
            unattributedErrorCodes
            sourceCurrencyCode
            targetCurrencyCode
        }
        ... on ReorderPermissionDeniedError { errorCode message }
        ... on ReorderListNotFoundError { errorCode message }
        ... on NoReorderableLinesError { errorCode message resolvedSourceLineCount }
        ... on NoActiveOrderError { errorCode message }
    }
}
```

**The expected response, stated as an exact observable.** A `ReorderResult` whose `requestOutcome` is `SETTLED`; whose `order` holds exactly three lines with `quantity` values [packages/core/src/entity/order-line/order-line.entity.ts:L97] of 1, 2 and 1 and a `totalQuantity` of 4 [packages/core/src/entity/order/order.entity.ts:L286]; whose `lineOutcomes` holds exactly two entries in `requestIndex` order 0 then 1, each carrying an `outcome` of `ADDED`, an `addedQuantity` equal to its `requestedQuantity`, a set `orderLineId` and a null `errorCode`; whose `unattributedErrorCodes` holds zero entries; and whose `sourceCurrencyCode` and `targetCurrencyCode` are both the channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88], every monetary value beneath `order` being an integer in the smallest unit of that currency.

**Step 7 — the read that proves whose cart changed, and it is read twice by two different customers.** As customer B, on B's own session, issue `activeOrder` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L11] and observe an `id` equal to the `order.id` returned at step 6 and exactly three lines. Then, as customer A, on A's own session, issue the identical `activeOrder` query and observe that A's cart is **unchanged** — it carries neither of the two variants that were added, because the write resolved its target from the calling session rather than from the list or its owner. That contrast is the point of the story, and section 5 turns it into a criterion rather than leaving it as an observation.

**No dashboard extension is opened at any point**, and the administrative credential appears at step 3 for one purpose only — creating the seat set that no Shop API surface can create.

> **Every credential in this demonstration is a disposable development fixture and none is a deployment credential.** The administrative pair and the seeded customer password exist only inside the throwaway database `bun run populate` clears and reseeds. Two consequences follow and both are stated rather than assumed. **A deployment that neglects to set `NODE_ENV=production` starts with the default password and a log line**, so the obligation is to set `NODE_ENV=production` *and* to wire `authOptions.superadminCredentials` from environment variables, which is what the guard's own remediation hint directs [packages/core/src/service/helpers/utils/check-superadmin-credentials.ts:L6-L8]. **And the seeded customer password sits outside the guard entirely** — nothing in the platform refuses to start over it — so its containment rests on the throwaway database and on nothing else.

## 5. Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria. Each names exactly one GraphQL operation, so a failing criterion points at a single published contract, and each states which customer is authenticated, whether that customer owns the list or holds a grant on it, the channel token the request carries and the `languageCode` it resolves under. **No criterion is expressed in terms of a resolver class, a file path or an internal method name**, and where a criterion rests on a fact about the existing platform it carries that fact's inline citation as evidence rather than making the cited path part of the assertion. The narrative in section 4 is not restated here: the story carries the value and the criteria carry the testable outcomes.

**The five required coverage classes map onto the eight as follows.** Valid input is AC-1 and AC-5. Invalid or incomplete input is AC-2. An error naming the exact type returned is AC-3, AC-4, AC-6 and AC-7. An edge case is AC-6. The assertion that an existing operation behaves unchanged is AC-8, which this story owes because it touches a shared write path.

**AC-3 and AC-4 together are the mandatory negative criterion, split into its two terminal branches so that a failure names one branch rather than two.** AC-3 is the caller with no relationship to the list at all; AC-4 is the caller holding a live grant whose capability does not cover a cart write. **AC-5 is the criterion this story exists for** — the cart-ownership assertion, in both of its halves.

**Six properties are stated once here rather than repeated in every criterion.**

**Naming convention used throughout.** Customer **A** owns list **L**. Customer **B** is a seat of the same buying account holding an `ACTIVE` `ReorderListShare` row on `L` with `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER`. Customer **C** is a third customer who neither owns `L` nor holds any grant on it. Customer **D** is a seat holding an `ACTIVE` grant on `L` whose `capability` is `READ` alone. Group **G** is the customer group every seat of the account belongs to. **T** is the channel token carried in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L58-L59] and stored as a unique column on the channel it identifies [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]; **T2** is a second channel's token. Unless a criterion says otherwise the request resolves its `languageCode` from that channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74], which is `en` in the fixture, and any monetary value resolves against its `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88] as an integer in the smallest unit of that currency.

**The gate is not the control, so every criterion below is a statement about resolver and service logic rather than about the annotation.** A customer session holds exactly `Permission.Authenticated` and never `Permission.Owner`, so `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L134] admits the request instead of refusing it. The platform states the consequence in its own source: ownership must be enforced by custom logic inside the resolver, and because ownership can be defined neither generally nor statically at build time, any resolver using that permission **must** include logic enforcing that only the owner of the resource has access, failing which the effect is the equivalent of using the public permission [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-permissions.ts:L32-L34], the whole caveat being written out in one block in that file [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-permissions.ts:L12-L33]. **A permission gate alone is not a control**, so the operation performs an explicit ownership-or-valid-share check of its own, drawn out as Figure F6-AUTH in the parent feature [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.9 Figure F6-AUTH — The Ownership-Plus-Share Authorisation Check].

**Refusals are normalized, and this story does not reopen that.** Every case in which the caller cannot see the list returns one identical `ReorderListNotFoundError` — an identifier naming nothing, another customer's list, another channel's list, a grant whose state is `REVOKED`, and a grantee who is no longer a member of the account. `ReorderPermissionDeniedError` survives at exactly three nodes of Figure F6-AUTH, and the only one this story reaches is the capability node: a grantee who can already see the list being told that a cart write exceeds what they hold [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.12 The Error Vocabulary This Feature Introduces].

**An error result is returned inside `data`, not thrown.** Every named error type below is asserted as a member of the operation's result union reached through an inline fragment on the payload. A thrown `ForbiddenError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L67] or `UserInputError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L27] surfaces instead as a top-level GraphQL error entry with no `applyReorderToActiveOrder` payload, and a criterion satisfied by a top-level entry has not been satisfied — except where the criterion names that shape explicitly, which AC-2 does.

**Four rulings are inherited and consumed rather than restated or contradicted.** Disabled and soft-deleted variants are excluded from the projection **before** the core call, because the bulk add loads each variant with an enabled flag set and a null deletion timestamp and throws for a disabled parent product rather than accumulating a per-item error [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L654] — established by `STORY-001-02-01`. `NoActiveOrderError` is the outcome when the session holds no active order and the configured strategy set cannot produce one, grounded in the published statement that `activeOrder` is null until an order is created [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L11] — established by `STORY-001-02-03` and ruled in the parent feature [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.8 The No-Active-Order Ruling — Stated, Not Left Implicit]. A variant already present in the active order accumulates onto the existing line rather than creating a second one — established by `STORY-001-02-03`. And the per-line outcome-report contract, being an addition at the requested quantity, an addition at a reduced quantity carrying the exact available quantity, a line not added with its exact error type named, and a line excluded before delegation, is owned by `STORY-001-02-04` and is **referenced here rather than redefined**.

**Every availability read behind these criteria is point-in-time and is not a reservation.** Saleable stock observed while the projection is built can move between that read and the write, so a criterion asserts what the response reports rather than promising that a quantity remains addable afterwards; the parent feature states the same property for the same path [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.10 A Point-In-Time Read Is Not A Reservation].

## 7. Edge Cases

Five scenarios, which is the maximum this tier permits, and the five categories this feature requires are covered one apiece in the order below. Each is a flat block rather than a row of a table, which is what keeps this section pasteable into an external tracker alongside the criteria above.

* Scenario: Zero, null or empty collection — the shared list holds no lines at all
   * Given customer A owns list L in the channel identified by token T, A and customer B are both current live members of group G in T's channel, exactly one `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B with `state` of `ACTIVE` and `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER`, L holds exactly zero `ReorderListLine` rows, and B holds an active order in T's channel carrying exactly one line
   * When customer B executes `applyReorderToActiveOrder` with `input.source.reorderListId` of L, omitting `lineSelections`
   * Then block the request and write nothing: the mutation returns `NoReorderableLinesError` as a member of the result union inside `data` with `resolvedSourceLineCount` reporting exactly 0, which is the declared outcome when a resolved source projects to zero **requested** lines; B's active order still carries exactly one line and its `totalQuantity` is unchanged; not one `OrderLine` row is written; and the refusal is a request-level result rather than a `ReorderResult` carrying an empty report, so a client distinguishes "nothing to add" from "everything added" by the shape of the response rather than by counting entries. The authorisation predicate still ran and still passed, which is why the answer is the empty-source error and not the not-found error — an empty list is a list the caller may see

* Scenario: An item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase — a line of the shared list names a variant that is now disabled or soft-deleted
   * Given customer B holds an `ACTIVE` grant with `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER` on list L, which customer A owns in T's channel; L holds exactly three lines, the first naming an enabled and in-stock variant V1 at quantity 1, the second naming a variant V2 whose enabled flag is now false, and the third naming a variant V3 whose deletion timestamp is now set; and B holds an active order in T's channel carrying exactly zero added lines while still being an active order
   * When customer B executes `applyReorderToActiveOrder` with `input.source.reorderListId` of L
   * Then partially fulfil with a report: V2 and V3 are excluded from the projection **before** the core call is made, because the bulk add loads each variant with an enabled flag set and a null deletion timestamp and throws for a disabled parent product rather than accumulating a per-item error [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L654] — so letting them reach it would end the batch and take V1 down with them; the response is a `ReorderResult` whose `requestOutcome` is `SETTLED`, whose `lineOutcomes` holds exactly three entries in ascending `requestIndex` order with entry 0 carrying an `outcome` of `ADDED` and entries 1 and 2 each carrying the unavailable-variant outcome code and a null `orderLineId`, and V1 is written to the cart while the other two are not. The exclusion rule and the per-line outcome vocabulary are both inherited: the first from `STORY-001-02-01` and the second from `STORY-001-02-04`, and this story redefines neither. Nothing about a variant's state changes what the grant permits, so no authorisation branch is reached by this scenario

* Scenario: A price changed since the last purchase — the catalogue price of a variant on the shared list has moved since the list was curated
   * Given customer B holds an `ACTIVE` grant with `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER` on list L, which customer A owns in T's channel; L holds exactly one line naming variant V1 at quantity 1; V1's price in T's channel has changed since A added that line; and B holds an active order in T's channel carrying exactly zero added lines
   * When customer B executes `applyReorderToActiveOrder` with `input.source.reorderListId` of L
   * Then proceed, with no warning from this story: the price applied to the new cart line is the **current** catalogue price, because neither the `ReorderList` line nor the `ReorderListShare` row stores a price and there is therefore no stored figure that could be applied instead or go stale. Every monetary value the response carries is an integer in the smallest unit of the currency `targetCurrencyCode` reports, which defaults to the channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88] — a current unit price of 129900 is reported as that integer alongside its currency code, and a decimal monetary value anywhere in this story is a defect rather than a formatting preference. Whether the figure includes tax follows `Channel.pricesIncludeTax` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L113], so two seats reading under two channel tokens may see two different figures. **Reporting the movement to the buyer is not this story's work**: the in-cart, post-add price-change mechanism already exists in the platform, and surfacing a delta before commit is FEATURE-001-03's territory [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-03-price-and-availability-delta-preview.md:§2.1 The Capability]. A criterion here that asserted a price warning would be asserting another feature's behaviour

* Scenario: Concurrent modification or race — the share is revoked while a reorder is in flight
   * Given customer B holds an open, unexpired session established before any withdrawal and holds one `ACTIVE` `ReorderListShare` row with `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER` on list L, which customer A owns in T's channel; L holds exactly two lines whose variants are enabled and in stock; B holds an active order in T's channel carrying exactly one line; and customer A executes `revokeReorderListShare` — the mutation `STORY-001-06-02` owns — while B's reorder request is in flight
   * When customer B executes `applyReorderToActiveOrder` with `input.source.reorderListId` of L, once with the revocation committed **before** the request's authorisation predicate is evaluated and once with it committed **after** that evaluation and after the write
   * Then the outcome is deterministic in both interleavings rather than timing-dependent, because the predicate is evaluated once per request against the row's state at that moment: in the first interleaving the whole request is blocked with `ReorderListNotFoundError` inside `data`, not one `OrderLine` row is written, and B's active order still carries exactly one line; in the second the write has already completed, so the request returns its `ReorderResult` and B's active order carries exactly three lines, with the revocation taking effect on **B's next request** rather than reaching back into a completed one. **The named observable signal is the exact cart line count on B's next `activeOrder` read together with the result member the in-flight call returned** — no re-authentication, no session invalidation and no new session mechanism is involved, and B's session stays valid throughout because what changed is what the session may reach rather than the session itself. This follows directly from the per-request re-evaluation `STORY-001-06-02` establishes, which is itself a consequence of ownership being resolvable neither generically nor at build time [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-permissions.ts:L12-L33]. **Whether the lines B already added survive the revocation is not decided here**: it is the decision `STORY-001-06-02` records, and no cart-mutating revocation behaviour ships under this story [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]

* Scenario: Channel, currency or language mismatch — a grant made under one channel token is exercised under another
   * Given customer A owns list L in the channel identified by token T, exactly one `ACTIVE` `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B in that channel, a second channel identified by token T2 exists, both A and B are assigned to both channels and are members of group G in both, and B holds one active order in T's channel and a separate active order in T2's channel
   * When customer B executes `applyReorderToActiveOrder` with `input.source.reorderListId` of L while sending T2 in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L58-L59], and then executes the identical call under T
   * Then block the first and proceed with the second: **a share granted under one channel token confers no access under another** [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62], so the call under T2 returns `ReorderListNotFoundError` inside `data` — the normalized answer every no-visibility case reaches, so that the pair of errors can never be differenced to learn whether a list exists in another channel — and B's T2 cart is unchanged with not one `OrderLine` row written; the call under T succeeds and its lines land in B's **T-channel** active order and not in the T2 one, because the target cart is resolved by inner-joining the order's channels on the request's channel identifier [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L429], so the two carts are separate rows and stay separate. Any list name or product name in the response is read under the request's `languageCode`, which defaults to that channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74] and is `en` in the fixture, while a capability is an enumerated member carrying no prose to translate; and every monetary value follows the acting channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88] as an integer in the smallest unit, with `sourceCurrencyCode` and `targetCurrencyCode` both reporting it because the list path takes the channel default for the source

**The remaining conditional category is stated rather than force-fitted, because it does apply here and its treatment is a rule rather than a scenario.** A point-in-time availability read gone stale between check and commit is a real condition on this path: the saleable stock observed while the projection is built can move before the write lands, so a line that looked addable can be reported as not added or added at a reduced quantity in the same response. **That is not a defect and it is not a reservation** — nothing on this path holds stock, and every criterion in section 5 asserts what the response reports rather than promising that a quantity remains addable afterwards. The rule and its consequences belong to the mutation's owning feature and are consumed here unchanged [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.10 A Point-In-Time Read Is Not A Reservation]; the outcome codes that express it are `STORY-001-02-04`'s.
