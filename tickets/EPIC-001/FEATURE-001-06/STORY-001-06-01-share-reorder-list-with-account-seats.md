**Share a reorder list with account seats, so that a Buying Account Administrator grants one colleague the capability to reorder from that list and nothing further.**

Short form, transcribed from the epic's story table without rewording so that the two files agree: **Share a reorder list with account seats** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table]. ## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line on any renderer and when the block is pasted into an external tracker. Every story file in this set uses that same convention.

```text
As a Buying Account Administrator,
I want to grant one named seat of my buying account the capability to view or reorder from a reorder list I own,
So that a colleague places the account's repeat order from that list without my re-entering it for them and without gaining access to any other list, cart or seat of the account.
```

## 5. Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria. Each names exactly one GraphQL operation, so a failing criterion points at a single published contract, and each states which customer is authenticated, whether that customer owns the list or holds a share, the channel token the request carries and the `languageCode` it resolves under. The five required coverage classes map onto them as follows: valid input is AC-1, invalid or incomplete input is AC-2, an error naming the exact type returned is AC-4 and AC-5, an edge case is AC-6, and the assertion that an existing operation is unchanged is AC-8.

**AC-3 and AC-4 together are the mandatory negative criterion this feature makes non-optional, split into its two terminal branches so that a failure names one branch rather than two.** AC-3 is the caller with no relationship to the list at all, refused with the normalized result; AC-4 is the caller holding a live grant attempting an operation reserved to the owner, refused with the one result this feature declares. AC-7 additionally carries the read-path regression the parent feature calls the one that matters most here — that a client which never passes the opt-in argument sees exactly what it saw before this story shipped.

**Three properties of the operation are stated once here rather than repeated in every criterion.**

- **Naming convention used throughout.** Customer **A** owns list **L** and is the only party that may share it. Customer **B** is the grantee seat. Customer **C** is a third customer who neither owns `L` nor holds a grant on it. Group **G** is the customer group all seats of the account belong to. **T** is the channel token carried in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L58-L59] and stored as a unique column on the channel it identifies [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]; **T2** is a second channel's token. Unless a criterion says otherwise the request resolves its `languageCode` from the channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74], which is `en` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/language-code.graphql:L72] in the fixture.
- **`shareReorderList` is a single synchronous write.** It enqueues no job, publishes no event and schedules no task, so there is no asynchronous trigger and no observable completion signal to name in any criterion below. The event publication in this domain belongs to `STORY-001-07-02`.
- **`ReorderPermissionDeniedError` is returned inside `data` as a member of the operation's result union, and is not a thrown error.** That distinction is asserted rather than assumed, because the two are observed in different places: a thrown `ForbiddenError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L67] or `UserInputError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L27] surfaces as a top-level GraphQL error entry with no `shareReorderList` payload — which is how the shipped precedent reports a variant it cannot resolve [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L38] — whereas every criterion below asserts a **returned** union member, reachable through an inline fragment on the payload. A criterion satisfied by a top-level error entry has not been satisfied.

AC-1: The owner grants one seat a reorder capability, and exactly one grant exists

## 7. Edge Cases

Five scenarios, which is the maximum this tier permits, and the five categories the parent feature requires are covered one apiece in the order below. Two of the five resolve to *proceed* rather than to a block or a warning, and that is stated honestly rather than force-fitted into a failure this operation cannot have: sharing a list evaluates neither availability nor price, so the two catalogue-movement categories are genuine non-events here and the value of recording them is precisely that a reader does not go looking for a check that would be wrong to build.

* Scenario: Zero, null or empty collection — the list being shared holds zero lines
   * Given customer A owns list L in the channel identified by token T, L holds exactly zero `ReorderListLine` rows, and customers A and B are current members of group G in T's channel
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L with `granteeEmailAddress` of B's address, `customerGroupId` of G and `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER`
   * Then proceed: the grant is written, exactly one `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B with `state` of `ACTIVE`, and customer B's opt-in read returns L with a line count of exactly zero. A grant is a capability over a list rather than over its contents, so an empty list is shareable and the emptiness surfaces at reorder time as `NoReorderableLinesError`, which `STORY-001-02-03` owns and this story does not return

* Scenario: An item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase — a shared list references a variant that is now disabled
   * Given customer A owns list L in T's channel, one of L's lines references a product variant whose enabled flag is now false, and A and B are current members of group G in T's channel
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L with B as the grantee and `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER`
   * Then proceed, with no warning and no audit record: **availability is not evaluated at share time at all.** This operation reads no variant, no stock level and no catalogue state, so a disabled variant cannot affect a grant. Its effect on a reorder is evaluated later — at preview time by FEATURE-001-03 and at resolution time by FEATURE-001-04 — and a grant that pre-checked availability would be asserting a fact that had expired by the time the grantee used it

* Scenario: A price changed since the last purchase — the catalogue price of a variant on the shared list has moved
   * Given the price of a variant on list L has changed since A last purchased it, A owns L in T's channel, and A and B are current members of group G in T's channel
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L with B as the grantee and `capability` of `APPLY_TO_ORDER`
   * Then proceed, with no warning: **a `ReorderListShare` row stores no price and no reservation, so no monetary value in it can go stale.** Where a price does surface to a grantee — reading L's lines, or receiving the cart that `STORY-001-06-03` produces — it is an integer in the smallest unit of its currency stated alongside the currency code, which defaults to the channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88]; a grant of a list whose line prices total 129900 in the currency code that channel declares carries neither that integer nor any part of it. A decimal monetary value anywhere in this story is a defect rather than a formatting preference, and copying a price onto a grant row would be a defect rather than an optimisation

* Scenario: Concurrent modification or race — two grants of the same list to the same seat are created at the same moment
   * Given customer A owns list L in T's channel, A and B are current members of group G in T's channel, no `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B, and two `shareReorderList` executions carrying that same pair are driven against a **deterministic barrier** — each opened on its own connection and advanced past its read or pre-check, then both released to write together — on the three engine jobs that can hold two concurrent transactions: `e2e-mariadb` [.github/workflows/build_and_test.yml:L202], `e2e-mysql` [.github/workflows/build_and_test.yml:L240] and `e2e-postgres` [.github/workflows/build_and_test.yml:L276]. **`e2e-sqljs` [.github/workflows/build_and_test.yml:L174] is named as excluded from this scenario rather than counted in it**, because it is the in-process WebAssembly initializer `@vendure/testing` ships [packages/testing/src/index.ts:L12] and two transactions there cannot interleave; the constraint-shape half runs on all four instead [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.8 The Seven Plugin-Owned Tables]
   * When the barrier releases both executions to the write together
   * Then proceed with a deterministic outcome rather than blocking either caller: **exactly one `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair afterwards**, both executions report success, and the `grants` field of the `ReorderListSharePayload` each of them returns reports `totalItems` of exactly 1. The guarantee is the named composite unique key `UQ_reorder_list_share_list_grantee` over the list and the grantee and not a service-level pre-check, because two requests can both read "no such grant" before either writes; the losing insert is caught, matched against that one constraint name and resolved to the existing row as an update, while every other database error is re-raised with no driver text, no statement fragment and no constraint name in the API-visible message [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.3 Named Entities Touched]. This scenario runs against a real database engine and not a repository test double, because the guarantee under test is a database constraint and a double has none
This scenario runs against a real database engine and not a repository test double, because the guarantee under test is a database constraint and a double has none. **The same scenario carries the second, different race in this category, and it is different because no unique key touches it:** two `shareReorderList` executions naming **two different grantees** of a list whose `activeGrantCount` is one below the configured maximum are in flight together, and afterwards exactly one reports success while the other returns `ReorderListLimitError` whose `maxItems` equals that maximum, the count of `ACTIVE` rows for the list equals the maximum rather than exceeding it by one, and `reorder_list.activeGrantCount` equals that same count. The guarantee here is the conditional counter update issued as the first write of the transaction, not a count-then-insert check that both callers could pass [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.3.1 The Seats-Per-List Bound Is Enforced By A Conditional Counter Update, Not By A Count-Then-Insert]; the fixture supplies the maximum and this story asserts no figure for it

* Scenario: Channel, currency or language mismatch — a grant made under one channel token is presented under another
   * Given customer A owns list L in the channel identified by token T, one `ACTIVE` `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B in that channel, a second channel identified by token T2 exists, and both A and B are assigned to both channels
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L while sending T2 in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L58-L59], and then customer B reads L under T2
   * Then block both: **a grant granted under one channel token confers no access under another** [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]. A's call returns `ReorderListNotFoundError`, because L belongs to T's channel and not to T2's; B's read under T2 returns L in neither its default nor its opt-in form; the row count for the pair is unchanged; and no row is written carrying T2's channel. **The name customer B sees for L is read under the request's `languageCode`, which defaults to the channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74]** — and the same buyer-supplied string is returned under `en` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/language-code.graphql:L72] and under `de` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/language-code.graphql:L106] alike, because a list name is stored as the buyer entered it and is not a translated string; a capability is an enumerated member and carries no prose to translate either. Whether the prices a grantee sees include tax follows the channel too [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L113], so two seats reading the same list under two tokens may legitimately see two different figures, which is a consequence of channel scoping rather than an inconsistency

The remaining conditional category does not apply and is not force-fitted. **A point-in-time read gone stale between check and commit** is not a case this story has: `shareReorderList` reads no availability and makes no reservation, so it holds nothing that can expire. The authorisation decision it makes *is* point-in-time, but that is not the same class of staleness — it is re-evaluated on every subsequent request, which is what makes `STORY-001-06-02`'s revoked-mid-session behaviour and the removed-seat refusal assertable at all [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.8 The Grant Lifecycle, And Why Revocation Is A State Rather Than A Delete].

Platform 3.7.0 links external authentication to a pre-existing account **only when the external email address is verified**, and marking a provider-verified email as verified additionally requires a custom authentication strategy. **Its effect on this story is narrower than an earlier version of this paragraph claimed, and the over-reach is corrected rather than carried into the criteria.** That version made a *verified* member a condition of grantee resolution, which would have this story re-enforcing an external-provider rule on an ordinary account lookup. **The release change governs external-login linking only.** Where a deployment authenticates seats through an external identity provider, that provider's identity is linked to a pre-existing account only if the external address is verified. **What follows here is a precondition on the environment, not a check this story performs:** a grant needs a grantee with a usable account that is a current member of the named group in the active channel, and how that account came to exist is the deployment's affair. **So `shareReorderList` reads no verification state and no criterion in section 5 asserts one.** A seat whose external identity was never linked because its address was unverified simply is not a current member of the group, which is already the second of AC-5's six indistinguishable cases reached by a different route and needs no further rule. It is also why this story declares no invitation surface, no pending-grant state and no email transport of its own.
