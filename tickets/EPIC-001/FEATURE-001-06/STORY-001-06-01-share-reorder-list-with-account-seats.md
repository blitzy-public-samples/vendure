## 1. Story Title

**Share a reorder list with account seats, so that a Buying Account Administrator grants one colleague the capability to reorder from that list and nothing further.**

Short form, transcribed from the epic's story table without rewording so that the two files agree: **Share a reorder list with account seats** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table]. The heading above is the same sentence in action-object-outcome form, measured at 162 characters and so inside the 255-character ceiling this tier sets.

What this story delivers is the one plugin-owned share table, its additive migration, the `shareReorderList` mutation gated `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L134], the in-resolver ownership-and-membership predicate that makes that gate a control rather than a label, and the two read-path values the sharing model finally populates.

Five things that could be mistaken for part of it are not.

- **Creating the list, naming it and writing its lines** are FEATURE-001-01's operations, not this story's [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.2 Contribution To The Epic]. This story grants a capability over a list that already exists and never writes a list line.
- **Revoking a grant** is `revokeReorderListShare`, owned by `STORY-001-06-02`, together with the revoked-mid-session, removed-seat and moved-seat cases [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§3. User Stories Index].

## 3. PRECEDENT In This Repository

**PRECEDENT:** partial

**The searched set, named so the verdict is checkable rather than asserted.** The whole of `packages/dev-server/example-plugins/`, the whole of `packages/dev-server/test-plugins/`, and `packages/dashboard/test-plans/`. **Half one — the mechanisms are shipped, and a reviewer should price them as re-application rather than invention.**

- **The permission machinery exists and is taught with a saved list.** `CrudPermissionDefinition` derives four permissions from one name — its own documentation states that a `name` of 'Wishlist' will create the four create, read, update and delete permissions [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L114-L115] — over the `PermissionDefinition` base [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L86] whose `Permission` getter the decorator consumes [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L107], registered through `authOptions.customPermissions` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L123-L128] and applied with `@Allow` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L134]. The class itself is at [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L146]. **This story registers none of it**, for the reason section 5 gives under AC-3; the family is disclosed as precedent because a reviewer will otherwise expect a definition to appear.
- **Customer-scoped plugin-owned data hanging off a customer is shipped.** The wishlist example plugin declares its entity, its provider and its Shop API extension in plugin metadata [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/wishlist.plugin.ts:L9-L16] and mutates configuration from the plugin's own configuration function rather than by editing core [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/wishlist.plugin.ts:L17-L26]. The entity shape, the additive migration, the `extend type Mutation` schema extension and the session-derived ownership guard are all re-application of that.
- **The gate this story uses is the shipped one for a buyer-owned resource.** Every customer-facing operation in that plugin is declared `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/wishlist.resolver.ts:L11-L12], and the service resolves the acting customer from the session, refusing a request with no active user [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L74-L76].

**Half two — nothing in this repository shares customer-owned data between customers, and that is the part with no analogue to copy.** The search above returns no plugin that grants one customer access to another customer's rows: its only hits under those two trees are shared-constant imports. The Shop API schema tree contains no sharing surface and no grant surface of any kind. And the closest shipped analogue is single-owner by construction — the wishlist plugin's entire published surface is three operations, `activeCustomerWishlist` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L13] declared through `extend type Query` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L12-L14], `addToWishlist` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L17] and `removeFromWishlist` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L18] declared through `extend type Mutation` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L16-L19]. **Three operations, no sharing surface, no grantee concept, no second reader.** Its data hangs off one customer through an internal relation custom field [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/wishlist.plugin.ts:L18-L24], which is a shape that cannot express a second party at all.

**So the verdict is `partial` and not `none`, and not `near-identical` either.** The mechanism is precedented; the cross-customer semantics are not. Concretely new in this story are the share row itself, the grantee-resolved-by-email-within-one-group rule, the current-membership revalidation that makes a grant stop working when a seat leaves the account, the channel-scoped grant, and the normalized refusal that keeps a pair of error results from becoming an enumeration oracle [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.13 Precedent In This Repository — Partial].

**One deliberate departure from the shipped precedent.** The wishlist plugin stores ownership as a relation custom field pushed onto `Customer` and hidden with an internal flag [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/wishlist.plugin.ts:L18-L24]. This story does not follow it. Ownership and capability are columns on the plugin-owned share table, because the custom-field route widens published operation signatures as a side effect, which the epic records as a reportable violation rather than an accepted cost [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.1 Repository-Discovered Collisions]. The dev-server custom-fields object is empty today [packages/dev-server/dev-config.ts:L116] and is still empty after this story ships.

## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line on any renderer and when the block is pasted into an external tracker. Every story file in this set uses that same convention.

```text
As a Buying Account Administrator,
I want to grant one named seat of my buying account the capability to view or reorder from a reorder list I own,
So that a colleague places the account's repeat order from that list without my re-entering it for them and without gaining access to any other list, cart or seat of the account.
```

### 4.1 INVEST Criteria

- **Independent:** this story needs no other story in this feature. Its prerequisites are the epic's project bootstrap [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.9 Project Bootstrap — A Prerequisite Of The Whole Epic, Owned By No Story], one `ReorderList` row from `STORY-001-01-01`, and two customer accounts in one customer group; the reverse edge does not exist, because `STORY-001-06-02` cannot revoke a grant until this story creates one [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§4.3 Intra-Feature Story Order]. Section 8 classifies each prerequisite.
- **Negotiable:** open to a maintainer are the seats-per-list bound and which result reports a share that would exceed it, whether an existing grant survives a later reduction of that bound, and the erasure window for a share row — all three recorded as decisions rather than answered here [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. **Not negotiable** is the number of permission definitions this story registers, which is zero: a custom permission cannot be granted to the Customer Role, so a buyer-facing mutation gated on one would be refused for every buyer in every channel [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings, And The Complete Published-Symbol Inventory]. Also not negotiable is that every refusal a caller without visibility can provoke returns one identical result.
- **Valuable:** without a grant row, a buying account's repeat purchase set is readable by exactly one person and every colleague rebuilds it. This story is the first point in the epic at which a second customer may legitimately act on a row they do not own, and it is what `STORY-001-06-02` revokes and `STORY-001-06-03` reorders from.
- **Estimable:** the shape is one plugin-owned table plus its additive migration plus one mutation, which the epic's rubric prices at exactly three points, and every mechanism it consumes already ships in this repository [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric, So The Numbers Are Reproducible].
- **Sized Appropriately:** three points on the epic's Fibonacci scale, sitting on the three-point anchor with no upward adjustment, and the scale deliberately omits thirteen so that an oversized story is split instead of estimated [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric, So The Numbers Are Reproducible].

**Step 2 — start the server and leave it running.** This command does not return; it owns terminal 1 until interrupted, and every step after it is issued from terminal 2 [packages/dev-server/package.json:L13] and [CONTRIBUTING.md:L178-L181].

**Step 4 — from terminal 2, authenticate as customer A over the Shop API** with the existing `login` mutation, using A's address and the password `test` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L116]. ## 5. Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria. Each names exactly one GraphQL operation, so a failing criterion points at a single published contract, and each states which customer is authenticated, whether that customer owns the list or holds a share, the channel token the request carries and the `languageCode` it resolves under. The five required coverage classes map onto them as follows: valid input is AC-1, invalid or incomplete input is AC-2, an error naming the exact type returned is AC-4 and AC-5, an edge case is AC-6, and the assertion that an existing operation is unchanged is AC-8.

**AC-3 and AC-4 together are the mandatory negative criterion this feature makes non-optional, split into its two terminal branches so that a failure names one branch rather than two.** AC-3 is the caller with no relationship to the list at all, refused with the normalized result; AC-4 is the caller holding a live grant attempting an operation reserved to the owner, refused with the one result this feature declares. AC-7 additionally carries the read-path regression the parent feature calls the one that matters most here — that a client which never passes the opt-in argument sees exactly what it saw before this story shipped.

**Three properties of the operation are stated once here rather than repeated in every criterion.**

- **Naming convention used throughout.** Customer **A** owns list **L** and is the only party that may share it. Customer **B** is the grantee seat. Customer **C** is a third customer who neither owns `L` nor holds a grant on it. Group **G** is the customer group all seats of the account belong to. **T** is the channel token carried in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L58-L59] and stored as a unique column on the channel it identifies [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]; **T2** is a second channel's token. Unless a criterion says otherwise the request resolves its `languageCode` from the channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74], which is `en` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/language-code.graphql:L72] in the fixture.
- **`shareReorderList` is a single synchronous write.** It enqueues no job, publishes no event and schedules no task, so there is no asynchronous trigger and no observable completion signal to name in any criterion below. The event publication in this domain belongs to `STORY-001-07-02`.
- **`ReorderPermissionDeniedError` is returned inside `data` as a member of the operation's result union, and is not a thrown error.** That distinction is asserted rather than assumed, because the two are observed in different places: a thrown `ForbiddenError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L67] or `UserInputError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L27] surfaces as a top-level GraphQL error entry with no `shareReorderList` payload — which is how the shipped precedent reports a variant it cannot resolve [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L38] — whereas every criterion below asserts a **returned** union member, reachable through an inline fragment on the payload. A criterion satisfied by a top-level error entry has not been satisfied.

AC-1: The owner grants one seat a reorder capability, and exactly one grant exists
* Given customer A is authenticated on the Shop API under channel token T with `languageCode` `en`, A's session holds exactly `Permission.Authenticated` — the single permission the Customer Role is created with [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L443] — together with `Permission.Owner` and no plugin-registered permission of any name; A owns list L in T's channel; customers A and B are both current members of group G in T's channel; and no `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B
* When customer A executes the `shareReorderList` mutation with `reorderListId` of L, `customerGroupId` of G, `granteeEmailAddress` of B's address and `capability` of `REORDER`
* Then the mutation succeeds rather than being refused and returns a `ReorderList` whose `id` equals L and whose `access` is `OWNED`; the owner's grant collection for L reports a total of exactly 1, ordered ascending by the date each grant was made with the grantee identifier as the tie-break so that the assertion is on a defined position rather than on an accident [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces — Two New Mutations, Zero Existing Signatures Changed]; exactly one `ReorderListShare` row exists carrying `reorderListId` of L, `granteeCustomerId` of B, `grantorCustomerId` of A, `customerGroupId` of G, `channelId` of T's channel, `capability` of `REORDER`, `state` of `ACTIVE` and a null `revokedAt`; and no column, relation or custom field is added to `Customer` [packages/core/src/entity/customer/customer.entity.ts:L23], `CustomerGroup` [packages/core/src/entity/customer-group/customer-group.entity.ts:L18] or `Channel` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]

**A note on AC-1, and on one gap between two sibling files that this story reports rather than papers over.** The parent feature rules that a list's grants are read *through the list* and that the collection is paginated with a deterministic order [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces — Two New Mutations, Zero Existing Signatures Changed]. **The `ReorderList` type as FEATURE-001-01 publishes it declares `id`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `name`, `lineCount`, `lines`, `access` and `grantedCapabilities`, and no grant field** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces — Eight Shop API Operations, Zero Existing Signatures Changed]. The two statements do not agree, and the epic's published-symbol inventory lists `ReorderListGrant` as an object type while listing no paginated list type for it [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings, And The Complete Published-Symbol Inventory]. **This story therefore names no field for that collection**, because inventing one would either contradict FEATURE-001-01's published contract or add a field to a type this feature ruled byte-identical. The criterion above is written against the collection as the parent feature defines it, and **declaring it in FEATURE-001-06's own schema-definition block is work that belongs to that file** — the searches that established the gap are the two section references in this paragraph, and it is reported here under the same rule that requires a discrepancy in a source to be noted rather than reproduced [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.3 Repository Documentation Discrepancies — Noted, Not Propagated]. Every other assertion in AC-1 is observable without it: the returned list, the `access` value and the stored row are each readable on their own.

AC-2: A list identifier naming no row is refused and writes nothing
* Given customer A is authenticated under channel token T with `languageCode` `en`, A and B are current members of group G in T's channel, and the identifier submitted as `reorderListId` matches no `ReorderList` row in any channel — so customer A neither owns the named list nor holds a share on it, because there is no row for either relationship to attach to — a case a caller can reach by iteration, because the default identifier strategy is auto-increment [packages/core/src/config/default-config.ts:L101]
* When customer A executes the `shareReorderList` mutation with that identifier, `customerGroupId` of G, `granteeEmailAddress` of B's address and `capability` of `VIEW`
* Then the mutation returns `ReorderListNotFoundError`, the count of `ReorderListShare` rows in the deployment is unchanged at its exact prior value, and the response carries no partial payload — the result is the single normalized refusal the parent feature requires for every case in which a caller cannot see a list [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.12 The Error Vocabulary This Feature Introduces]

AC-3: A customer who neither owns the list nor holds a grant on it is refused, and the refusal discloses nothing
* Given customer C is authenticated under channel token T with `languageCode` `en`, C's session holds `Permission.Authenticated` [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L443] and `Permission.Owner` and no plugin-registered permission; list L exists in T's channel and is owned by customer A; and no `ReorderListShare` row exists whose `granteeCustomerId` is C
* When customer C executes the `shareReorderList` mutation with `reorderListId` of L, `customerGroupId` of G, `granteeEmailAddress` of C's own address and `capability` of `REORDER`
* Then the mutation returns `ReorderListNotFoundError`, that response is identical field by field to the response AC-2 obtains for an identifier naming no row at all, no `ReorderListShare` row is written, and customer A's grant collection for L is unchanged in both its total and its contents

**A note on AC-3, which is the ruling this whole feature inherits and the reason a permission gate is not the control.** The platform states the caveat in its own source: ownership must be enforced by custom logic inside the resolver, and because ownership cannot be defined generally nor statically encoded at build time, any resolver using `Permission.Owner` **must** include logic enforcing that only the owner of the resource has access — and without that logic the effect is the equivalent of using `Permission.Public` [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-permissions.ts:L32-L34], the whole caveat being written out in one block in that file [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-permissions.ts:L12-L33]. **A permission gate alone is not a control.** Two independent reads confirm it rather than restate it: `Permission.Owner` is declared with `assignable: false` and `internal: true` [packages/core/src/common/constants.ts:L27-L31], so no deployment configuration can make ownership hold; and the guard sets its owner-only flag precisely when the session does **not** hold the listed permission [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L109], which the access-control strategy then admits [packages/core/src/config/auth/default-entity-access-control-strategy.ts:L56]. So the gate refuses essentially nobody, and `shareReorderList` performs an explicit ownership-or-valid-share check of its own. **A `CrudPermissionDefinition` gate [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L146] is not the alternative:** the Customer Role is created holding one permission [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L433-L444] and cannot be amended, the update path throwing when asked to modify it [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L290-L291], so a registered permission would refuse every buyer instead. **The refusal is `ReorderListNotFoundError` and not `ReorderPermissionDeniedError`, deliberately:** distinguishing the two would let a caller iterating identifiers tell a real list belonging to somebody else from an identifier that names nothing, which is the enumeration oracle the platform's own single-order read refuses to open, with its inline comment naming that reason [packages/core/src/api/resolvers/shop/shop-order.resolver.ts:L141-L143].

AC-4: A grantee holding an active grant cannot re-share the list, and receives ReorderPermissionDeniedError
* Given customer B is authenticated under channel token T with `languageCode` `en`; B holds an `ACTIVE` `ReorderListShare` row on list L with `capability` of `REORDER`; B is a current member of group G in T's channel; and B does not own L, whose owner is customer A
* When customer B executes the `shareReorderList` mutation with `reorderListId` of L, `customerGroupId` of G, `granteeEmailAddress` of a third current member of G and `capability` of `VIEW`
* Then the mutation returns `ReorderPermissionDeniedError` as a member of the result union inside `data` rather than as a top-level error entry, no second `ReorderListShare` row is written, the total of customer A's grant collection for L is still exactly 1, and B's own grant is untouched with `state` still `ACTIVE` and `revokedAt` still null — because the capability set is closed at `VIEW` and `REORDER` and neither member confers the power to grant, so sharing and revoking are reachable by ownership alone [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces — Two New Mutations, Zero Existing Signatures Changed]

AC-5: A grantee address that resolves to no live current member of the named group is refused identically in all four cases
* Given customer A is authenticated under channel token T with `languageCode` `en` and owns list L in T's channel, and A is a current member of group G in T's channel
* When customer A executes the `shareReorderList` mutation with `reorderListId` of L, `customerGroupId` of G and `capability` of `REORDER`, once for each of these four `granteeEmailAddress` values in turn — an address matching no `Customer` row at all, the address of a customer who exists but is not a current member of G, the address of a customer who is a member of G and has been soft-deleted [packages/core/src/entity/customer/customer.entity.ts:L29], and the address of a customer outside T's channel
* Then each of the four calls returns `ReorderPermissionDeniedError`, the four responses are identical field by field so that none of them discloses which case occurred, no `ReorderListShare` row is written by any of the four, and the membership decision is read live for each call through the platform's own single-customer membership query rather than from a stored copy [packages/core/src/service/services/customer.service.ts:L179], which is channel-scoped [packages/core/src/service/services/customer.service.ts:L184] and excludes soft-deleted customers [packages/core/src/service/services/customer.service.ts:L188] without the plugin implementing either rule

**A note on AC-5, and on why one error result carries four outcomes.** A grantee lookup that searched every customer would be an account-existence oracle: identifiers are sequential [packages/core/src/config/default-config.ts:L101] and an address is guessable, so a response distinguishing "no such customer" from "not a member of your account" would confirm registration to anyone who asked. The four cases are therefore indistinguishable by construction [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.5 Named API Surfaces — Two New Mutations, Zero Existing Signatures Changed]. `ReorderPermissionDeniedError` is safe here for the reason the parent feature gives: the caller already owns the list, so the response discloses nothing about the list and nothing about whether the address belongs to anybody. **The read direction is also asserted rather than left to implementation taste:** the seat set is never hydrated to answer a single yes-or-no question, so `CustomerGroup.customers` [packages/core/src/entity/customer-group/customer-group.entity.ts:L26] is not the read, and the platform's own customer-group promotion condition resolves membership in the required direction [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.6.1 The Authorisation Read Shape — One Customer's Memberships, Never One Account's Members]. Neither this criterion nor any other in this story asserts a bound on the number of seats a list may be shared with, because no such bound exists to assert against; section 8 records it as a decision the epic carries.

AC-6: Granting the same pair twice leaves one row, and a grant after a revocation reuses that row

## 7. Edge Cases

Five scenarios, which is the maximum this tier permits, and the five categories the parent feature requires are covered one apiece in the order below. Two of the five resolve to *proceed* rather than to a block or a warning, and that is stated honestly rather than force-fitted into a failure this operation cannot have: sharing a list evaluates neither availability nor price, so the two catalogue-movement categories are genuine non-events here and the value of recording them is precisely that a reader does not go looking for a check that would be wrong to build.

* Scenario: Zero, null or empty collection — the list being shared holds zero lines
   * Given customer A owns list L in the channel identified by token T, L holds exactly zero `ReorderListLine` rows, and customers A and B are current members of group G in T's channel
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L with `granteeEmailAddress` of B's address, `customerGroupId` of G and `capability` of `REORDER`
   * Then proceed: the grant is written, exactly one `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B with `state` of `ACTIVE`, and customer B's opt-in read returns L with a line count of exactly zero. A grant is a capability over a list rather than over its contents, so an empty list is shareable and the emptiness surfaces at reorder time as `NoReorderableLinesError`, which `STORY-001-02-03` owns and this story does not return

* Scenario: An item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase — a shared list references a variant that is now disabled
   * Given customer A owns list L in T's channel, one of L's lines references a product variant whose enabled flag is now false, and A and B are current members of group G in T's channel
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L with B as the grantee and `capability` of `REORDER`
   * Then proceed, with no warning and no audit record: **availability is not evaluated at share time at all.** This operation reads no variant, no stock level and no catalogue state, so a disabled variant cannot affect a grant. Its effect on a reorder is evaluated later — at preview time by FEATURE-001-03 and at resolution time by FEATURE-001-04 — and a grant that pre-checked availability would be asserting a fact that had expired by the time the grantee used it

* Scenario: A price changed since the last purchase — the catalogue price of a variant on the shared list has moved
   * Given the price of a variant on list L has changed since A last purchased it, A owns L in T's channel, and A and B are current members of group G in T's channel
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L with B as the grantee and `capability` of `REORDER`
   * Then proceed, with no warning: **a `ReorderListShare` row stores no price and no reservation, so no monetary value in it can go stale.** Where a price does surface to a grantee — reading L's lines, or receiving the cart that `STORY-001-06-03` produces — it is an integer in the smallest unit of its currency stated alongside the currency code, which defaults to the channel's `defaultCurrencyCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L88]; a grant of a list whose line prices total 129900 in the currency code that channel declares carries neither that integer nor any part of it. A decimal monetary value anywhere in this story is a defect rather than a formatting preference, and copying a price onto a grant row would be a defect rather than an optimisation

* Scenario: Concurrent modification or race — two grants of the same list to the same seat are created at the same moment
   * Given customer A owns list L in T's channel, A and B are current members of group G in T's channel, no `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B, and two `shareReorderList` executions carrying that same pair are in flight together
   * When both executions reach the write
   * Then proceed with a deterministic outcome rather than blocking either caller: **exactly one `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair afterwards**, both executions report success, and customer A's grant collection for L reports a total of exactly 1. The guarantee is the named composite unique key `UQ_reorder_list_share_list_grantee` over the list and the grantee and not a service-level pre-check, because two requests can both read "no such grant" before either writes; the losing insert is caught, matched against that one constraint name and resolved to the existing row as an update, while every other database error is re-raised with no driver text, no statement fragment and no constraint name in the API-visible message [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.3 Named Entities Touched]. This scenario runs against a real database engine and not a repository test double, because the guarantee under test is a database constraint and a double has none

* Scenario: Channel, currency or language mismatch — a grant made under one channel token is presented under another
   * Given customer A owns list L in the channel identified by token T, one `ACTIVE` `ReorderListShare` row exists for the pair of L and B in that channel, a second channel identified by token T2 exists, and both A and B are assigned to both channels
   * When customer A executes `shareReorderList` for L while sending T2 in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L58-L59], and then customer B reads L under T2
   * Then block both: **a grant granted under one channel token confers no access under another** [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]. A's call returns `ReorderListNotFoundError`, because L belongs to T's channel and not to T2's; B's read under T2 returns L in neither its default nor its opt-in form; the row count for the pair is unchanged; and no row is written carrying T2's channel. **The name customer B sees for L is read under the request's `languageCode`, which defaults to the channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74]** — and the same buyer-supplied string is returned under `en` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/language-code.graphql:L72] and under `de` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/language-code.graphql:L106] alike, because a list name is stored as the buyer entered it and is not a translated string; a capability is an enumerated member and carries no prose to translate either. Whether the prices a grantee sees include tax follows the channel too [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L113], so two seats reading the same list under two tokens may legitimately see two different figures, which is a consequence of channel scoping rather than an inconsistency

The remaining conditional category does not apply and is not force-fitted. **A point-in-time read gone stale between check and commit** is not a case this story has: `shareReorderList` reads no availability and makes no reservation, so it holds nothing that can expire. The authorisation decision it makes *is* point-in-time, but that is not the same class of staleness — it is re-evaluated on every subsequent request, which is what makes `STORY-001-06-02`'s revoked-mid-session behaviour and the removed-seat refusal assertable at all [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.8 The Grant Lifecycle, And Why Revocation Is A State Rather Than A Delete].

### 8.1 Decisions This Story Waits On

Dependencies on a decision rather than on code. **None is taken here, no figure is supplied for any of them, and each belongs to a maintainer.** The epic's own decision list is the authority and this sub-section refers to it rather than re-arguing it [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8. Decisions Required Before Build].

- **Whether a cap exists on the number of seats a single reorder list may be shared with, or an explicit ruling that there is none.** This is the decision this story most directly waits on, and the epic carries it as a ranked product decision in three parts: the cap or the explicit no-cap ruling; which result reports a share that would exceed a cap, given that one of the six error results this ticket set declares must be chosen or a seventh admitted; and whether an existing grant survives a later reduction of the cap [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. **An explicit "no cap" is a valid outcome**, which is why the entry is phrased as a ruling rather than as a number. **Why the fan-out is otherwise unbounded, stated from the platform rather than asserted:** seat membership is the existing customer-group relation [packages/core/src/entity/customer-group/customer-group.entity.ts:L26] and nothing in it limits how many customers a group may hold, its own documentation describing it as a grouping for features such as promotions and tax rules and declaring no member limit [packages/core/src/entity/customer-group/customer-group.entity.ts:L12-L13]. **What this story can and cannot finalise while the decision is open is separated rather than blurred:** every authorisation branch, the identical-refusal requirement, the idempotence of a repeat grant and the deterministic ordering of the grant collection are all independent of the figure and are built and accepted now; the one criterion that cannot be written is the refusal at the bound, because "the grant that would exceed the maximum is refused" needs the maximum to be a number before it is a test. - **The erasure window for a share row. It gates this story's sign-off rather than its construction.** The mechanism is settled by the parent feature — a share row is purged rather than anonymised when its grantee is soft-deleted or leaves the group, and access stops at the first request after either event because membership is re-read [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-06-buying-account-list-sharing.md:§2.3 Named Entities Touched]. **What is undecided is how long after those events the purge runs**, and this story is named among the five the epic's erasure-window decision blocks [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. ### 8.2 One Inherited Precondition — A Research Finding, Not A Repository Citation

**This paragraph carries no `[path:locator]` citation, and its absence is deliberate: the finding was established by research into the published release rather than by reading a file in this checkout, so there is nothing in the repository to cite and inventing a citation would be worse than stating the gap.** It is sourced from the epic's research-discovered findings [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§2.3 Confirmed Platform Version].

Platform 3.7.0 links external authentication to a pre-existing account **only when the external email address is verified**, and marking a provider-verified email as verified additionally requires a custom authentication strategy. Its practical effect on this story is narrow and worth naming, because this story resolves a seat by email address: a seat invited by address cannot be silently linked to an unverified external account, so a grant naming an address that does not resolve to a live, current, verified member of the named group in the active channel **has no grantee to attach to and is refused rather than held pending**. That is the second of AC-5's four indistinguishable cases reached by a different route, and it is why this story declares no invitation surface, no pending-grant state and no email transport of its own.
