## 1. Story Title

**Add a product variant with a quantity to a named reorder list, so that a Returning Buyer stores the habitual pack size alongside the item and reproduces both together later.**

Short form, transcribed from the epic's story table without rewording so that the two files agree: **Add a variant with a quantity to a list** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table]. The heading above is the same sentence, measured at 172 characters and so within the 255-character ceiling this tier sets.

The unit of work the title names is one finished slice: the first write to the `ReorderListLine` table, the `addItemToReorderList` mutation that performs it, the per-list-and-variant deduplication rule that decides what a second add for the same variant does, and the rejection of a non-positive quantity at both the service and the database.

Four things that could be mistaken for part of it are not. The `ReorderList` row a line hangs from is created by `STORY-001-01-01`, and so is the additive migration that creates both tables, in the plural [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§3. User Stories Index] — this story writes the first row into the second of those tables rather than creating it. Changing a line's quantity after the fact and removing a line are two of the four mutations `STORY-001-01-03` owns. Reading the lines back over the Shop API is `STORY-001-01-04`. And the plugin package itself, together with its registration in the dev configuration, is a prerequisite of the whole epic that no story owns, prices or claims [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.9 Project Bootstrap].

**One boundary is worth stating because the operation's name invites the opposite reading.** Adding a variant to a list is not adding it to a cart. Nothing in this story touches the active order, no `OrderLine` row is written, and the two published Shop API mutations that do add to an order — `addItemToOrder` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L72] and `addItemsToOrder` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L74] — are untouched in both signature and behaviour. Materialising a list into a cart is `applyReorderToActiveOrder`, owned by FEATURE-001-02.

## 2. Source Traceability

> "Make it effortless for returning buyers to reorder the items they purchase regularly"

That sentence is clause C1 of the objective statement, and the epic maps it to five of the twenty-five stories with this one second [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§10.2 Objective-Clause Map]. The label on this story is therefore a quoted clause of the objective statement rather than a derived one.

How a per-line quantity serves the clause: the items a buyer purchases regularly are bought in habitual amounts, so a saved set that records only *which* variants were bought still leaves the buyer re-entering *how many* of each on every reorder. Storing an integer quantity on the line is what makes the saved set reproduce the amount as well as the identity, and it is the single column that separates this feature from the saved-list plugin the platform already ships.

**The clause is quoted, and the scope of what this story can claim from it is narrower than the clause itself.** Clause C1 spans five stories, and this one contributes the quantity dimension and nothing else. It does not make a reorder reach a cart, which is FEATURE-001-02, and it does not tell the buyer that a price or an availability changed, which is FEATURE-001-03. A reader pricing this story against the whole of C1 would over-price it.

## 3. PRECEDENT In This Repository

**PRECEDENT:** near-identical

**A complete saved-list plugin already ships in this repository, and its add path is the direct analogue of this story's.** `packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/` is not a sketch: its own README states that it is the complete plugin described in the platform's "Writing your first plugin" tutorial [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/README.md:L3]. Its add operation is `addToWishlist(productVariantId: ID!)`, declared through an `extend type Mutation` block [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L17] and [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L16], and it is gated exactly as this story's mutation will be, with `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/wishlist.resolver.ts:L12].

**The delta is one column, and stating it precisely is the whole justification for this story existing.** `addToWishlist` takes **no quantity argument** — its only argument is a variant identifier [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L17]. The entity behind it carries a `ProductVariant` relation [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/entities/wishlist-item.entity.ts:L10-L11] and a `productVariantId` column [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/entities/wishlist-item.entity.ts:L13-L14] and nothing else; the whole file is fifteen lines and **there is no quantity column anywhere in it**. So the precedent can express "this variant is saved" and cannot express "six of this variant is saved". A per-line integer quantity is the novelty this story delivers, and it is a small one — which is why the verdict above is near-identical rather than partial.

**The duplicate-handling precedent, disclosed rather than glossed.** The shipped service resolves the variant through the variant service [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L36], throws the platform's input-error class when the identifier resolves to nothing [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L38], and then, on finding the variant already present, **returns the collection unchanged and silently skips the add** — the comment in the source says so in as many words [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L42].

**This story departs from silent skipping, and the quantity is the reason.** Silence is a defensible outcome when a second add can have no observable effect: adding a variant that is already saved leaves the precedent's collection byte-identical, so there is nothing to report. Once a line carries a quantity that is no longer true — a second add of the same variant either changes the stored amount or does not, and the two outcomes are distinguishable by the caller. Returning silently would leave the buyer unable to tell whether asking for six more of something they already had saved did anything at all. The parent feature therefore fixes the outcome rather than inheriting it: an add for a variant already present resolves to the existing line and **adds to its quantity**, and the unique constraint over the list and the variant exists to make that rule unbypassable rather than to raise an error [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]. AC-2 asserts it.

**One further departure, inherited from the parent feature rather than chosen here.** The shipped plugin stores ownership as a `relation` custom field pushed onto `Customer` and hidden with an internal flag; this feature stores ownership as a `customerId` column on its own table instead, because the custom-field route widens published operation signatures as a side effect and that is a reportable violation rather than an accepted cost [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.1 Repository-Discovered Collisions]. The consequence for this story is that a list line reaches its owner through the parent list row rather than through a customer custom field, and the dev-server custom-fields object is empty today [packages/dev-server/dev-config.ts:L116] and is still empty after this story ships.

**The searched set that produced the verdict.** The whole of `packages/dev-server/example-plugins/`, the whole of `packages/dev-server/test-plugins/`, and `packages/dashboard/test-plans/`. The closest match in that set is the wishlist plugin's add path, and it is close enough that the verdict is near-identical rather than partial. Nothing in the searched set carries a saved-list line with a quantity on it, so the one column this story adds is genuinely absent from the repository rather than merely rearranged.

**The consequence, stated plainly.** Because the precedent is near-identical, this story is disqualified from the epic's demonstration-slice nomination on the same ground its sibling is: demonstrating an add-to-saved-list operation would demonstrate a shape the platform already ships and teaches. That nomination belongs to `STORY-001-02-01` [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.6 Nomination]. Nor does this story hold the harness-proving nomination, which belongs to `STORY-001-01-01` because that story owns the first additive migration and has no prerequisite story, neither of which is true here.

## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line when the file is read on any renderer and when it is pasted into an external tracker. **Every story file in this set uses that same convention**, so a reader comparing two stories compares like with like.

```text
As a Returning Buyer,
I want to add a product variant to one of my named reorder lists at a quantity I choose,
So that the list stores the amount I habitually buy alongside the item itself, and a later reorder reproduces the quantity as well as the identity of what I bought.
```

### 4.1 INVEST Criteria

- **Independent, with one prerequisite named rather than hidden.** A line has nowhere to hang until a `ReorderList` row exists, and that row is created by `createReorderList` in `STORY-001-01-01`, which also ships the single additive migration creating both tables [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§4.3 Intra-Feature Story Order]. The dependency is one-way and is a prerequisite rather than a co-requisite: nothing in `STORY-001-01-01` waits on this story. **Once one list exists, this story delivers value on its own terms** — a buyer with a single empty list gains the ability to populate it at chosen quantities, which is an outcome observable through one mutation with no other story merged. Section 8 classifies the prerequisite as a data dependency on one list row and a shared code path over the entity, the service and the migration.
- **Negotiable, and the boundary of what is negotiable is narrower than it looks.** Open to a maintainer: the value of the plugin option bounding the number of lines one list may hold, which AC-6 asserts against a configured value rather than a chosen default [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. **Not negotiable is the duplicate-add outcome**, which the parent feature fixes as resolve-and-accumulate rather than leaving to the implementation [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]; **nor is the argument list, the input object or the result union**, all three of which are published in the feature's contract so that two stories cannot ship two shapes of one operation [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]; **nor is the number of permission definitions this story registers, and the answer is zero.** A custom permission cannot be granted to the Customer Role, which ships holding a single permission [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L443] and cannot be amended because the update path refuses to modify it [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L290], so a buyer-facing mutation gated on a registered permission would be refused for every buyer. The epic records that as settled rather than open [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings].
- **Valuable:** a saved set that records identity without amount still makes the buyer re-enter every quantity on every reorder, which is the effort clause C1 exists to remove. This is the story that writes the quantity, and every later path that materialises a list into a cart reads the column it writes.
- **Estimable:** the shape is a re-application of shipped work — one row written into a table another story creates, one service method, one permission-gated resolver, one result union — anchored to the wishlist plugin's add path, which exists in this repository and differs by one column [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L34].
- **Sized Appropriately:** three points on the epic's Fibonacci scale, transcribed from this story's row rather than recomputed here [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table]. The scale deliberately omits thirteen so that an oversized story is split instead of estimated [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric]. Section 9 records why this row sits at three rather than at the two-point band even though it adds no table of its own.
- **Testable:** every criterion in section 5 names one GraphQL operation and asserts a state a test can read — an exact line count, an exact integer quantity, an exact error result type name, an exact stored column value, or an exact unchanged total on the active order. No criterion asserts a timing, a rate or a proportion, because this repository declares none and none is invented here.

**Step 6 — execute the mutation below from terminal 2**, substituting the list identifier from step 4 and the variant identifier from step 5, and read the returned line collection.

```graphql
mutation {
    addItemToReorderList(
        input: { reorderListId: "<id from step 4>", productVariantId: "<id from step 5>", quantity: 6 }
    ) {
        ... on ReorderList {
            id
            name
            lineCount
            lines {
                totalItems
                items { id productVariantId quantity }
            }
        }
        ... on ReorderListNotFoundError {
            errorCode
            message
        }
        ... on ReorderListLimitError {
            errorCode
            message
        }
        ... on ReorderListNotFoundError {
            errorCode
            message
        }
        ... on ErrorResult {
            errorCode
            message
        }
    }
}
```

**Expected:** a `ReorderList` payload whose `lineCount` is exactly 1, whose `lines` connection reports a `totalItems` of exactly 1 with its `items` collection holding exactly one entry, and whose single entry carries the `productVariantId` submitted in step 5 and a `quantity` of exactly 6.

**Then execute the identical mutation a second time, unchanged.** Expected on the second call: still exactly one entry in `lines`, still the same `productVariantId`, and a `quantity` of exactly 12 — the deduplication rule resolving to the existing line and accumulating rather than writing a second row. That second call is what makes the quantity dimension observable to a reader who runs this path, and it is the outcome AC-2 asserts.

The two blocks above are request shapes, not implementations. The `... on ErrorResult` selection is present so that a reader whose server returns a member the client does not yet know still sees a code and a message rather than an empty object, which is the default-branch discipline the epic requires of every consuming example because the published error enum grows whenever an error result is declared [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.1 Repository-Discovered Collisions].

## 5. Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria. Each names exactly one GraphQL operation, so a failing criterion points at a single published contract. The five required coverage classes map onto them as follows: valid input is AC-1, invalid or incomplete input is AC-3, an error naming the exact type returned is AC-3, AC-4 and AC-6, an edge case is AC-5, and the assertion that an existing operation is unchanged is AC-8. AC-2 and AC-6 are the two criteria the epic names as mandatory for this story — the duplicate-variant outcome and the non-positive-quantity rejection.

AC-4 and AC-7 between them carry the four authorisation assertions the parent feature makes mandatory in every story of this feature: an unauthenticated request is refused, **a session holding no custom permission succeeds**, a request authenticated as a different customer is refused, and a request carrying a foreign channel token reads nothing [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.7 The Existing Mechanism This Feature Consumes Rather Than Rebuilds]. The second of those is written as a positive assertion because its absence is what would let the design fault the epic records as collision C7 reach production.

Two properties of the operation are stated once here rather than repeated in every criterion. `addItemToReorderList` is a single synchronous write: it enqueues no job and schedules no task, so there is no asynchronous trigger and no observable completion signal to name. And it stores no monetary value, because a list line carries a variant reference and an integer quantity and no price column at all [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.9 Channel Scoping, Language Scoping And Monetary Values] — so no criterion below asserts an amount of money, and the currency dimension enters only where the parent feature already pairs `price: Money!` with `currencyCode: CurrencyCode!` on the existing catalogue type [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L53-L54].

AC-1: A variant is added to an empty list at the requested integer quantity
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated against the Shop API in the channel identified by the token sent in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62], the request resolves its language from that channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74], that customer owns one `ReorderList` in that channel holding exactly zero lines, and a `ProductVariant` exists in that channel whose `enabled` flag is true [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L53]
* When that customer executes the `addItemToReorderList` mutation with that list's identifier, that variant's identifier and a `quantity` of 6
* Then the mutation returns a `ReorderList` whose `lineCount` is exactly 1 and whose `lines` connection reports a `totalItems` of exactly 1 with its `items` collection holding exactly one entry; that entry's `productVariantId` equals the submitted variant identifier and its `quantity` is exactly 6; the stored `ReorderListLine` row carries that list as its `reorderListId` and that variant as its `productVariantId` and holds no monetary column [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched]; the same line is returned under every `languageCode` because a quantity is an integer rather than a translated string, while the variant-derived fields the line exposes resolve against the request's language through the existing catalogue path [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L48]; and no `OrderLine` row is written by the call

AC-2: A second add for a variant already on the list resolves to the existing line and accumulates its quantity
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns a `ReorderList` in that channel already holding exactly one line whose `productVariantId` is that of an enabled variant and whose `quantity` is exactly 6, the uniqueness of one line per list and variant being enforced by the named database constraint `UQ_reorder_list_line_list_variant` rather than by a service-level check alone [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched]
* When that customer executes the `addItemToReorderList` mutation a second time with the same list identifier, the same variant identifier and a `quantity` of 6
* Then the mutation returns a `ReorderList` whose `lineCount` is still exactly 1 and whose `lines` connection reports a `totalItems` of exactly 1 and whose `items` collection still holds exactly one entry for that variant; that entry's `quantity` is exactly 12, being the sum of the stored 6 and the requested 6; the entry's identifier is unchanged from the identifier returned by the first add, which is what proves the existing row was resolved rather than replaced; no second `ReorderListLine` row exists for that list and variant; the accumulation is issued as a single atomic statement addressed by the line identifier and asserted to have affected exactly 1 row, rather than as a read of the stored quantity followed by a save of a value computed outside the database; and the mutation returns no error result on this path, because a duplicate add is a defined outcome rather than a failure

**A note on AC-2, because it is a deliberate departure from the shipped precedent and the departure has to be reviewable.** The wishlist plugin's add path, on finding the variant already present, returns the collection unchanged and skips the write, with the comment in its source saying exactly that [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L42]. Silence is defensible there because that entity has no quantity column [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/entities/wishlist-item.entity.ts:L13-L14], so a second add genuinely changes nothing a caller could observe. Here a second add either changes the stored amount or does not, and the two are distinguishable, so an outcome has to be chosen and asserted. **The parent feature chooses resolve-and-accumulate and this criterion transcribes that choice rather than making it** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]; if the feature file is ever amended to fix a different duplicate contract, the feature file governs and this criterion follows it. Two consequences are worth naming: **the accumulation is an addition and not an overwrite**, so a caller wanting to set an absolute quantity uses `adjustReorderListLine`, which `STORY-001-01-03` owns; and **a violation of `UQ_reorder_list_line_list_variant` reaching the service is an internal defect rather than a buyer outcome**, which is why no error result covers it and why no member of the published result union corresponds to it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces].

AC-3: An identifier for a list that does not exist, or that belongs to another customer, returns ReorderListNotFoundError
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel; a second customer exists in that same channel and owns a `ReorderList` in it; and the number of `ReorderListLine` rows on that second customer's list is recorded before the call
* When the authenticated customer executes the `addItemToReorderList` mutation once with an identifier that matches no `ReorderList` row at all, and once with the identifier of the second customer's list, both times with an enabled variant and a `quantity` of 6
* Then both calls return `ReorderListNotFoundError`, the two responses are indistinguishable from one another in `errorCode` and in the presence or absence of every field, no `ReorderListLine` row is written by either call, and the recorded number of lines on the second customer's list is unchanged at its exact prior value

**A note on AC-3, and why the unowned case reports not-found rather than a permission failure.** Returning a distinct permission error for another customer's list would confirm that the list exists to anyone who supplies an identifier, and with the platform's default auto-increment identifier strategy an identifier is guessable, so the distinction would be an enumeration channel rather than a courtesy. One result for absent, foreign-customer and foreign-channel alike discloses nothing, and the platform makes the identical choice on its own single-order read for the same reason [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]. `ReorderListNotFoundError` is one of the four error results this feature declares new, and declaring it grows the published `ErrorCode` enum on every boot, because that enum's members are derived from every type implementing the `ErrorResult` interface [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-error-code-enum.ts:L9] matched against the interface-name constant in the same file [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-error-code-enum.ts:L3] by a filter over each type's declared interfaces [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-error-code-enum.ts:L17]. The epic records that growth as collision C2 and requires every consuming example switching on `ErrorCode` to carry a default branch, which is why the demonstration block in section 4 ends with an `ErrorResult` selection [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.1 Repository-Discovered Collisions].

AC-4: An unauthenticated request writes no line
* Given the request carries a valid channel token in the `vendure-token` header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62], no authenticated session, and a `ReorderList` exists in that channel holding exactly zero lines
* When the `addItemToReorderList` mutation is executed with that list's identifier, an enabled variant and a `quantity` of 6

**A note on AC-5, which is where the point-in-time rule is stated for this story.** Any availability figure a buyer sees is read at one instant and is not a reservation: it can go stale between the read and a later commit, because nothing in this feature holds stock and another order may allocate the same units in between. This story makes that harmless by reading no availability at all — the parent feature records that a reorder list stores no stock state and asserts none, availability being publicly observable only as a string on the existing catalogue type [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L56] and `StockLevel` not being a Shop API type. **The staleness therefore belongs to whoever performs the read, and this criterion exists to assert that this operation is not that reader.** A criterion that blocked an add on present availability would both duplicate FEATURE-001-03's work and imply a reservation this feature does not make. **And the reason the criterion now counts calls rather than columns is that the column assertion cannot fail when the behaviour is absent.** An earlier version of AC-5 evidenced "no availability read" solely by `stockAllocated` still being exactly 0 and no stock error being returned — both of which hold just as firmly for an implementation that resolves the saleable level, discards it and writes the line anyway. A discarded read is still a read: it is a query issued, a row loaded and a cost paid, and it is the thing this story claims not to do. The zero-call assertion is the only form of that claim a test can fail.

**Four notes on AC-6, because it carries three distinct rejection classes and one of them is bounded by an open decision.**

*Why the quantity rejections are request-level input errors and not union members.* An earlier version of this criterion returned `NegativeQuantityError` for a quantity of zero. **That is reporting one condition with another condition's name:** the type's own description is "attempting to set a negative OrderLine quantity" [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L43-L47], the platform returns it for `quantity < 0` only [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2267-L2271], and it is about an `OrderLine` rather than a list line — zero is neither negative nor an order line. The contract that replaces it is the epic's ruling R13 [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings]: a deterministic input-contract violation is thrown as `UserInputError` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L26-L30] with a plugin-owned message key registered through the platform's translation mechanism [packages/core/src/i18n/i18n.service.ts:L137-L144], and the criterion asserts the `extensions.code` a client actually observes plus that key. **This is also why this feature declares four new error results rather than five** [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.10 The Error Vocabulary This Feature Introduces]: a quantity that cannot be stored is a malformed request, not a business outcome, so it stays out of every union — and the `CHK_reorder_list_line_quantity_positive` constraint means the database refuses it too if a second code path ever tries.

*Why an upper bound exists at all, which an earlier draft omitted entirely.* A duplicate add accumulates onto the existing line, so the stored quantity is a running sum, and the column is a 32-bit `int` while the GraphQL `Int` is a signed 32-bit integer — an unbounded accumulation therefore overflows both, silently on some engines. `maxQuantityPerLine` closes it, the check is applied to the *resulting* quantity, and the ordering is fixed and asserted: resolve the existing line, validate the resulting quantity, then consult the line bound. **That ordering is what makes the duplicate-at-cap case deterministic** — an add of a variant already on a list that is at `maxLinesPerList` succeeds by accumulating, because it creates no new line and therefore breaches no line bound, which is the outcome AC-2 relies on and which an earlier draft left unspecified.

*The honest note on the precedent.* The shipped plugin reports a bad input by throwing the platform's input-error class [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L38]. That class is not a member of any error-result union, so a criterion cannot read an `errorCode` off it as though it were an `ErrorResult` — what it can and does assert is the `extensions.code` the platform puts on every such error [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L26-L30] together with the message key. **This story therefore follows the precedent rather than departing from it**, which is the reverse of what an earlier draft of this note claimed.

*Which half of this criterion waits on a decision.* The **shipped default** for `maxLinesPerList` is an open product decision that the epic records as blocking eight stories including this one [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. Configuring 2 for a test deployment is not the same as choosing that default, and the distinction is what makes the criterion writable today while leaving the default unsignable until a maintainer sets it. **No product default is invented here.** The shape is idiomatic rather than novel: the platform already ships an order-level limit error [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L37] carrying its own maximum in its payload [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L40], so what is missing is the value and not the pattern. Note also that the bound is evaluated by this mutation and **not** by `adjustReorderListLine`, which changes a quantity on a line that already exists and so cannot breach a line-count bound.

**A note on AC-7, and on why it asserts a success where the obvious design would assert a permission.** An earlier reading of this feature gated these operations on a permission produced by a `CrudPermissionDefinition` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L146], registered through the authentication options [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L125] and applied with `@Allow` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L134]. **A customer session can never hold such a permission.** The Customer Role is created holding one permission [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L443], it is the role every customer user is given, and the role service refuses to amend it [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L290], so no Admin API call, seeding step or configuration adds a permission to it. ## 7. Edge Cases

Five scenarios. The three unconditionally required categories are covered by the first three, and the last two cover the conditional categories that genuinely apply to an accumulating write — a concurrent race on the same variant, and a duplicated delivery of one intent. Two of the required three resolve to *proceed* rather than to a block, and that is stated honestly rather than force-fitted into a failure the operation cannot have.

* Scenario: Zero, null or empty collection — the target list holds zero lines and the request itself is incomplete
   * Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns a `ReorderList` in that channel holding exactly zero lines
   * When that customer executes `addItemToReorderList` once omitting the `quantity` argument, once supplying an empty string as the `productVariantId`, and once supplying a `productVariantId` that resolves to no variant in the active channel
   * Then block all three, and the list still holds exactly zero lines after each. The first is refused before any resolver executes, because `quantity` is declared non-nullable in the published input object and a missing value fails schema validation [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]. The second and third are refused inside the service as request-level input failures rather than as members of the result union, and the contract is named rather than gestured at: each returns no payload and a top-level error whose `extensions.code` is `USER_INPUT_ERROR` [packages/core/src/common/error/errors.ts:L26-L30] carrying the message key `error.reorder-list-variant-not-found`, which is exactly how the shipped precedent treats an identifier that resolves to nothing [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L36-L39]. **An earlier draft of this scenario left both paths with no named contract at all**, which is untestable: a criterion that says "refused" without saying how cannot distinguish a validation refusal from a crash. The variant is resolved through `ProductVariantService` in the active channel before any row is written, so a variant that exists in another channel resolves to nothing here and takes the same path.

* Scenario: An item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase — the variant being added is disabled
   * Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel, owns a `ReorderList` holding exactly zero lines, and the variant to be added has its `enabled` flag set to false [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L53]
   * When that customer executes `addItemToReorderList` with that variant and a `quantity` of 6
   * Then proceed, with no warning and no audit record: the line is stored and the list holds exactly one line at a `quantity` of exactly 6. **The justification is that a saved list records intent rather than availability** — a buyer who bought something regularly still intends to buy it, and a list that silently refused the item would lose the intent without telling anyone. The disabled state is surfaced at preview time by FEATURE-001-03 and resolved at commit time by FEATURE-001-04, which is where the parent feature places it: this feature reads the `enabled` flag and reports it but does not act on it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched]. If the parent feature file is ever amended to block instead, the feature file governs and this scenario follows it. Note the separate case of a *soft-deleted* variant, which sets `deletedAt` rather than removing the row [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L48] and so leaves an existing line in place, and which the read story reports

* Scenario: A price changed since the last purchase — the catalogue price of the variant being added has moved
   * Given the price of the variant to be added has changed since the buyer last purchased it, and a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel owning a `ReorderList` holding exactly zero lines
   * When that customer executes `addItemToReorderList` with that variant and a `quantity` of 6
   * Then proceed, with no warning: **the `ReorderListLine` table stores no price at all**, so this story holds no monetary value that could go stale and there is nothing for a price change to invalidate [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.9 Channel Scoping, Language Scoping And Monetary Values]. A price delta is computed at preview time by FEATURE-001-03 against the live variant price, where the existing catalogue type already pairs an integer money value with its currency code [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L53-L54]. Copying a price onto a list line would be a defect rather than an optimisation, and a reader must not infer a price column on a list line from anything in this story

* Scenario: Concurrent modification or race — two requests add the same variant to the same list at the same moment
   * Given a Returning Buyer owns a `ReorderList` in the active channel holding exactly zero lines, and two requests adding the same enabled variant to that list at a `quantity` of 6 each are in flight together
   * When both executions of `addItemToReorderList` reach the write
   * Then proceed on both, with the outcome reconciled to a single line: the list ends holding exactly one line for that variant whose `quantity` is exactly 12, being the sum of both requested quantities, and no second `ReorderListLine` row exists for that list and variant. **Two different races are covered by that one outcome and they are decided by two different mechanisms, which is why both are named.** In the *insert* race described above the list starts empty, both requests find no line, and a service-level read-then-write cannot reconcile them on its own because both may read "no such line" before either writes; the named unique constraint `UQ_reorder_list_line_list_variant` is what makes exactly one insert win [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched], and **the loser is reconciled rather than reported** — the caught violation is retried as an accumulation against the now-existing row. That is the distinguishing feature of this constraint against the name constraint in the sibling story: a duplicate name is a buyer-visible conflict, whereas a duplicate line is a defined accumulation, so a violation that reaches the caller is an internal defect rather than a buyer outcome. In the *update* race the line already exists, **both requests take the update path, no insert is attempted, and the constraint therefore never fires at all** — so it decides nothing here and a reader who assumes it does will ship a lost update. What decides that race is the single atomic statement AC-2 requires: each request increments the stored value rather than overwriting it with a value computed outside the database, so two concurrent six-unit adds against a line already holding six leave eighteen and not twelve [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency — The Rules That Make The Contract Deterministic]. **Both races are tested by forcing the interleaving rather than by awaiting it, on every engine job that already exists** [.github/workflows/build_and_test.yml:jobs], because a lost update that appears only under one engine's isolation default is exactly the failure this scenario exists to prevent. On either path the API-visible message carries no driver text, no SQL fragment and no constraint name, because a raw driver message can disclose the schema and the conflicting values

* Scenario: A duplicated request rather than a duplicated intent — the same add is delivered twice
   * Given a Returning Buyer owns a `ReorderList` in the active channel holding exactly one line for an enabled variant at a `quantity` of 6, and the client's first `addItemToReorderList` call carrying a `quantity` of 6 and an `idempotencyKey` of `add-7f3a` timed out at the transport after the server had committed it
   * When the client retries the identical mutation, unchanged, with the same `idempotencyKey` of `add-7f3a`
   * Then proceed with a replay rather than a second accumulation: the mutation returns the recorded result of the first call, the line's `quantity` is exactly 12 and not 18, exactly one `ReorderListLine` row exists for that list and variant, and no second increment is issued [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency — The Rules That Make The Contract Deterministic]. **Two adds carrying no `idempotencyKey` are two intents and leave 18**, which is the truthful reading of two requests that carry nothing distinguishing them — the key is what tells a retried request from a buyer who pressed add twice, and timing is not, because a fast second press and a slow retry are indistinguishable at the server [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-02-reorder-from-order-history.md:§2.5.2 Replay Safety]

The two remaining conditional categories do not apply here and are not force-fitted. **A point-in-time read gone stale between check and commit does not apply**, because this operation performs no availability read and makes no reservation — which is asserted as AC-5 rather than left as prose, precisely so that the absence is tested rather than claimed. **A channel, currency or language mismatch between an original order and a reorder context does not apply**, because this operation reads no order and carries no monetary value; the channel dimension it does have is asserted as the second half of AC-7 instead, and the language dimension as the third clause of AC-1.

## 9. Story Estimation Guidance

The four numeric values below are transcribed from this story's row in the epic's delivery-split table, which is the single source of truth for every estimate in this ticket set, and they are not recomputed here [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table].

Estimation Factors:
* Effort: Medium — one service method carrying three rules that interact rather than stacking, being quantity validation, the line-count bound and the resolve-and-accumulate deduplication; one permission-gated resolver; one result union with its type resolver; and one end-to-end specification covering eight criteria including a concurrent case. **The plugin scaffold and the two tables are not among them, because neither is priced by this story** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric] and [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.9 Project Bootstrap]
* Complexity: Low — every mechanism it uses already ships in this repository, from the schema-extension shape [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L16] to the variant-resolution and deduplication path this story adapts [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L34] and the reused non-positive-quantity error [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L44], so the work is re-application over one new column rather than invention
* Uncertainty: Low — the operation's arguments, input object and result union are fixed by the parent feature's published contract [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces] and the duplicate outcome is fixed rather than left open, so the one variable that remains is the line-count bound, which AC-6 asserts against a configured value and which therefore blocks the shipped default rather than the build [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]
* Suggested Story Points: 3
* Estimated Lines of Code to Generate: 350, split as 200 production / 150 test
* Estimated Autonomous Generation Time: 2.5 hours
* Estimated Human Review Time: 1.2 hours

**Why the row sits at three points rather than at two, since this story adds no table of its own.** The rubric's two-point band prices one resolver plus one service method over an existing plugin-owned table with no new table, and its three-point band prices a new plugin-owned table plus its additive migration plus one or two operations [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric]. Read literally, one mutation over a table another story creates looks like the two-point band. **The epic prices it at three, and the epic governs** — validation of the estimate is a comparison against that row and not against a rederivation from the rubric. The reason the row sits where it does is visible in the work: this story is the first write to `ReorderListLine` and therefore owns that table's write-path contract in full, including the named unique and check constraints and the concurrent reconciliation the fourth scenario in section 7 describes, none of which is a plain resolver-over-existing-table. Where this paragraph and the epic row ever disagree, the row wins and this paragraph is the thing to amend.
