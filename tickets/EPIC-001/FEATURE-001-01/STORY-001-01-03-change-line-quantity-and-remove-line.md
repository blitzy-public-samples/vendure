## 1. Story Title

**Change a reorder list line quantity, remove a line, and rename or delete a list, so that a Returning Buyer keeps a saved set current in place rather than rebuilding it.**

Short form, transcribed from the epic's story table without rewording so that the two files agree: **Change a line quantity, remove a line, and rename or delete a list** [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table]. The heading above is the same sentence, measured at 186 characters and so within the 255-character ceiling this tier sets.

**The filename slug names only the line-level half of that scope, and the mismatch is deliberate rather than an error to be tidied.** This file is `STORY-001-01-03-change-line-quantity-and-remove-line.md` because the epic fixes the thirty-four filenames, while the story's title and scope cover all four operations. The parent feature states the same thing from its own side [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§3. User Stories Index]. A reader who prices this story from its filename alone will under-price it by two operations.

The unit of work the title names is one finished slice: every write this feature performs against a row that already exists. Two of those writes address a line — setting its integer quantity, and removing it — and two address the list that holds the lines — renaming it under the same uniqueness rule that governed its creation, and deleting it together with its lines in one transaction.

**Why four operations sit in one story rather than in two or four.** All four are the same shape: resolve the row, enforce ownership against the authenticated session, write, and return either the affected list or a named error result. None of them adds a table and none of them owns a migration that creates one. The parent feature makes that the explicit reason for the single owner, and records that splitting a rename away from a delete would produce stories whose acceptance criteria repeat each other verbatim while this feature's story count is fixed at four [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6.1 Operation Ownership And Test Matrix]. Two of the four reached this story by correction rather than by design: `updateReorderList` and `deleteReorderList` were published in the feature's contract while no story delivered them, and an operation with no owning story is surface that nobody builds and nobody tests [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2.1 Every Published Operation Has Exactly One Owning Story]. The epic raised this row from two points to three when the assignment was made rather than absorbing the scope silently at the old size.

Four things that could be mistaken for part of this story are not. The two tables and the first additive migration that creates them belong to `STORY-001-01-01`, in the plural [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§3. User Stories Index]. Writing the first line into `ReorderListLine`, and the deduplication rule that decides what a second add for the same variant does, belong to `STORY-001-01-02`. Reading lists and lines back over the Shop API belongs to `STORY-001-01-04`. And the plugin package itself, together with its registration in the dev configuration, is a prerequisite of the whole epic that no story owns, prices or claims [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§7.9 Project Bootstrap].

**One boundary is worth stating because two of these operation names collide with published order operations.** Adjusting a reorder list line is not adjusting an order line, and removing a reorder list line is not removing an order line. Nothing in this story touches the active order, no `OrderLine` row is read or written, and the published Shop API mutations whose names this story's operations echo — `adjustOrderLine` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L80], `removeOrderLine` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L76] and `removeAllOrderLines` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L78] — are untouched in both signature and behaviour. ## 2. Source Traceability

> "so that repeat purchasing on the marketplace requires materially less effort than rebuilding an order from scratch"

That sentence is clause C2 of the objective statement, and the epic maps it to four of the twenty-five stories with this one first [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§10.2 Objective-Clause Map]. The label on this story is therefore a quoted clause of the objective statement rather than a derived one.

How editing a saved set in place serves the clause: a list a buyer cannot amend decays into a list the buyer stops trusting, and a buyer who stops trusting a saved set goes back to search results and rebuilds the order item by item — which is the alternative the clause exists to displace. Once a quantity can be changed, a line dropped, a name fixed and a stale list discarded, the saved set stays the thing the next reorder starts from, and the cost of the correction is one mutation against one row instead of a fresh assembly of the whole order.

**The clause is quoted, and the scope this story can claim from it is narrower than the clause itself.** Clause C2 spans four stories, and this one contributes the maintenance half and nothing else. It does not make a reorder reach a cart, which is `STORY-001-02-01` under FEATURE-001-02, and it does not tell the buyer that a price or an availability changed, which is FEATURE-001-03. **No part of the clause's comparison is quantified anywhere in this file**, and that is a constraint rather than an omission: this repository declares no baseline for the effort of rebuilding an order and no target for reducing it, so a figure here would be invented [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§2.2 Business Value]. Every criterion in section 5 therefore asserts a verifiable state instead — an exact line count, an exact integer quantity, an exact stored name, an exact error result type name, or an exact unchanged total on the active order.

## 3. PRECEDENT In This Repository

**PRECEDENT:** near-identical

**A complete saved-list plugin already ships in this repository, and one of this story's four operations is its direct analogue.** `packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/` is not a sketch: its own README states that it is the complete plugin described in the platform's "Writing your first plugin" tutorial [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/README.md:L3]. Its removal path is `removeFromWishlist(itemId: ID!)`, declared inside an `extend type Mutation` block [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L18] and [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L16], and gated exactly as this story's mutations will be, with `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/wishlist.resolver.ts:L12].

**The delta is asymmetric across the four operations, and stating it precisely is the whole justification for the verdict.** `removeReorderListLine` is the near-identical one: remove one row from a customer-owned saved collection addressed by the row's identifier, which is what `removeFromWishlist` already does. `adjustReorderListLine` has **no precedent in that plugin at all**, and the reason is structural rather than incidental — the shipped plugin has no quantity to adjust. Its item entity carries a `ProductVariant` relation [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/entities/wishlist-item.entity.ts:L10] and a `productVariantId` column [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/entities/wishlist-item.entity.ts:L13-L14] and nothing else, so there is no third column an adjust operation could target and the plugin publishes no adjust operation. `updateReorderList` and `deleteReorderList` are likewise absent from it, because the shipped plugin has no *named list* to rename and no *list row* to delete: its saved collection is a single unnamed set hanging off the customer, so there is exactly one of them and it has no name and no lifecycle. **The verdict is near-identical rather than partial because the closest match in the searched set is close enough that a reviewer must be told about it before reading a line of the criteria**, and because the shape of the work — a permission-gated mutation resolving an owned row through a plugin service — is shipped, taught and reused throughout this repository.

**The core naming precedent these operations follow, disclosed so the resemblance is not mistaken for reuse.** The platform's own order service already carries the two verbs this story's line-level operations use: `adjustOrderLine` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L774], which delegates the work to `adjustOrderLines` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L805], and `removeItemFromOrder` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L918]. Their published Shop API counterparts are `adjustOrderLine(orderLineId: ID!, quantity: Int!)` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L80] and `removeOrderLine(orderLineId: ID!)` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L76]. **The new operations mirror the naming shape of those order operations and must not modify them.** Nothing in this story extends, wraps, overrides or re-registers either one, and the reason the resemblance stops at the name is that the two act on different tables: the order operations write `OrderLine` rows inside an order's state machine, while these write plugin-owned `ReorderListLine` rows that no order state governs. AC-8 asserts the boundary rather than leaving it as prose here.

**One precedent is deliberately *not* followed, and the divergence is the sharpest technical disclosure in this file.** The core guard behind the order-side quantity rejection is `assertQuantityIsPositive` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2267], and its documented purpose is to return an error when the quantity is negative [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2265]. Read literally, the guard tests `quantity < 0` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2268] before returning `NegativeQuantityError` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2269], and it is invoked from the order-adjust path [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L819] as well as the add path [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L676]. **So a quantity of exactly zero does not trip that guard.** What the order path then does with a zero quantity is deliberately not asserted anywhere in this file, because this story reads the guard and does not read the rest of that path, and a citation this story cannot verify is a citation it does not make. What *is* fixed here is this story's own behaviour: `adjustReorderListLine` refuses zero on the same path as a negative value, so the plugin is stricter than the core guard it is named after. AC-2 asserts it and explains why.

**Two consequences of the verdict, stated plainly.** Because the precedent is near-identical, this story is disqualified from the epic's demonstration-slice nomination: demonstrating a remove-from-saved-list operation would demonstrate a shape the platform already ships and teaches in a tutorial. That nomination belongs to `STORY-001-02-01` [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.6 Nomination]. Nor does this story hold the harness-proving nomination, which belongs to `STORY-001-01-01` because that story owns the first additive migration and has no prerequisite story, neither of which is true here.

**The searched set that produced the verdict.** The whole of `packages/dev-server/example-plugins/`, the whole of `packages/dev-server/test-plugins/`, and `packages/dashboard/test-plans/`. Nothing in that set carries a *named* saved list, a per-line quantity that can be adjusted after the fact, or a saved-list delete that cascades to line rows, so three of this story's four operations are genuinely absent from the repository rather than merely rearranged — and the fourth is present, which is what fixes the verdict at near-identical rather than at partial.

## 4. User Story

The three mandated lines are rendered as a fenced plain-text block so that each of WHO, WHAT and WHY occupies its own visible line when the file is read on any renderer and when it is pasted into an external tracker. **Every story file in this set uses that same convention**, so a reader comparing two stories compares like with like.

```text
As a Returning Buyer,
I want to change the quantity on a line of one of my named reorder lists, drop a line I no longer buy, rename a list, and delete a list I have finished with,
So that the saved set is amended in place and my next reorder starts from the amended set rather than from the catalogue.
```

### 4.1 INVEST Criteria

- **Independent, with two prerequisites named rather than hidden.** A line cannot have its quantity changed or be removed until a line exists, and `STORY-001-01-02` is the story that writes the first one; a list cannot be renamed or deleted until a list exists, and `STORY-001-01-01` is the story that creates it together with the single additive migration for both tables [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§4.3 Intra-Feature Story Order]. **The dependency is partial, and the partition is worth stating because it changes what can be built first:** only the two line-level operations need a line, while `updateReorderList` and `deleteReorderList` need only a list and therefore depend on `STORY-001-01-01` alone. Both edges run one way — nothing in either prerequisite waits on this story. **Once one list holding one line exists, this story delivers value on its own terms:** a buyer gains the ability to keep that set current, which is observable through four mutations with no other story merged. Section 8 classifies each prerequisite as a data dependency or a shared code path.
- **Negotiable, and the boundary of what is negotiable is narrower than it looks.** Open to a maintainer: the normalisation applied to a name before the uniqueness comparison that AC-6 exercises, and the length bound on the name, both of which this story asserts against the configured value rather than against a figure of its own [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. **Not negotiable is the treatment of a zero quantity**, which this story fixes as a refusal rather than as an implicit removal, so that removing a line stays the declared responsibility of one named operation. **Nor is the argument list, the input object or the result union of any of the four**, all of which are published in the parent feature's contract so that two stories cannot ship two shapes of one operation [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]. **Nor is the number of permission definitions this story registers, and the answer is zero.** A custom permission cannot be granted to the Customer Role, which ships holding a single permission [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L443] and cannot be amended because the update path refuses to modify it [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L290], so a buyer-facing mutation gated on a registered permission would be refused for every buyer. The epic records that as settled rather than open [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings].
- **Valuable:** a saved set that can be created and added to but never amended is a set that goes stale on first contact with a changed shopping habit, and a stale set sends the buyer back to rebuilding the order — the outcome clause C2 exists to displace. This is the story that makes the set maintainable, and it is also the story that makes a list disposable, which matters because a buyer who cannot delete a list accumulates ones they no longer read.
- **Estimable:** the shape is a re-application of shipped work — four permission-gated resolvers over two tables another story creates, four service methods sharing one ownership predicate, four result unions with their type resolvers, and no new table — anchored to the wishlist plugin's remove path, which exists in this repository and differs from one of the four by nothing but the table it writes [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L18].
- **Sized Appropriately:** three points on the epic's Fibonacci scale, transcribed from this story's row rather than recomputed here [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table]. The scale deliberately omits thirteen so that an oversized story is split instead of estimated [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric]. **Four operations in one story is the sizing question this criterion has to answer, and the answer is that they share one contract rather than four.** All four write to rows in the two tables this feature owns, all four are gated the same way, all four resolve their target through the same ownership and channel predicate, and the two line-level ones share a single not-found error result while the two list-level ones share another. Splitting them would duplicate that contract across two to four files whose criteria restate each other, and it would breach the feature's fixed count of four stories [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6.1 Operation Ownership And Test Matrix]. Section 9 records why the row sits at three rather than at the two-point band.
- **Testable:** every criterion in section 5 names one GraphQL operation and asserts a state a test can read — an exact line count with its declared ordering, an exact integer quantity, an exact stored name, an exact error result type name, an exact count of remaining rows, or an exact unchanged total on the active order. No criterion asserts a timing, a rate or a proportion, because this repository declares none and none is invented here. Each of the four operations is a single synchronous write: none enqueues a job and none schedules a task, so there is no asynchronous trigger and no observable completion signal to name for any of them.

## 5. Acceptance Criteria

Eight criteria. Each names exactly one GraphQL operation, so a failing criterion points at a single published contract, and between them all four of this story's operations are exercised — `adjustReorderListLine` in AC-1, AC-2 and AC-3, `removeReorderListLine` in AC-4 and AC-5, `updateReorderList` in AC-6, `deleteReorderList` in AC-7, and the existing `activeOrder` query in AC-8. The five required coverage classes map onto them as follows: valid input is AC-1, AC-4, AC-6 and AC-7; invalid or incomplete input is AC-2; an error naming the exact type returned is AC-2, AC-3, AC-5, AC-6 and AC-7; an edge case is AC-5; and the assertion that an existing operation is unchanged is AC-8.

**Two properties of all four operations are stated once here rather than repeated in every criterion.** Each is a single synchronous write: none enqueues a job and none schedules a task, so no criterion names an asynchronous trigger or a completion signal. And none of them reads or stores a monetary value, because a list line carries a variant reference and an integer quantity and no price column at all [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.9 Channel Scoping, Language Scoping And Monetary Values] — so no criterion below asserts an amount of money.

**Each error result named below is a declared member of the union its own operation returns, and no criterion names an error that operation cannot produce.** The parent feature fixes that membership per operation rather than letting any mutation return any of the feature's four errors: a rename can conflict on a name and a line adjustment cannot, and a remove cannot conflict on a name either [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]. `NegativeQuantityError` is the one error here that already exists in this checkout [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L44] and is reused rather than redeclared; the three not-found and conflict results are declared new by this feature [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.10 The Error Vocabulary This Feature Introduces].

**The four authorisation assertions the parent feature makes mandatory in every story of this feature are carried by AC-1, AC-3 and AC-5 between them:** a session holding no custom permission **succeeds** (AC-1), a request authenticated as a different customer is refused (AC-3), a request carrying a foreign channel token reaches nothing (AC-3), and an unauthenticated request writes nothing (AC-5) [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.7 The Existing Mechanism This Feature Consumes Rather Than Rebuilds]. The first of those is written as a positive assertion because its absence is what would let the design fault the epic records as collision C7 reach production.

AC-1: A line's quantity is set to a new positive integer and nothing else about the list changes
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated against the Shop API in the channel identified by the token sent in the `vendure-token` request header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62], the request resolves its language from that channel's `defaultLanguageCode` [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L74], that session holds the single permission the Customer Role is created with [packages/core/src/service/services/role.service.ts:L443] together with `Permission.Owner` and no plugin-registered permission of any name, and that customer owns one `ReorderList` in that channel holding exactly one line whose `quantity` is exactly 2
* When that customer executes the `adjustReorderListLine` mutation with that list's identifier, that line's identifier and a `quantity` of 6
* Then the call **succeeds** rather than being refused, returning a `ReorderList` whose `lineCount` is still exactly 1 and whose `lines` collection still holds exactly one entry; that entry's identifier equals the identifier it held before the call and its `quantity` is exactly 6, which is the submitted value rather than the sum of the submitted value and the stored 2; the entry's `productVariantId` is unchanged; the list's stored `name` is unchanged at its exact prior value; the same line is returned under every `languageCode`, because a quantity is an integer rather than a translated string; and no `OrderLine` row is written by the call

**A note on AC-1, and on why an adjustment sets rather than adds.** The sibling story fixes a second add of the same variant as resolve-and-accumulate, so `addItemToReorderList` adds to a stored quantity [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]. This operation is the absolute-value counterpart, and the two would be indistinguishable to a caller if this one also accumulated — a buyer who wants six of something needs one call that means six rather than a calculation against whatever is stored. **The criterion asserts the exact value 6 rather than a change of 4 precisely so that an accumulating implementation fails it.** Note also that the line-count bound is not evaluated here: `ReorderPluginOptions.maxLinesPerList` is evaluated by the add operation and explicitly not by this one, because changing a quantity on a line that already exists cannot breach a bound on the number of lines [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]. So no criterion in this story returns `ReorderListLimitError`, and that absence is deliberate rather than an omission.

AC-2: A zero or negative quantity is refused and returns NegativeQuantityError
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns a `ReorderList` in that channel holding exactly one line whose `quantity` is exactly 2
* When that customer executes the `adjustReorderListLine` mutation twice against that list and that line, once with a `quantity` of 0 and once with a `quantity` of -1
* Then both calls return `NegativeQuantityError` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L44] and neither writes anything, so after both the list still holds exactly one line whose `quantity` is exactly 2, unchanged at its exact prior value; and the zero case is refused rather than being treated as a removal, so the line still exists after it

**Three notes on AC-2, because it carries a fixed ruling and a disclosed divergence.**

*The ruling on zero, and why it is a refusal rather than a removal.* A quantity of zero on an existing line is refused. Treating it as an implicit removal would give this feature two ways to delete a line, one of them undocumented in its own name, and would make `removeReorderListLine` optional for a caller who knew the trick. **Removal stays the declared responsibility of one named operation**, which is what makes AC-4 and AC-5 the only places a line disappears. The rule is also enforced below the service: the sibling story establishes the named check constraint `CHK_reorder_list_line_quantity_positive` on the quantity column, so a second code path cannot bypass it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched].

*The disclosed divergence from the core guard this operation is named after.* The order service's own quantity guard is `assertQuantityIsPositive` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2267], documented as returning an error when the quantity is negative [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2265], and it tests `quantity < 0` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2268] before returning `NegativeQuantityError` [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L2269]. It is invoked from the order-adjust path [packages/core/src/service/services/order.service.ts:L819]. **That comparison is strictly less than zero, so a quantity of exactly zero does not trip it.** This plugin is therefore deliberately stricter than the guard whose error type it reuses, and a reviewer comparing the two will find the difference by design rather than by accident. **What the order path goes on to do with a zero quantity is not asserted anywhere in this story**, because this story read the guard and did not read the remainder of that path, and an unverified claim about an existing behaviour is worse than a stated gap.

*Why a missing argument is not a criterion.* The `quantity` field is declared non-nullable in the published input object, so an omitted value fails schema validation before any resolver executes [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]. It is covered as the first scenario in section 7 instead.

AC-3: A line the caller does not hold returns a not-found result that discloses nothing
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns a `ReorderList` in it holding exactly one line; a second customer exists in that same channel and owns a `ReorderList` in it holding exactly one line; a second channel exists with its own distinct token [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]; and the number of lines on the second customer's list is recorded before any call
* When the authenticated customer executes the `adjustReorderListLine` mutation three times with a `quantity` of 6 — once naming their own list together with a line identifier that matches no row at all, once naming the second customer's list together with that list's line, and once naming their own list and line while sending the second channel's token
* Then the first call returns `ReorderListLineNotFoundError` because the list resolved and the line did not; the second and third calls each return `ReorderListNotFoundError` because the list itself did not resolve under the ownership and channel predicate, and those two responses are indistinguishable from one another and from the response to an identifier that matches no `ReorderList` row at all, in `errorCode` and in the presence or absence of every field; no row is written by any of the three; and the recorded number of lines on the second customer's list is unchanged at its exact prior value

**A note on AC-3, and on why the unowned case reports not-found rather than a permission failure.** Returning a distinct permission error for another customer's list or line would confirm that the row exists to anyone who supplies an identifier, and with the platform's default auto-increment identifier strategy an identifier is guessable, so the distinction would be an enumeration channel rather than a courtesy. One result for absent, foreign-customer and foreign-channel alike discloses nothing [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]. **The two error types are not interchangeable, though, and the split is contractual:** a line that is absent from a list the caller does own is a different fact from a list the caller does not own, and a client can act on the first by refreshing the list while the second means the list is not theirs to refresh. That is why this criterion asserts one type for the first case and another for the other two rather than normalising all three onto a single result.

**And a note on what is enforcing the refusal, because the gate is not the control.** All four of this story's mutations are gated `@Allow(Permission.Owner)` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L134], following the shipped saved-list precedent [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/wishlist.resolver.ts:L12] and the platform's own Shop resolvers for customer-owned data [packages/core/src/api/resolvers/shop/shop-order.resolver.ts:L75]. The platform's source states that a resolver relying on ownership **must** include logic enforcing that only the owner of the resource has access, and that without it the effect is the same as public access [packages/core/src/api/config/generate-permissions.ts:L32-L34]; `Permission.Owner` is additionally declared unassignable and internal [packages/core/src/common/constants.ts:L28-L31], so no deployment-side configuration could make ownership hold on the resolver's behalf. **The control is therefore the in-service predicate**, a conjunction of three conditions applied before any row is returned or written — the row's owning customer equals the customer resolved from the authenticated session, the row's channel equals the active channel resolved from the token header, and the session carries an active user at all [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L74-L76]. **This story registers no custom permission definition and grows the published `Permission` enum by zero members.** `CrudPermissionDefinition` [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L146], whose update and delete members are exposed as getters [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L190] and [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L199] and which is registered through the authentication options [packages/core/src/common/permission-definition.ts:L125], remains the right helper for this epic's *administrative* operations, which FEATURE-001-04 and FEATURE-001-08 register. Gating a buyer-facing mutation that way would publish four operations no customer could ever call, which is why the epic settles it as rulings R2 and R3 rather than leaving it open [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings].

AC-4: A named line is removed and the line that remains is the one not named
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns a `ReorderList` in it holding exactly two lines, resolved in their default order ascending by the creation timestamp inherited from the base entity [packages/core/src/entity/base/base.entity.ts:L31], the first referencing one enabled variant at a `quantity` of 2 and the second referencing a different enabled variant at a `quantity` of 3
* When that customer executes the `removeReorderListLine` mutation naming that list and the identifier of the first of those two lines
* Then the mutation returns a `ReorderList` whose `lineCount` is exactly 1 and whose `lines` collection holds exactly one entry; that entry is the second line and not the first — its identifier equals the second line's identifier, its `productVariantId` equals the second variant's identifier and its `quantity` is exactly 3 — so the line that remains is the one the request did not name; exactly one `ReorderListLine` row remains for that list; the list's stored `name` is unchanged at its exact prior value; and no `ProductVariant` row is deleted or altered by the call

**A note on AC-4, and on why the ordering is stated rather than assumed.** An assertion that a collection holds exactly one entry after a removal is only meaningful if the entry can be identified, and an assertion about "the first line" is only meaningful if first is defined. The parent feature declares a default sort for lines — ascending by the inherited creation timestamp — so that an exact count in a stated order is a fact a test can assert rather than a coincidence of insertion order [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]. The criterion also asserts the surviving line by identifier and by variant rather than by position alone, so that an implementation which removed the wrong row and happened to leave a collection of one entry still fails.

AC-5: An unauthenticated remove writes nothing, and a second remove of the same line is refused
* Given a `ReorderList` exists in the active channel, owned by a customer and holding exactly one line, and the request carries a valid channel token in the `vendure-token` header [packages/core/src/entity/channel/channel.entity.ts:L62]
* When the `removeReorderListLine` mutation is executed three times naming that list and that line — first with no authenticated session, then twice by the owning customer
* Then the unauthenticated call writes nothing, returns no successful payload, and leaves the list holding exactly one line; the owning customer's first call removes the line, so the list then holds exactly 0 lines and exactly 0 `ReorderListLine` rows remain for it; and the second owner call returns `ReorderListLineNotFoundError`, writes nothing, and leaves the count at exactly 0, so a repeated remove is refused rather than reported as a success

**A note on AC-5, which is where the idempotence question is settled and where the gate's weakness is visible.** A second remove of a row that is already gone could be defined either as a success that reports the intended end state or as a refusal that reports the missing row, and the parent feature's test matrix fixes it as the second — a second remove of the same line is a required case returning the not-found result [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6.1 Operation Ownership And Test Matrix]. **The end state is idempotent while the response is not, and the distinction is the point:** a caller retrying a removal reaches the same zero-line list either way, so no data is at risk, but a caller who never removed anything and receives a success has been misinformed about a row that was never theirs. As for the unauthenticated call, the request is stopped by the resolver's own ownership logic rather than by the `@Allow` gate, because that gate admits a session which does not hold the permission it lists — the request context is marked authorised-as-owner-only when the session lacks the listed permission and `Permission.Owner` was among those listed [packages/core/src/service/helpers/request-context/request-context.service.ts:L109] — so an unauthenticated request reaches the resolver and is refused there by the session guard the shipped precedent already demonstrates for a request holding no active user [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/service/wishlist.service.ts:L74-L76].

AC-6: A rename is stored, and a rename onto a name the same customer already holds returns ReorderListNameConflictError
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns exactly two `ReorderList` rows in that channel, the first with a stored `name` of `Weekly Kitchen Restock` and the second with a stored `name` of `Monthly Cleaning`, the second holding exactly one line, and uniqueness being evaluated on the canonical name column this feature stores rather than on a database column collation so that the comparison behaves identically on every engine [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]
* When that customer executes the `updateReorderList` mutation twice against the second list, once supplying the name `Fortnightly Cleaning` and once supplying the name `weekly kitchen restock`
* Then the first call returns a `ReorderList` whose stored `name` is exactly `Fortnightly Cleaning`, whose `lineCount` is unchanged at exactly 1 and whose single line's identifier and `quantity` are unchanged at their exact prior values, so a rename reads and writes no line row; and the second call returns `ReorderListNameConflictError`, stores nothing, and leaves the two stored names at exactly `Weekly Kitchen Restock` and `Fortnightly Cleaning`, the collision being raised despite the difference in letter case

**Two notes on AC-6.**

*Why a case-different name collides, and what would not collide.* The canonical form this feature stores is the buyer's input trimmed, its internal whitespace runs collapsed to one space, normalised to Unicode NFC and then lower-cased, and uniqueness is evaluated on that canonical value alone [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]. So `weekly kitchen restock` collides with `Weekly Kitchen Restock`, which is what this criterion asserts. **Canonicalization preserves accents rather than stripping them**, so an accented spelling and its unaccented counterpart remain two distinct names and would not collide — an assertion this criterion deliberately does not make numerically, because the stripping question is settled by that section and not by this story.

*Which half of this criterion waits on a decision, and which does not.* The **normalisation rule** and the **length bound** on a name are open product decisions that the epic records as blocking three of this feature's four stories [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. This criterion is writable today because it asserts the shape against the configured normalisation rather than inventing a bound: a name failing the configured bound is refused and stores nothing, and that refusal is a request-level input failure rather than a member of the result union, because a name that cannot be stored is a malformed request rather than a business outcome [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]. **No value is invented here.** A blank or whitespace-only new name is refused on that same path, which is why it appears in section 7 rather than as an error-result criterion.

AC-7: A deleted list takes its lines with it in one transaction, and a repeat delete is refused
* Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns exactly one `ReorderList` in that channel holding exactly two lines, so that exactly 2 `ReorderListLine` rows belong to it before any call, and that customer also holds an active order whose total quantity is recorded before any call
* When that customer executes the `deleteReorderList` mutation naming that list's identifier, and then executes it a second time with the same identifier
* Then the first call returns a `DeletionResponse` [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-types.graphql:L64] whose `result` field reports the deletion, after which exactly 0 `ReorderListLine` rows belong to that list and the customer owns exactly 0 `ReorderList` rows in that channel, both effects landing in one transaction so that no `ReorderListLine` row is observable without its parent at any point; the second call returns `ReorderListNotFoundError` and changes nothing, leaving both counts at exactly 0; and no `Order` row and no `OrderLine` row is created, altered or deleted by either call, the recorded active-order total quantity being unchanged at its exact prior value

**A note on AC-7, and on why the cascade is asserted by counting rows rather than by naming a database clause.** The line table's foreign key to the list table is declared with a cascading delete in the parent feature's mapping, so the removal of the line rows is the database's work rather than a loop in the service [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched]. A criterion that asserted the clause would test the migration's text; a criterion that asserts the remaining row count tests the behaviour, and it fails whether the cause is a missing clause, a service that deletes the parent and abandons the children, or a transaction that commits half the work. **This is also the one criterion in this story that needs both sibling stories rather than one**, because a delete that cascades cannot be observed on a list that holds no lines — which is why section 8 keeps the data dependency on `STORY-001-01-02` rather than dissolving it.

AC-8: The existing order operations are unchanged and the active order is untouched
* Given `ReorderPlugin` is registered in the dev-server plugin array [packages/dev-server/dev-config.ts:L121], the Shop API schema has been regenerated with this story's `extend type Mutation` block loaded, and an authenticated Returning Buyer owns a `ReorderList` holding at least one line and holds an active order whose total quantity is recorded before any call
* When that customer executes all four of this story's mutations in turn against their own list and line, and then executes the existing `activeOrder` query [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L11], which is the one operation under test in this criterion
* Then `activeOrder` returns an order whose total quantity equals the recorded prior value exactly; no `OrderLine` row was read or written by any of the four preceding calls; and the declared signatures of `adjustOrderLine` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L80], `removeOrderLine` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L76] and `removeAllOrderLines` [packages/core/src/api/schema/shop-api/shop.api.graphql:L78] are byte-identical to their pre-plugin form, none of the three gaining an argument or changing its return type

**A note on AC-8, which is the additive-only constraint made countable for this story.** Two of this story's four operations carry names that echo published order operations, so a signature comparison is not optional here — it is the assertion that a reader most needs. The criterion is written as two assertions rather than one because a schema comparison alone would not catch a behavioural leak: a plugin that adjusted a list line *and* an order line would pass a signature check and fail a buyer. **One widening this story must specifically not trigger is a `customFields` argument on those order mutations**, and the reason it cannot is that this story declares no custom field on any core entity, ownership living on the plugin-owned table instead — which is the epic's ruling R1 and the obligation it puts on a story [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings]. Where a platform mechanism would widen one of those signatures as a side effect of an otherwise additive change, it is reported to the epic's Feasibility Collisions section rather than accepted silently [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.1 Repository-Discovered Collisions].

## 7. Edge Cases

Four scenarios. The three unconditionally required categories are covered by the first three, and the fourth covers a conditional category that genuinely applies to a story whose whole subject is mutating rows that already exist. **Two of the required three resolve to *proceed* rather than to a block**, and that is stated honestly rather than force-fitted into a failure these operations cannot have.

* Scenario: Zero, null or empty collection — the list holds no lines and the requests are incomplete
   * Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns a `ReorderList` in that channel holding exactly zero lines, whose stored name is recorded before any call
   * When that customer executes `removeReorderListLine` naming that list together with any line identifier; then executes `adjustReorderListLine` naming that list and any line identifier while omitting the `quantity` argument; then executes `updateReorderList` naming that list with a new name consisting only of whitespace
   * Then block all three, and after each the list still holds exactly zero lines and its stored name is unchanged at its exact recorded value. The first returns `ReorderListLineNotFoundError`, because the list resolved under the ownership predicate and the line did not exist to remove. The second is refused before any resolver executes, because `quantity` is declared non-nullable in the published input object and a missing value fails schema validation [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces]. The third is refused as a request-level input failure rather than as a member of the result union, because a name whose canonical form is empty cannot be stored and a name that cannot be stored is a malformed request rather than a business outcome [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency] — **a blank rename is deliberately not made a fifth error result**, which is why section 5 names no error outside the three this feature declares for these operations and the one it reuses

* Scenario: An item unavailable, deleted or disabled since the last purchase — the variant behind a line is disabled, or its row has been soft-deleted
   * Given a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel and owns a `ReorderList` holding exactly two lines, the first referencing a variant whose `enabled` flag has been set to false since the line was added [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L53] and the second referencing a variant whose row has since been soft-deleted, which sets `deletedAt` rather than removing the row [packages/core/src/entity/product-variant/product-variant.entity.ts:L48]
   * When that customer executes `adjustReorderListLine` on the first line with a `quantity` of 6, and then executes `removeReorderListLine` on the second line
   * Then proceed on both, with no warning and no audit record. The adjustment succeeds and the first line's `quantity` is exactly 6, **because a saved list records intent rather than availability** — a buyer who bought something regularly still intends to buy it, and refusing to let them change the amount would strand the line in a state they cannot amend. The removal succeeds and the list then holds exactly one line, **because a line is removable in every case whatever the state of the variant behind it**; a list a buyer cannot prune because the catalogue moved underneath them is the failure mode this scenario exists to rule out. Neither operation reads the `enabled` flag as a precondition: the disabled state is surfaced at preview time by FEATURE-001-03 and resolved at commit time by FEATURE-001-04, which is where the parent feature places it [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.4 Named Entities Touched]. Note that a *hard* variant deletion would take the line with it through the declared cascade on the line's variant foreign key, so the case this scenario covers is the soft delete, which leaves the line in place. If the parent feature file is ever amended to block instead, the feature file governs and this scenario follows it

* Scenario: A price changed since the last purchase — the catalogue price of a variant behind a line has moved
   * Given the price of the variant behind a line has changed since the buyer last purchased it, and a Returning Buyer is authenticated in the active channel owning a `ReorderList` holding exactly one line for that variant at a `quantity` of 2
   * When that customer executes `adjustReorderListLine` on that line with a `quantity` of 6, and then executes `updateReorderList` renaming the list
   * Then proceed on both, with no warning: **the `ReorderListLine` table stores no price at all**, so an adjustment changes an integer quantity and nothing monetary, a rename changes a string and nothing monetary, and there is no stored value for a price change to invalidate [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.9 Channel Scoping, Language Scoping And Monetary Values]. A price delta is computed at preview time by FEATURE-001-03 against the live variant price, where the existing catalogue type already pairs an integer money value with its currency code [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L53-L54]. Copying a price onto a list line would be a defect rather than an optimisation, and a reader must not infer a price column on a list line from anything in this story

* Scenario: Concurrent modification or race — one request adjusts a line while another removes the same line
   * Given a Returning Buyer owns a `ReorderList` in the active channel holding exactly one line whose `quantity` is exactly 2, and two requests are in flight together against that same line — one `adjustReorderListLine` with a `quantity` of 6 and one `removeReorderListLine`
   * When both reach the write
   * Then exactly one of the two outcomes is observable afterwards and the list's line count is deterministic rather than duplicated: either the adjustment committed first, in which case the removal then succeeds and the list holds exactly 0 lines, or the removal committed first, in which case the adjustment finds no line under the ownership predicate and returns `ReorderListLineNotFoundError` while the list holds exactly 0 lines. **Both orderings end at exactly 0 lines, and no ordering leaves a line at a `quantity` of 6 that a caller was told had been removed**, which is the outcome a read-then-write without a row-level guard would permit. The loser is reported rather than reconciled, and that is the distinguishing feature of this race against the duplicate-add race in the sibling story: a duplicate add has a defined accumulation to fall back on, whereas an adjustment of a removed row has no state to reconcile with, so the caller is told the row is gone. On either path the API-visible message carries no driver text, no SQL fragment and no constraint name, because a raw driver message can disclose the schema and the conflicting values [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.11 Names, Bounds And Concurrency]

The two remaining conditional categories do not apply here and are not force-fitted. **A point-in-time read gone stale between check and commit does not apply**, because none of these four operations performs an availability read and none makes a reservation — the parent feature records that a reorder list stores no stock state and asserts none, availability being publicly observable only as a string on the existing catalogue type [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/product.type.graphql:L56]. The staleness therefore belongs to whoever performs that read, which is FEATURE-001-03, and these operations are not that reader. **A channel, currency or language mismatch between an original order and a reorder context does not apply either**, because none of the four reads an order and none carries a monetary value; the channel dimension these operations do have is asserted as the third call in AC-3, and the language dimension as a clause of AC-1.

## 8. Dependencies

Each entry states the required thing, then why it is required and where it belongs, and each is classified as a **data dependency** or a **shared code path**.

- **Story `STORY-001-01-02` — the story that writes the first `ReorderListLine` row:** required because a quantity cannot be adjusted and a line cannot be removed until a line exists, so there is no valid input to two of this story's four operations without it; it belongs to this same feature, FEATURE-001-01, and the parent feature records the edge in one direction only, with 01-03 depending on 01-02 and never the reverse [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§4.3 Intra-Feature Story Order]. **Data dependency** on exactly one list line — two, for AC-4 and AC-7, which assert an ordering and a cascade that a single line cannot demonstrate. **The dependency is partial rather than total, and the partition is the sequencing fact worth carrying:** `updateReorderList` and `deleteReorderList` need only a list, so they do not wait on this story at all.

### 8.1 Decisions This Story Waits On

Dependencies on a decision rather than on code. None is taken here, and each belongs to a maintainer.

- **The list-name normalisation rule and the length bound.** Both are the comparison-exact and numeric halves of AC-6, and the epic records them as an objective ambiguity blocking three of this feature's four stories including this one [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. The criterion is written against the configured normalisation so it is buildable today; the shipped rule cannot be signed off until it is set. **What is already fixed rather than waiting is that the stored name is data and every consumer renders it as text**, which this story inherits rather than decides [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.10.1 The Buyer-Supplied List Name Is Hostile Input].
- **The customer-data lifecycle for this feature's two tables.** It matters more to this story than to its siblings, because this is the story that owns deletion: `deleteReorderList` removes a list and its lines on the buyer's instruction, while the erasure window for a *deleted customer's* rows is undecided [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]. The two are separate, and this story implements only the first. **No deletion, anonymisation or purge behaviour beyond the buyer-instructed delete ships before the decision is taken**, and in particular this story adds no data-lifetime window, no soft-delete column and no scheduled purge.
- **The branch target.** It blocks nothing in this story's design and gates its merge, and it is a conflict in this repository's own documentation rather than an oversight [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§11.1 The Branch Target Is A Three-Way Conflict].
- **Two decisions that stood here are closed, and are recorded as closed rather than removed.** The treatment of a zero quantity is fixed as a refusal by this story rather than left to the implementation, so that removal stays the responsibility of one named operation. And the ownership mechanism is settled with zero permission definitions registered for buyer-facing operations, the alternative having been not merely open but unbuildable [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§6.4 The Settled Rulings].

## 9. Story Estimation Guidance

The four numeric values below are transcribed from this story's row in the epic's delivery-split table, which is the single source of truth for every estimate in this ticket set, and they are not recomputed here [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2 Story Table].

Estimation Factors:
* Effort: Medium — four service methods and four permission-gated resolvers, four input objects and four result unions with their type resolvers, one shared ownership and channel predicate applied by all four, and one end-to-end specification covering eight criteria and four scenarios including a concurrent case; offset by the fact that no table, column, index or constraint is added and no plugin option is declared, so none of the schema work that usually accompanies four operations is present here
* Complexity: Low — every mechanism it uses already ships in this repository, from the schema-extension shape [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L16] and the remove path that is its near-identical precedent [packages/dev-server/example-plugins/wishlist-plugin/api/api-extensions.ts:L18] to the reused non-positive-quantity error [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-error-results.graphql:L44], the existing deletion payload [packages/core/src/api/schema/common/common-types.graphql:L64] and the declared cascade that removes the line rows without a loop; the work is re-application over rows that already exist rather than invention
* Uncertainty: Low — the arguments, input objects and result unions of all four operations are fixed by the parent feature's published contract [tickets/EPIC-001/FEATURE-001-01-named-reorder-lists.md:§2.6 Named API Surfaces], the zero-quantity treatment is fixed by this story and the duplicate-name comparison is fixed by the feature, so the one variable that remains is the name normalisation rule, which AC-6 asserts against the configured value and which therefore blocks the shipped rule rather than the build [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§8.1 Product Decisions]
* Suggested Story Points: 3
* Estimated Lines of Code to Generate: 350, split as 200 production / 150 test
* Estimated Autonomous Generation Time: 2.5 hours
* Estimated Human Review Time: 1.2 hours

**Why the row sits at three points, and why the rubric read on its own would say two.** The rubric's two-point band prices one resolver plus one service method over an existing plugin-owned table with no new table, and its three-point band prices a new plugin-owned table plus its additive migration plus one or two operations [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.1 The Estimation Rubric]. This story adds no table at all, so a literal read of the rubric lands it in the two-point band — and **the epic prices it at three, and the epic governs**, because validation of an estimate is a comparison against that row and not a rederivation from the rubric. The reason the row sits where it does is visible in the scope rather than in the schema: the row was raised from two points to three at the moment `updateReorderList` and `deleteReorderList` were assigned here, so the extra point buys the two list-level operations rather than a table [tickets/EPIC-001-reorder-and-replenishment.md:§9.2.1 Every Published Operation Has Exactly One Owning Story]. Four operations at the two-point band would have priced this story as though it still owned two. Where this paragraph and the epic row ever disagree, the row wins and this paragraph is the thing to amend.
